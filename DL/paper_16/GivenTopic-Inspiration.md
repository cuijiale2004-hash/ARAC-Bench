Given Topic: Equilibrium Propagation (EP) represents a promising biologically plausible alternative to conventional gradient-based learning in recurrent neural networks; however, its practical deployment is severely hindered by protracted convergence dynamics and training instability, which engender substantial computational overhead. While backpropagation remains the dominant paradigm for training deep networks, its reliance on non-local error transport and symmetric weight updates contradicts known neurobiological constraints. Current EP implementations necessitate extensive iterative settling phases to reach equilibrium, rendering them orders of magnitude slower than conventional approaches. Furthermore, extending EP to deep architectures exacerbates credit assignment difficulties, particularly under weakened recurrent feedback. Consequently, a critical scientific challenge lies in developing a learning framework that preserves the biological plausibility and local computation of EP, while simultaneously ensuring rapid dynamical convergence, stable training across depth, and computational efficiency competitive with conventional backpropagation.
