Given Topic: Reinforcement learning post-training for large language models has achieved remarkable success in tasks with clear-cut success criteria, yet remains fundamentally constrained when applied to free-form generation tasks. Existing paradigms rely on either enumerative verification of task-specific rubrics, which incurs prohibitive computational costs and fails to scale with output complexity, or static reward models that collapse under distribution shift and induce reward hacking. Moreover, the optimal aggregation of heterogeneous evaluation rubrics is inherently prompt-dependent and cannot be captured by a fixed proxy. The core problem is to develop a scalable reinforcement learning framework for open-ended generation that avoids exhaustive rubric enumeration, eliminates reliance on static reward proxies, and provides prompt-specific, verifiable training signals.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that free-form generation tasks possess large or unbounded sets of evaluation rubrics, rendering exhaustive verification computationally intractable as output complexity increases.
2. The limitation that static reward models, whether learned from offline preferences or prompted, misalign with evolving generator distributions and are vulnerable to exploitation through reward hacking.
3. The principle from robust optimization that evaluating against a worst-case adversary yields more reliable improvements than average-case or fixed-criteria evaluation.
4. The insight that verification should be dynamic and adaptive, targeting only the most informative failure modes rather than uniformly checking all possible criteria.
5. The motivation to ground reward signals in external, objective validation rather than internal proxy predictions, ensuring that supervision remains faithful to task requirements during generator exploration.
