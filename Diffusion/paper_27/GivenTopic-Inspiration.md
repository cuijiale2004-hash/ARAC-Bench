Given Topic: Post-training quantization for diffusion models aims to compress noise estimation networks for efficient inference. Mainstream methods typically sample calibration data uniformly across the denoising trajectory and assign equal importance to all calibration instances. This uniform paradigm overlooks the varying informativeness of individual samples and ignores the evolutionary frequency characteristics inherent to the denoising process, where distinct spectral contents are recovered at different stages. Consequently, existing quantization strategies inadequately preserve the behavior of the full-precision model. The central challenge is to devise a principled, non-uniform weighting mechanism that accounts for heterogeneous sample significance and stage-dependent frequency dynamics during quantization, thereby enhancing fidelity without architectural modification or extensive retraining.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical finding that non-uniform weighting of calibration data can surpass uniform treatment, indicating the existence of an optimal importance distribution over samples.
2. The temporal frequency evolution inherent to diffusion denoising, where coarse structural information is recovered early and fine textural details emerge in later stages.
3. The principle of optimizing instance-level importance weights guided by an outer validation objective, allowing automatic discovery of data points most critical to quantization fidelity.
4. Multi-resolution signal decomposition, which isolates low-frequency structure from high-frequency texture, enabling selective preservation of different spectral components.
5. The stage-specific heterogeneity of the denoising process, where different timesteps exhibit distinct spectral properties and thus demand differentiated optimization objectives.
