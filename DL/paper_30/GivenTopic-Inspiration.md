Given Topic: Speech brain-computer interfaces aim to restore communication by translating neural activity into text. Mainstream approaches rely on cascaded pipelines that separately decode phonemes and assemble sentences via external language models, preventing joint optimization and causing mismatched performance across components. Prior end-to-end attempts have been limited by shallow architectures and lack of large-scale pretraining, while scarce labeled data restricts generalization across tasks such as attempted and imagined speech. The core challenge is to develop a unified, differentiable framework that directly maps neural dynamics to coherent language, leveraging modern representation learning and cross-condition transfer to overcome data scarcity and modality misalignment.

Given Inspiration:
1. The performance dissociation observed in cascaded decoders—where improved intermediate representations fail to reduce final output errors—suggests that end-to-end optimization is necessary for coherent speech restoration.
2. Modern sequence architectures in other domains have demonstrated that scalable pretraining on diverse unlabeled data yields robust representations, implying that neural population activity may similarly benefit from large-scale self-supervised pretraining across subjects and behavioral conditions.
3. Recent multimodal language models indicate that highly capable language backbones can interpret novel modalities through lightweight alignment, suggesting that speech-related neural signals might be treated as an emergent modality for direct sentence generation.
4. The structural similarity between motor cortical activity during articulated and imagined speech implies that these tasks share latent semantic representations, motivating cross-task alignment to transfer knowledge from high-data to low-data conditions.
5. Acoustically pretrained language models encode inductive biases closer to the neural speech decoding problem than text-only models, hinting that neural activity can be positioned within an acoustic-semantic embedding space.
6. Self-supervised reconstruction objectives widely used for continuous signals offer a natural mechanism to handle non-stationarity and probe drift inherent in chronic intracortical recordings.
7. The progression from complex modality-specific projectors to simple mappings in multimodal learning suggests that neural-to-language alignment may not require elaborate intermediate interfaces if the encoder produces sufficiently structured representations.
