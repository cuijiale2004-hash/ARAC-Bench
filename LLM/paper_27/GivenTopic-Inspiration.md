Given Topic: The field of parameter-efficient fine-tuning aims to adapt large pre-trained models via lightweight auxiliary modules, yet a critical bottleneck remains: these modules are rigidly bound to their original backbone architectures, rendering them non-transferable across heterogeneous models. As foundation models evolve rapidly, the inability to reuse task-specific adaptations across different families, scales, or versions incurs prohibitive retraining costs. Existing knowledge transfer paradigms either demand access to original downstream datasets—which are frequently proprietary or inaccessible—or rely on synthetic data pipelines that necessitate auxiliary discrimination networks, introducing substantial engineering overhead. Furthermore, prevailing sequence-level distillation strategies indiscriminately process all tokens, diluting task-relevant signals with redundant or noisy information. The core scientific problem is to enable cross-model transplantation of task knowledge encapsulated in lightweight adapters without dependency on original training corpora or auxiliary filtering systems, while achieving granular selection of informative content to maximize transfer efficiency.

Given Inspiration:
1. Spatial concentration of task knowledge: Task-specific expertise in generative outputs is not uniformly distributed across token sequences; rather, it concentrates in discrete semantic regions where the adapted model diverges most from its base backbone.
2. Contrastive uncertainty reduction: Comparing predictive confidence between a base model and its parameter-efficient augmentation yields an implicit attribution signal that localizes where task-specific knowledge has been injected.
3. Likelihood-ratio principles from statistical testing: Distributional divergence metrics grounded in classical hypothesis testing provide theoretically optimal criteria for identifying tokens that maximally differentiate an adapted model from its backbone.
4. Token-level selectivity in representation learning: The observation that unequal token contributions suffice for model optimization suggests that cross-model knowledge transfer need not replicate entire sequences, but can instead prioritize high-salience tokens.
5. Intrinsic data curation without auxiliary models: Synthetic exemplars generated by the source expert itself can be self-evaluated and filtered using internal model signals, eliminating the need for separately trained discriminator networks.
