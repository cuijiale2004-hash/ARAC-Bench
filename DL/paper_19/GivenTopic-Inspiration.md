Given Topic: Research in deep representation learning for vision and language has increasingly relied on architectures where token dependencies are parameterized through learnable linear projections. Despite extensive efforts to improve scalability and expressiveness, the initialization of the projections governing these interactions has received limited theoretical scrutiny. Current practice predominantly employs generic random schemes or heuristic weight transfer from converged models, neither of which is derived from the intrinsic optimization geometry of the interaction mechanism. This creates a fundamental bottleneck: the absence of a principled protocol for prescribing the initial spectral structure of these projections to ensure stable gradient dynamics and favorable optimization landscapes from the outset of training.

Given Inspiration:
1. Classical weight initialization theory establishes that variance-preserving scaling at network inception is critical for preventing unstable gradients in deep architectures, suggesting that analogous principled criteria are needed for modern token-interaction mechanisms.
2. The optimization stability of token-interaction layers is fundamentally governed by the singular value spectra of their constituent projection matrices, as reflected in the conditioning of the layer's local linearization.
3. Analyses of kernel dynamics in over-parameterized regimes indicate that well-conditioned initial linearizations correlate with faster convergence and more reliable gradient-based optimization.
4. Projections that contribute multiplicatively to pairwise affinity computations and those that contribute linearly to output representations serve algebraically distinct roles, implying that their initializations should respect these functional asymmetries.
5. Matrix families characterized by perfectly uniform singular value distributions provide a natural source of inductive bias, as they minimize spectral imbalance in the interaction layer's local linearization without modifying the training objective.
