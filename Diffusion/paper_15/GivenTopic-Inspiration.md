Given Topic: The research concerns efficient inference for discrete diffusion language models via parallel decoding strategies. While bidirectional attention enables theoretical parallelization, practical implementations face a severe efficiency-quality dilemma: simultaneous token prediction introduces distributional inconsistency due to violated conditional independence assumptions, and per-step computation costs exceed those of autoregressive counterparts. Existing confidence-based selection strategies overlook the spatial configuration of undecoded positions, causing significant distributional drift when masked tokens form dense contiguous spans. The central problem is to devise a decoding organization principle that enables high-throughput parallel generation while preserving sequential distributional fidelity and contextual coherence.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observed phenomenon that the spatial arrangement of masked tokens strongly affects distributional consistency during parallel decoding, where scattered layouts better preserve sequential fidelity than contiguous spans.
2. The divide-and-conquer paradigm, which motivates recursive decomposition of complex sequence generation into smaller, structurally independent subproblems to mitigate the difficulty of simultaneous multi-token prediction.
3. The incompatibility between unconditional parallel generation and the conditional dependencies inherent in natural language, suggesting that correlated tokens must be structurally isolated to maintain coherence when decoded simultaneously.
4. The principle that high-confidence predictions can serve as reliable spatial anchors, enabling dynamic partitioning of remaining undecoded regions into localized, parallelizable areas.
