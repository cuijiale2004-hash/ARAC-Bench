Given Topic: Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems enhance large language models with external knowledge, yet introduce substantial computational overhead through dramatically elongated input sequences. Existing approaches suffer from two critical inefficiencies: redundant recomputation of frequently reused knowledge fragments across independent queries, and uniform deep-retrieval strategies that indiscriminately provision extensive context regardless of query complexity, incurring unnecessary cost and potential accuracy degradation from irrelevant information. Conventional caching mechanisms enforce overly rigid matching constraints that fail under variable long-context compositions, or sacrifice generation fidelity by neglecting inter-chunk dependencies. Moreover, prevailing methods predominantly rely on static retrieval depths without per-query adaptation. The core challenge is to simultaneously eliminate cross-query computational redundancy and avoid contextual over-provisioning while preserving the quality benefits of retrieved knowledge.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that retrieved knowledge units exhibit highly skewed reuse patterns across queries, suggesting that caching strategies should exploit non-uniform access frequencies to amortize computational costs.
2. The recognition that query complexity is inherently heterogeneous, with the majority of questions satisfiable by shallow evidence while complex cases remain rare, rendering fixed retrieval depths inherently inefficient.
3. The structural insight that transformer attention across retrieved chunks follows distinct layer-dependent patterns, exhibiting localized predecessor dependencies in early layers and concentrated sink behavior in deeper layers.
4. The concept that exact sequence matching is overly restrictive for cache reuse in compositional retrieval settings, motivating graduated matching granularities calibrated to empirical dependency strength.
5. The realization that cross-chunk dependencies are sparsely distributed across tokens, such that only positions with significant inter-chunk attention require recomputation to maintain inference fidelity.
6. The principle that internal model states, including layer-wise logit evolution and output distribution entropy, can serve as lightweight proxies for epistemic confidence during incremental context construction.
7. The idea that context augmentation can be formulated as an adaptive process where evidence is accumulated incrementally and terminated based on predictive certainty rather than predetermined retrieval budgets.
