Given Topic: Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (c-MARL) is increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains, yet existing execution-time robustness paradigms predominantly rely on worst-case adversarial training. Current methods typically formulate agent-adversary interactions as zero-sum games, yielding monolithic policies that are overly conservative under nominal cooperation and brittle against non-worst-case adversaries. Furthermore, the inherent non-convexity of such formulations often leads to convergence toward local equilibria, while exposure to narrow adversarial distributions during training induces overfitting that undermines generalization to unseen attack strategies at deployment. The core problem is to develop adaptive robustness frameworks that account for the continuum of potential adversarial objectives and behavioral types, enabling policies to maintain near-optimal performance across diverse adversarial conditions without sacrificing cooperative optimality.

Given Inspiration:
1. Existing execution-time robustness paradigms in cooperative multi-agent systems suffer from a fundamental mismatch between worst-case training assumptions and deployment reality, where adversarial objectives are private, heterogeneous, and may encompass non-malicious faults or only partially destructive behaviors.
2. Bayesian game theory provides a principled framework for strategic uncertainty, motivating the treatment of adversarial robustness as an incomplete-information problem with a continuum of adversarial types rather than a single worst-case opponent.
3. The performance degradation an adversary inflicts relative to a nominal cooperative baseline yields a natural severity metric, suggesting that continuous adversarial uncertainty can be partitioned into discrete, behaviorally distinct equivalence classes without requiring knowledge of private adversarial reward functions.
4. Perfect Bayesian equilibrium offers a normative solution concept for adaptive robustness, where defenders optimize expected team performance conditioned on maintained beliefs over adversarial types rather than committing to fixed max-min strategies.
5. Global max-min optimization over unconstrained adversarial policy spaces is inherently susceptible to local stationary points and representation overfitting; constraining adversarial search to disjoint, semantically isolated subsets can preserve behavioral diversity and mitigate suboptimality.
6. The empirical observation that adversarial impact correlates with behavioral abnormality implies that adversaries belonging to divergent severity classes induce statistically distinguishable state-action distributions, which in principle supports online belief formation from observed trajectories.
7. Standard constrained reinforcement learning assumes that costs and rewards are drawn from the same environmental trajectory; however, in adversarial type optimization, the objective faced by the attacker and the feasibility constraints imposed by the defender's reference dynamics naturally reside on distinct Markov decision processes.
