Given Topic: The field concerns efficient deployment of large-scale diffusion-based video generation models. While these models demonstrate remarkable generation capabilities, their prohibitive computational and memory costs severely limit practical applications. Existing compression techniques primarily pursue either low-precision quantization or sparse attention patterns independently; however, aggressive quantization collapses representational capacity, while aggressive sparsification discards crucial contextual information. Moreover, a naive combination of these orthogonal strategies suffers from a compounded degradation mechanism where sparsity-induced information loss amplifies quantization perturbations, resulting in distorted attention distributions and impaired temporal-spatial dependency modeling. The core scientific issue is to establish a unified compression paradigm that enables the synergistic integration of weight-activation quantization and attention sparsification, breaking the traditional trade-off between efficiency and generation fidelity.

Given Inspiration:
1. The recognition that quantization noise and attention sparsification are not statistically independent in diffusion-based video generation; their interaction produces compounded, non-linear distortions in attention distributions that exceed the sum of individual degradations, necessitating a unified co-design perspective.
2. The inherent spatio-temporal locality of video data, where adjacent tokens exhibit strong similarity due to temporal smoothness and spatial continuity; this suggests that coarse structural topology can provide sufficient global guidance without full-resolution supervision.
3. The heavily skewed, long-tailed distribution of attention saliency observed in video generation models, wherein a minority of tokens accounts for the majority of attention mass; this indicates that preserving fidelity for dominant tokens is disproportionately critical to output quality.
4. The practical constraint that exact dense attention alignment during calibration incurs prohibitive quadratic complexity for long video sequences, implying that supervisory signals must be approximated through compact, scalable representations.
5. The empirical observation that direct first-order attention residuals between full and compressed computations fluctuate significantly across denoising timesteps when quantization is present, whereas second-order temporal differences remain substantially more stable because adjacent timesteps share similar noise characteristics.
6. The mathematical insight that temporally stable correction signals reside in low-dimensional dominant subspaces of the residual manifold; this motivates approximating dynamic errors via projection onto stable principal components rather than raw high-dimensional residuals.
