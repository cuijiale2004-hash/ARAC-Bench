Given Topic: Object counting constitutes a fundamental task in computer vision, yet fully-supervised approaches require prohibitively expensive point-level annotations that become impractical in densely populated scenes. Weakly-supervised methods mitigate this burden by utilizing only image-level counts, but prevailing approaches are largely confined to single predefined categories. Recent advances in multimodal large language models reveal inherent visual reasoning capacities that could enable class-agnostic counting through language-guided specification. Nevertheless, directly leveraging such models for precise count estimation is impeded by a severe modality gap between continuous visual representations and discrete scalar outputs, particularly under high-density conditions where performance degrades sharply. The central scientific problem resides in constructing a weakly-supervised paradigm that effectively unlocks the latent quantitative reasoning of multimodal language models for arbitrary categories while ensuring robustness in dense scenes and bridging cross-modal alignment challenges.

Given Inspiration:
1. Progressive range decomposition: Precise scalar regression across vision and language modalities is inherently challenging; decomposing counting into sequential coarse-to-fine range decisions reduces cognitive load and aligns with curriculum learning.
2. Relative ordinal supervision: Judging the relative magnitude of quantities between images is perceptually more reliable than absolute enumeration, offering a visually grounded optimization signal for numerical reasoning.
3. Cross-scale bias compensation: Holistic scene interpretation and localized patch analysis exhibit complementary systematic errors in dense environments; integrating both perspectives can mutually correct inherent estimation biases.
4. Language-guided category abstraction: Specifying counting targets through high-level semantic descriptions rather than visual exemplars enables generalization across unrestricted object taxonomies.
5. Latent quantity activation: Pretrained multimodal models possess implicit numerical awareness that can be refined through structured reasoning tasks rather than direct count regression from visual features.
6. Weak-label efficiency: Image-level count supervision, though coarse, can sufficiently guide the development of robust quantity estimators when paired with training strategies that exploit relative and ordinal visual relationships.
