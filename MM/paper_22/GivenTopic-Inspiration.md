Given Topic: Post-training multimodal foundation models for interactive agentic systems, particularly in domains such as computer-use and mobile device control, is fundamentally constrained by the scarcity of high-quality interactive task datasets. Existing paradigms predominantly rely on costly human annotation or on static, limited environment descriptions to synthesize tasks, resulting in poor scalability and insufficient coverage of real-world functionalities. Moreover, tasks generated without direct environmental grounding often lack feasibility and cannot be reliably verified, limiting their utility for supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. The core scientific issue is how to scalably generate diverse, executable, and verifiable task definitions that are explicitly grounded in the dynamic states and interaction possibilities of target environments, thereby reducing reliance on manual curation while ensuring broad functional coverage.

Given Inspiration:
1. The fundamental bottleneck in scaling agent post-training is the absence of large-scale task definitions that simultaneously exhibit diversity, executability, and verifiability within target environments.
2. Foundation models, despite possessing broad world knowledge, lack explicit grounding in specific environment dynamics and runtime state information when tasked with synthesizing instructions without interaction.
3. Direct, goal-agnostic exploration of interactive environments can systematically uncover novel states and functionalities, providing a dynamic knowledge base that static descriptions cannot capture.
4. Feasible task synthesis requires conditioning on actual observable environment states rather than relying solely on parametric knowledge or manually annotated feature lists.
5. Environment interaction trajectories implicitly encode transition dynamics and semantic state information that can serve as contextual anchors for generating grounded instructional tasks.
6. Maximizing coverage over an environment's state space through exploration is essential to ensure that generated tasks span the full spectrum of available functionalities and content.
7. The disconnect between abstract task descriptions and concrete environment capabilities leads to hallucinated or non-executable instructions, necessitating state-aware task proposal mechanisms.
8. Synthetic task generation for agent training should leverage discovered environmental context to align proposed instructions with realistically achievable interaction sequences.
