Given Topic: In topological neural networks, which augment traditional graph learning by modeling higher-order interactions through structures such as simplicial complexes, cell complexes, and hypergraphs, the transformation of raw graphs into richer topological domains—known as graph lifting—is a critical prerequisite. Conventional lifting strategies are predominantly handcrafted, static, and determined prior to training, rendering them insensitive to downstream task demands. Consequently, the constructed higher-order structures are often suboptimal, and their efficacy varies unpredictably across datasets, creating a significant reproducibility bottleneck. The central scientific challenge is to develop a principled mechanism that jointly learns the topological lifting and the predictive model in an end-to-end manner, thereby replacing fixed preprocessing rules with data-driven, task-adaptive topology inference.

Given Inspiration:
1. The inconsistency and dataset-dependent variability of static, rule-based lifting performance indicate that topological structure construction should be treated as an integral, optimizable component of the learning pipeline rather than a fixed preprocessing stage.
2. The hierarchical composition inherent in topological objects, wherein high-dimensional structures are founded upon lower-dimensional constituents, suggests an iterative generation principle where each dimensional layer informs the next.
3. Learned vertex embeddings encapsulate both semantic and structural properties of nodes, providing a natural latent basis for determining which subsets of vertices should constitute meaningful higher-order relational units.
4. Framing the selection of higher-order cells as a probabilistic decision under learned parameters allows discrete topological choices to be optimized continuously against task-specific loss functions.
5. The divergence in downstream performance across distinct topological domains for identical input graphs implies that lifting strategies must be domain-agnostic and adaptively selectable based on data characteristics.
6. Existing graph structure learning methods demonstrate that relational topology can be inferred from data, motivating the extension of such differentiable topology inference beyond the graph domain to hypergraphs and cell or simplicial complexes.
7. The observation that local connectivity patterns and global feature geometry often misalign inspires leveraging embedding-space proximity to identify candidate node groupings that may not be evident from the original edge structure alone.
8. The need to balance expressivity and computational tractability in higher-order structure generation suggests constraining candidate search through adaptive, locally conditioned neighborhood sizes rather than exhaustive enumeration.
