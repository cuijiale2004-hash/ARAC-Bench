Given Topic: In the field of reinforcement learning for language model reasoning, current on-policy policy gradient methods suffer from training instability and poor sample efficiency during early stages. Specifically, low-quality rollouts in initial training phases introduce high-variance gradient estimates, while the prevalent single-update-per-batch paradigm underutilizes collected data and yields unreliable optimization directions. Although reusing rollout batches could improve efficiency, direct off-policy updates induce distribution mismatch and progressive performance degradation. The core challenge lies in designing an update mechanism that stabilizes noisy gradients, enhances sample efficiency through better batch exploitation, and preserves distributional alignment with the data collection policy, without modifying the underlying training objective or rollout generation pipeline.

Given Inspiration:
1. **Trajectory integration over noisy gradient fields**: Inspired by the view of optimization as integrating vector fields, the work considers that sequential gradient observations on a fixed batch trace a local trajectory whose cumulative direction is more stable than any individual vector. The motivation is to filter high-frequency noise inherent in early-training rollouts without introducing extrinsic regularization.
2. **Trust-region regularization of distributional drift**: Inspired by proximal optimization principles, the perspective is that parameter updates computed from static batches progressively violate the on-policy assumption. The motivation is to constrain the iterate toward the data-collection distribution, treating aggressive updates as deviations that must be regularized to maintain reliable gradient estimates.
3. **Dynamical systems separation of timescales**: Drawing from numerical analysis, the insight is that optimization can benefit from separating exploratory dynamics from conservative alignment. The motivation is to allow aggressive variance reduction while subsequently re-aligning with local curvature through a slower corrective process.
4. **Information-theoretic phase detection via policy entropy**: Inspired by the relationship between entropy and optimization landscape geometry, the work treats entropy fluctuations not merely as an exploration metric but as a signal of proximity to local optima. The motivation is to adaptively modulate update aggressiveness when noise begins to dominate over signal.
