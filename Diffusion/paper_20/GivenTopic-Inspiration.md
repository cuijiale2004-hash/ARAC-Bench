Given Topic: The field concerns generative modeling of discrete sequential data, including biological sequences and natural language, via diffusion-based approaches. Current methodologies are fragmented into three distinct paradigms—discrete-state forward processes, continuous Gaussian corruption in embedding spaces, and simplex-constrained diffusion—each employing incompatible algorithmic frameworks and non-comparable likelihood objectives. Practitioners face critical trade-offs between domain naturalness, algorithmic maturity, and numerical stability, particularly as simplex-based methods suffer from severe numerical instabilities during training and sampling. The absence of a unifying theoretical foundation prevents principled cross-domain model comparison, hyperparameter translation, and architectural flexibility, irrevocably locking practitioners into a single representational domain prior to training. The core problem is to establish a coherent theoretical framework that reconciles these disparate paradigms, stabilizes simplex-based processes, enables rigorous comparison of objectives across representational spaces, and obviates the need for domain-specific commitments during model development.

1. Population genetics theory, particularly the mathematical modeling of allele frequency dynamics under mutation and reproduction, provides a foundational stochastic process perspective.

3. Established mathematical genetics literature on exact ancestral sampling and computational stabilization of population processes offers insight into mitigating numerical instabilities inherent to simplex-constrained diffusion.
4. The theoretical prospect that discrete, continuous, and simplex-based diffusion correspond to distinct parameterizations or asymptotic limits of an identical base process motivates the search for a unified, domain-agnostic theoretical framework.
5. Statistical sufficiency principles suggest that the essential information required for reverse-time denoising can be represented in a form invariant to the specific forward process parameterization, circumventing the need for modality-specific architectural lock-in during training.
