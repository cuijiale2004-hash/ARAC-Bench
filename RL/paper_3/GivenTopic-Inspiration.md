Given Topic: In the field of reinforcement learning for large language model reasoning, data scheduling is essential for improving training efficiency and final performance. Current scheduling strategies predominantly rely on path-level outcome metrics—such as final solution accuracy—to measure query difficulty and prioritize training examples. These approaches treat the reasoning process as a flat sequence, fundamentally neglecting the hierarchical, branching topology of the underlying solution space. As a result, they conflate poor initial performance with high learning difficulty, failing to distinguish between queries with concentrated, easily correctable errors and those with structurally fragmented error distributions. The core issue is how to quantify a query's intrinsic learning difficulty based on the structural properties of its hierarchical decision topology, and how to design a principled scheduling mechanism that organizes training examples from structurally tractable to complex instances during policy optimization.

Given Inspiration:
1. The conceptualization of reinforcement learning optimization as a dynamic node-editing process within a hierarchical decision structure, where each intermediate state represents a branching point and training progressively prunes erroneous trajectories while reinforcing correct ones.
2. The realization that final solution accuracy is an insufficient proxy for learning difficulty, as queries with low initial accuracy may possess highly concentrated error structures that are easily remedied, whereas queries with moderate accuracy may exhibit fragmented, diffuse error distributions requiring extensive modification.
3. The perspective of evaluating learning potential through a constrained modification budget, measuring how much performance can be recovered by refining only a limited number of critical decision points rather than evaluating entire solution trajectories.
4. The insight that the topological distribution of correct and incorrect branches within a hierarchical solution space—specifically whether errors are clustered or scattered across disparate subtrees—determines the true difficulty of policy optimization.
5. The foundational principle of curriculum learning, reinterpreted through a structural lens: training should proceed from examples whose hierarchical topologies permit rapid improvement via localized adjustments, toward those demanding global structural reorganization.
