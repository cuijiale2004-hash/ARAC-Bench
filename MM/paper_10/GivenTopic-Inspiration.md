Given Topic: Knowledge distillation for efficient multimodal large language models aims to compress large vision-language models into compact counterparts. Existing approaches effectively transfer visual recognition capabilities for conventional question answering, yet they yield marginal improvements on compositional reasoning tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception, such as understanding object relationships and attributes. This limitation indicates that current paradigms inadequately transfer the teacher model's underlying visual attention mechanisms to the student. The core scientific problem is how to rectify the visual attention misalignment between teacher and student models to enable effective distillation of compositional reasoning abilities while preserving existing recognition performance.

Given Inspiration:

2. Attention distribution analysis over visual tokens indicates that alignment within intermediate semantic integration layers is a key predictor of downstream visual reasoning performance.
3. Direct attention transfer is constrained by embedding space incompatibility, implying that attention mechanisms are intrinsically coupled with their native vision-language feature spaces.
4. Depth disparities between teacher and student architectures necessitate aggregating distributed attention knowledge across contiguous teacher layers to guide individual student layers.
5. Preserving the relative directional structure of attention vectors is more conducive to relational reasoning than enforcing exact numerical correspondence, as compositional understanding depends on relative patch importance.
6. Effective capability transfer requires a progressive strategy: first establishing cross-model space compatibility, then transferring attention behavior, and finally consolidating instruction-following competence.
