Given Topic: As large-scale model training increasingly shifts from centralized data centers to decentralized, geographically distributed computing nodes connected via wide-area networks, reinforcement learning-based post-training encounters fundamental architectural incompatibility with this paradigm. Existing reinforcement learning frameworks inherently couple environment interaction and parameter optimization, demanding strict low-latency synchronization that becomes untenable under heterogeneous computational resources and unpredictable network delays. The resulting policy staleness induces severe distributional shifts between the behavior and target policies, causing explosive variance in off-policy correction weights and consequent training instability or reward collapse. The core scientific challenge lies in designing principled learning algorithms and system architectures that tolerate arbitrary communication delays and hardware heterogeneity without sacrificing optimization stability or final model performance.

Given Inspiration:
1. Network latency in geographically distributed systems inherently creates temporal gaps between policy execution and parameter updates, causing statistical divergence that manifests as explosive variance in off-policy correction weights.
2. Fine-grained, per-sample importance weighting schemes are structurally fragile under large policy divergence; statistical estimation over natural groups of samples offers a more robust alternative for controlling variance.
3. The relative sampling probabilities within a cohort of responses generated from the same prompt encode stabilizing information that can regularize the scale of importance ratios without relying on individual sample likelihoods.
4. The architectural separation of experience collection and policy optimization in wide-area networks should be embraced as a fundamental characteristic rather than remedied through forced synchronization.
5. The instability of asynchronous reinforcement learning stems from the exponential growth of importance weight variance with policy divergence, suggesting that variance suppression mechanisms must specifically target high-divergence regimes.
