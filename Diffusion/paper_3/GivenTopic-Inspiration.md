Given Topic: Efficient generative modeling for resource-constrained deployment faces the challenge that structural and temporal acceleration techniques introduce approximation errors in iterative diffusion models. These errors propagate and accumulate exponentially across sequential denoising timesteps, leading to severe degradation of output fidelity. Conventional remedies typically mandate re-execution of the efficiency pipeline or require access to original high-precision parameters, which is infeasible after deployment due to system immutability, storage constraints, and the irretrievability of original model artifacts. The central scientific issue is therefore to develop strictly test-time methodologies capable of suppressing accumulated inference errors without modifying model weights, altering architectures, or repeating pre-deployment calibration.

Given Inspiration:
1. The paradigm of test-time scaling, which posits that additional inference-time computation can be leveraged to improve output quality without retraining or modifying pretrained parameters.
2. The observation that approximation errors in iterative generative processes exhibit exponential amplification dynamics as they propagate across sequential timesteps.
3. Principles from numerical analysis concerning iterative refinement and contraction mappings, suggesting that successive local corrections can bound and stabilize error growth.
4. The engineering reality of deployed edge and production systems, wherein model binaries are immutable and original precision representations are permanently unavailable, necessitating purely post-hoc correction strategies.
5. A dynamical systems perspective of the denoising trajectory, which treats timestep-wise perturbations as deviations amenable to stabilization through localized feedback-like refinement.
