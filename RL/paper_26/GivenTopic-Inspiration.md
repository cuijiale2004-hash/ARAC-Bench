Given Topic: Offline reinforcement learning aims to learn effective policies from static datasets without online interaction. A fundamental challenge is the overestimation of values for out-of-distribution actions caused by distribution shift. Current mainstream methods typically penalize unseen samples indiscriminately or constrain policies close to the behavioral distribution. However, existing approaches struggle to accurately identify out-of-distribution actions and often rely on restrictive distributional assumptions, leading to either excessive suppression of potentially beneficial explorations or insufficient conservatism. Moreover, most methods apply uniform penalties across out-of-support regions without distinguishing the nature of deviation. The core scientific issue is how to develop a precise detection mechanism for out-of-distribution actions and design an adaptive regularization strategy that selectively suppresses risky extrapolations while permitting high-potential explorations beyond the behavioral support.

Given Inspiration:
1. Existing approaches to behavior modeling in offline reinforcement learning often rely on unimodal distributional assumptions, which inadequately represent complex, multi-modal behavioral data and consequently misidentify out-of-distribution actions.
2. Reconstruction error under controlled corruption provides a distribution-free surrogate for identifying data manifold membership, circumventing the limitations of density-based estimators that may invert likelihood rankings for anomalous inputs.
3. Not all out-of-distribution actions carry equivalent risk; the impact of an action should be judged by its predicted consequences on future state distributions and value trajectories rather than by deviation from behavioral support alone.
4. Conservative value estimation can be reconciled with strategic exploration by distinguishing detrimental extrapolations from beneficial ones, applying penalization and compensation differentially based on anticipated outcomes.
5. Theoretical guarantees in approximate dynamic programming suggest that controlled, bounded adjustments to Q-function targets preserve contraction properties while enabling policy improvement beyond in-distribution regions.
