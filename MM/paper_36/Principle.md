**Principle 1: Causal Attribution and Mechanistic Validation of Reasoning-Driven OOD Generalization**

**Definition:**  
When a method claims to improve out-of-distribution generalization via explicit reasoning chains, reviewers must evaluate whether the empirical gains genuinely stem from semantically meaningful reasoning content rather than from surface-level stylistic patterns, generic text-conditioned supervision, or standard multi-task regularization effects. The work is expected to isolate the causal contribution of reasoning through carefully designed ablations—such as neutralizing semantic content while preserving textual form, or evaluating predictive accuracy under reasoning-only inference without visual input. Furthermore, token-level diagnostics (e.g., entropy trajectories, probability shifts on class-relevant versus domain-specific tokens) should demonstrate that the optimization process actively leverages reasoning content rather than incidental linguistic structure. Without this mechanistic layer of evidence, claims of “reasoning-driven” robustness remain experimentally indistinguishable from conventional auxiliary-task or pseudo-labeling dynamics.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Semantic Isolation via Neutralized Ablations**: Does the work include controlled experiments where task-relevant reasoning semantics are removed but surface structure preserved, and does this produce a significant performance drop?
- **Reasoning-Only Discriminative Power**: Does the model exhibit improved classification accuracy when forced to rely solely on generated reasoning chains without visual input, proving that the reasoning process itself has become more robust and class-discriminative?
- **Token-Level Mechanistic Evidence**: Are quantitative analyses provided (e.g., entropy reduction patterns, high/low-confidence token proportions) showing that optimization selectively strengthens class-relevant reasoning tokens rather than stylistic or background descriptors?

**Principle 2: Cross-Model Reasoning-Pattern Alignment and Teacher Dependency Mitigation in Synthetic Supervision**

**Definition:**  
In settings where structured reasoning supervision is synthetically generated by an external teacher model (e.g., a proprietary large MLLM), reviewers must scrutinize whether the authors diagnose and mitigate the distributional mismatch between the teacher’s reasoning style and the student model’s native reasoning tendencies. This requires explicit analysis of cross-model divergence in reasoning patterns—such as differences in chain length, semantic focus, and token-level entropy—and demonstration that naive distillation of external reasoning is suboptimal. A high-quality framework must progressively replace teacher-generated supervision with self-aligned, model-native reasoning chains through validated iterative mechanisms, and quantifiably reduce dependency on the external teacher’s stylistic priors. Key evidence includes rejection-rate dynamics across self-labeling rounds, comparisons of embedding-space divergence, and performance gains attributable solely to supervision-distribution shifts rather than additional training.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Mismatch Quantification**: Does the work explicitly measure the reasoning-pattern gap between external teacher chains and the student’s self-generated reasoning (e.g., via token probability distribution shifts, top-token entropy reduction analysis, or qualitative semantic-focus comparisons)?
- **Dependency Reduction Evidence**: Does the proposed self-alignment procedure demonstrably reduce reliance on the external teacher, verified by downstream performance improvements when moving from teacher-supervised to self-aligned reasoning stages?
- **Filtering Dynamics Across Iterations**: Are the behaviors of self-labeling filtering mechanisms thoroughly characterized (e.g., rejection-rate curves per round), proving systematic semantic alignment rather than random data curation or simple rejection sampling?

**Principle 3: Task-Agnostic Generalization and Backbone-Scale Robustness in Multimodal Reasoning Training**

**Definition:**  
A reasoning-driven domain-generalization framework must demonstrate that its supervision mechanisms are task-agnostic and effective across diverse backbone architectures with varying inherent reasoning capabilities. Reviewers expect validation beyond the primary task modality—typically image classification—to distinct multimodal tasks requiring joint visual-linguistic reasoning (e.g., visual question answering, visual entailment) to confirm that the method exploits generalizable reasoning invariances rather than task-specific shortcuts. Additionally, the framework must be shown effective on smaller, fully open-source models lacking dedicated reasoning pretraining, ensuring that reported gains are not artifacts of large proprietary backbones or instruction-tuned models. This cross-task and cross-architecture validation prevents overfitting to a specific experimental setting and establishes the method as a general training paradigm.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Multimodal Task Breadth**: Does the evaluation extend beyond classification to tasks with different output structures and cross-modal grounding requirements (e.g., VQA, VE, or captioning) under consistent domain-shift protocols?
- **Backbone Diversity**: Are experiments conducted on multiple model families and parameter scales, including smaller open-source alternatives without strong reasoning initialization?
- **Robustness to Backbone Quality**: Do improvements remain consistent and monotonic across models with weak baseline reasoning capabilities, indicating that the framework does not depend on a strong pretrained reasoning backbone?

**Principle 4: Computational Efficiency and Convergence Diagnostics of Iterative Self-Labeling Regularization**

**Definition:**  
Iterative self-labeling and multi-round training procedures—such as successive rounds of self-generated reasoning and fine-tuning—must be accompanied by rigorous efficiency analyses and convergence diagnostics to be considered practically viable. Reviewers should assess whether the authors quantify the marginal computational cost of each round relative to its performance gain, identify the point of diminishing returns, and provide lightweight configurations that preserve most benefits at substantially reduced cost. Evidence should demonstrate that later rounds require fewer optimization steps or that the majority of alignment benefits are captured in early iterations. Without such cost-benefit transparency, iterative methods risk being dismissed as computationally impractical for standard academic or industrial research budgets, regardless of their theoretical appeal.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Cost-Benefit Per Round**: Is the accuracy gain of each self-labeling iteration explicitly quantified alongside its computational overhead (e.g., generation passes, training epochs, wall-clock time)?
- **Diminishing Returns Analysis**: Does the work identify a statistical convergence point where additional rounds provide negligible improvements, supported by significance tests or plateau detection?
- **Lightweight Configuration Validation**: Are reduced-epoch or reduced-round settings evaluated to demonstrate that strong performance is achievable without incurring the full iterative cost (e.g., single-epoch-per-round configurations)?

**Principle 5: Embedding-Space Verification of Process-Level Domain Invariance in Reasoning Chains**

**Definition:**  
Central claims that reasoning chains provide superior “process-level” domain invariance compared to visual features require direct quantitative verification beyond qualitative examples or end-task accuracy metrics alone. Reviewers expect embedding-space evidence—such as cross-domain Maximum Mean Discrepancy, cosine similarity, or other distributional divergence metrics—showing that reasoning representations exhibit significantly lower cross-domain divergence than corresponding visual feature embeddings. This statistical validation must ideally be disaggregated across semantic categories to ensure consistency and should establish a clear link between the degree of measured invariance in reasoning embeddings and final out-of-domain accuracy. Qualitative case studies, while illustrative, are insufficient; the core hypothesis must be grounded in representational statistics that distinguish process-level invariance from latent feature-level invariance.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Embedding Divergence Metrics**: Are quantitative measures reported (e.g., MMD, mean embedding distance) comparing cross-domain divergence of reasoning-chain embeddings versus visual embeddings under identical domain splits?
- **Category-Level Consistency**: Does the invariance analysis demonstrate consistent reductions in divergence across diverse semantic categories, rather than relying solely on aggregate averages that may obscure category-specific failures?
- **Link to Downstream Generalization**: Is a correlational or causal connection established between the measured domain invariance of reasoning chains and the magnitude of OOD accuracy improvements?
