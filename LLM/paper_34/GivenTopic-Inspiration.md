Given Topic: The detection of hallucinations and factual errors in autoregressive language models remains a critical challenge. Existing approaches predominantly depend on supervised probe classifiers trained over internal representations or activation-intervention techniques, which suffer from severe cross-task generalization deficits and impose prohibitive deployment overhead in open-ended, foundation-model settings. Concurrently, training-free baselines relying on raw output confidence exhibit weak discriminative power and degraded calibration following instruction tuning, rendering them unreliable for distinguishing correct generations from erroneous outputs. The core problem is to establish a theoretically principled, training-free credibility assessment framework that generalizes robustly across heterogeneous tasks and architectures without requiring task-specific supervision or internal model modification.

Given Inspiration:
1. The autoregressive factorization of sequence probability implies that mathematically equivalent quantities computed at adjacent generation steps should remain mutually consistent; deviations from this expected conservation may indicate underlying generation errors.
2. Discriminative probabilistic predictors can be reinterpreted within an energy-based modeling framework, wherein scoring functions implicitly encode global distributional constraints exploitable for credibility assessment.
3. Post-hoc classifiers trained on latent representations exhibit fundamental transfer limitations across diverse downstream tasks, suggesting that robust detection signals should instead be derived from intrinsic, output-level mathematical properties.
4. Semantic truth and factual content in generated sequences tend to be spatially concentrated in specific localized spans rather than uniformly distributed, warranting targeted assessment of critical generation segments.
5. Conventional output-level confidence metrics suffer from persistent miscalibration under distributional shift and fine-tuning, necessitating alternative discrepancy measures grounded in the model's native probabilistic structure.
