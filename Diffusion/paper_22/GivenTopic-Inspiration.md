Given Topic: Reinforcement learning post-training for diffusion language models faces critical exploration bottlenecks when tackling complex reasoning tasks. Existing methods frequently encounter sparse reward landscapes where policies struggle to discover correct solutions, resulting in degenerate learning signals and substantial sample inefficiency. In group-based policy optimization frameworks, the prevalent occurrence of entirely incorrect response groups leads to uniform reward estimates, completely suppressing gradient updates and halting policy improvement. Furthermore, the unique architectural properties of diffusion language models remain underutilized in current reinforcement learning paradigms, creating a gap between supervised fine-tuning and online exploration. Addressing how to inject meaningful guidance into the exploration process without compromising on-policy learning integrity constitutes a central challenge. This work investigates how to restore effective gradient propagation and alleviate exploration failures during reinforcement learning training for diffusion language models.

Given Inspiration:
1. The bidirectional generative mechanism of diffusion language models enables partial sequence conditioning during generation, suggesting that incomplete guidance can be naturally injected without requiring sequential factorization.
2. Group-based policy optimization for language models encounters a critical failure mode under sparse rewards: when all sampled responses are incorrect, reward estimates become uniform and policy gradients vanish entirely.
3. A fundamental tension exists between pure supervised fine-tuning, which induces distribution shift away from policy rollouts, and pure reinforcement learning, which offers no structural guidance during exploration failures.
4. Partial trajectory grounding offers a principled intermediate regime between full supervision and unguided exploration, allowing the policy to retain self-generated reasoning while receiving directional signals toward high-reward regions.
5. Uncertainty-aware selective learning provides a natural stabilization criterion: external guidance should primarily update model parameters where the current policy exhibits low confidence, avoiding destabilizing conflicts with strongly held predictions.
6. Reasoning datasets and practical generation budgets frequently exhibit severe length misalignment, where verbose demonstrations exceed deployment constraints, motivating trajectory length calibration to preserve distributional consistency across training stages.
