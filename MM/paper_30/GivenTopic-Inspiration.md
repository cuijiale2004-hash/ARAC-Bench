Given Topic: In the field of multimodal large language models, post-training methodologies have predominantly adopted text-centric reasoning paradigms, wherein dense visual signals are compressed into sparse cues to support textual inference. This imbalance has led to underdeveloped intrinsic visual perception capabilities, particularly in fine-grained spatial, temporal, and three-dimensional understanding. Existing attempts to enhance visual understanding typically require architectural alterations, auxiliary visual generation modules, or pixel-level reconstruction objectives, which are computationally expensive and potentially suboptimal for text-generation models. The core challenge is to establish a lightweight, self-supervised post-training framework that operates within existing text-output architectures, requiring no additional generative components or intermediate textual mediation, to systematically elevate the model's intrinsic comprehension of visual structure across multiple modalities.

Given Inspiration:
1. Classical self-supervised visual representation learning indicates that structural ordering tasks can yield effective supervision without demanding pixel-perfect reconstruction fidelity.
2. The jigsaw puzzle paradigm demonstrates that recovering original arrangements from shuffled spatial or temporal elements serves as a cognitively meaningful proxy task for visual understanding.
3. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards suggests that deterministic, automatically checkable tasks provide robust post-training signals that generalize beyond mere memorization.
4. The inherent text-generation constraint of current multimodal architectures necessitates supervisory objectives expressible through natural language permutations rather than dense visual reconstruction.
5. Visual modalities exhibit intrinsic structural regularities—including spatial continuity in images, chronological order in videos, and depth stratification in scenes—that naturally map to permutation recovery problems.
6. Post-training stages are conducive to generalizable skill acquisition, as foundational visual understanding enables models to benefit from high-level structural reasoning challenges.
