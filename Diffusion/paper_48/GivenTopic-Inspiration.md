Given Topic: Inverse problem reconstruction leveraging diffusion-based generative priors has garnered significant attention, yet existing paradigms exhibit critical limitations. Approaches that enforce measurement consistency during intermediate stages of the generative process frequently perturb states away from the learned data manifold, yielding accumulated artifacts and degraded sample fidelity. Conversely, optimization-based methods that directly target image-space fidelity are prone to overfitting measurement noise and collapsing into local modes, particularly when noise levels are high or a priori unknown, and they necessitate cumbersome, task-specific hyperparameter tuning. Deterministic formulations in the noise space mitigate some drift issues but sacrifice posterior exploration, often failing in severely ill-posed settings. Consequently, a pressing scientific challenge is to devise a posterior sampling framework that simultaneously preserves manifold feasibility, avoids noise overfitting without task-specific calibration, and enables thorough exploration of multimodal solution spaces under diverse and unknown noise conditions.

Given Inspiration:
1. Geometric insight on manifold feasibility: In high-dimensional generative manifolds, direct gradient-based corrections applied to intermediate states introduce components orthogonal to the local tangent space, pushing samples off the data manifold and motivating confinement of inference to a domain where the prior inherently maintains on-manifold proposals.
2. Latent noise-space perspective: Conceptualizing the reverse generative trajectory as a deterministic mapping from an initial noise latent to a clean observation permits posterior factorization entirely in the latent domain, where the prior distribution is simple and exact likelihoods are accessible without intermediate approximations.
3. Momentum-driven exploration in high dimensions: Employing dynamical sampling trajectories guided by auxiliary momentum variables facilitates efficient traversal of high-dimensional latent spaces and helps escape local modes that trap conventional gradient-based optimizers.
4. Annealed noise scheduling: Gradually annealing an effective measurement noise scale during early sampling stages promotes broader exploration of the posterior landscape before convergence, reducing sensitivity to initialization in highly multimodal inverse problems.
5. Bayesian marginalization of nuisance parameters: Adopting a non-informative, scale-invariant prior on unknown measurement noise variance and marginalizing it analytically yields an adaptive likelihood that automatically calibrates to the true noise level without requiring task-specific tuning.
