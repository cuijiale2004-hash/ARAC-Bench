Given Topic: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has significantly advanced the logical reasoning capabilities of large language models. However, prevailing paradigms require exhaustive annotation of entire datasets and uniformly distribute computational resources across all training instances. Empirical evidence suggests that samples contribute heterogeneously to policy optimization, implying the existence of compact, high-value subsets capable of matching full-dataset performance. The unsupervised identification of such critical instances without access to ground-truth answers remains an unresolved scientific challenge. This research addresses the problem of discovering annotation-free, high-impact training data to enable annotation-minimal and computationally efficient policy optimization for reasoning tasks.

Given Inspiration:
1. The lottery sample hypothesis, which posits that large-scale training corpora inherently contain compact subsets whose isolated optimization yields performance comparable to full-data regimes.
2. Observations from data valuation literature indicating that training instances exert non-uniform influence on policy gradient dynamics and final model performance.
3. The intuition that reasoning complexity and learning potential can be diagnosed by examining how perturbations or truncations in intermediate thought processes alter final conclusions.
4. The principle that diverse final outcomes generated from a fixed policy for an identical query reflect richer, multi-directional optimization signals than homogeneous responses.
5. Conformal prediction theory, which provides distribution-free, model-agnostic uncertainty quantification through statistically rigorous prediction sets for evaluating answer reliability.
