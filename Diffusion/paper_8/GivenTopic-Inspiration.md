Given Topic: Unsupervised sequential disentanglement aims to decompose sequential data into static and dynamic factors of variation without annotations, which is fundamental for interpretable representation learning across modalities such as video, audio, and time series. Current mainstream approaches predominantly rely on variational autoencoders and generative adversarial networks, which suffer from complex optimization landscapes involving multiple competing loss terms and extensive hyperparameter tuning. Moreover, existing methods are primarily evaluated on simplistic toy datasets and lack standardized protocols for assessing performance on high-dimensional real-world sequential data. Although diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative frameworks, their theoretical formalization for sequential disentanglement remains absent. The core problem is establishing a principled, modality-agnostic framework that can robustly separate static and dynamic factors in real-world sequential data while circumventing the optimization complexities inherent in prior paradigms.

Given Inspiration:
1. The complexity and fragility of multi-objective optimization in variational and adversarial sequential disentanglement frameworks, which limits their applicability to real-world data, motivates the pursuit of a single, unified loss formulation grounded in score-based principles.
2. The theoretical and empirical successes of diffusion processes in unconditional generation suggest that their underlying probabilistic machinery—particularly score matching and iterative denoising—could be adapted to structured sequential factorization, despite the absence of formal sequential disentanglement theory in this domain.
3. Real-world sequential phenomena inherently exhibit coupled static and dynamic characteristics; this motivates abandoning strict independence assumptions between time-invariant and time-variant factors in favor of a joint probabilistic structure that improves marginal expressiveness and enables parallelized sampling.
4. The pronounced gap between evaluations on synthetic or low-resolution datasets and the demands of high-resolution real-world video, audio, and time series data inspires the integration of compact latent representations and perceptual compression paradigms to manage dimensional complexity across modalities.
5. The tendency of well-structured latent spaces in deep generative models to align with human-interpretable semantic directions—observed in unsupervised linear subspace analyses—suggests that static and dynamic representations can be further decomposed into finer-grained factors through post-training exploratory procedures.
6. The modal-dependent nature of existing state-of-the-art disentanglement solutions, which exploit domain-specific priors such as spatial consistency or spectral cues, inspires the development of a unified theoretical framework compatible with arbitrary sequential modalities without architectural redesign.
