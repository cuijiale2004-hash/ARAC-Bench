### Principle 1: Empirical Diagnosis and Causal Attribution of Initial-Stage Collapse in High-Noise 3D Diffusion Sampling

**Definition:**
Reviewers must verify that the work does not merely assert the existence of "initial-stage collapse" (failure to recover structure from pure noise in 3D volumes) as a motivation, but provides rigorous empirical diagnosis distinguishing it from generic training instability. The evaluation should assess whether the authors have systematically characterized the failure mode across varying noise levels (e.g., t=1000 vs. t=600) and established a causal link between the high-dimensional manifold of 3D data/vanishing structural cues and the observed collapse. Crucially, the work must demonstrate that the proposed stabilization mechanism (e.g., APDS) specifically targets this root cause rather than acting as a generic performance booster, and must rule out alternative explanations such as insufficient model capacity or improper noise scheduling.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
*   **Granularity of Failure Analysis:** Does the paper provide a step-wise performance curve (e.g., Dice vs. starting timestep) showing *where* exactly the baseline fails, rather than just a binary "works/fails" comparison?
*   **Causal Isolation:** Are there controlled experiments demonstrating that the collapse is intrinsic to the 3D diffusion process under high noise, and not an artifact of specific hyperparameters or dataset preprocessing?
*   **Mechanism-Specific Validation:** Does the ablation study explicitly show that removing the stabilization component re-introduces the *specific* collapse pattern diagnosed, confirming the solution matches the problem definition?
*   **Differentiation from 2D Analogues:** Is there a clear explanation or evidence showing why this collapse is unique to or exacerbated in 3D volumetric tasks compared to 2D slice-based approaches?

### Principle 2: Non-Interference Verification and Dynamic Decay Rationality in Adaptive Prior Guidance Strategies

**Definition:**
For methods employing adaptive prior guidance (like APDS) to stabilize early diffusion stages, reviewers must critically evaluate the risk of "prior over-interference," where strong external guidance degrades the model into a simple refinement network or suppresses the generative capability of the diffusion backbone. The assessment should focus on whether the decay schedule of the prior weight is theoretically justified and empirically validated to ensure the model transitions from "prior-dominated" to "diffusion-dominated" generation. It is insufficient to only show final performance; the work must prove that the prior recedes appropriately and that the final high-fidelity details are generated by the learned denoiser, not copied from the conditional input.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
*   **Decay Schedule Justification:** Is the time-weighted function (e.g., $\omega_t$) derived from principled reasoning or systematic search, rather than being an arbitrary heuristic? Is sensitivity analysis provided for different decay rates?
*   **Output Source Attribution:** Do intermediate visualization or metric analyses confirm that the main diffusion branch surpasses the prior branch's quality in later timesteps, proving the model learns beyond the prior?
*   **Robustness to Prior Quality:** Has the authors analyzed or discussed what happens if the conditional prior is noisy or inaccurate? Does the adaptive mechanism prevent error propagation from a flawed prior?
*   **Ablation of Adaptivity:** Is there a comparison against static or non-adaptive guidance to prove that the *adaptive* nature is necessary for preventing late-stage detail loss?

### Principle 3: Architectural Novelty Justification via Synergistic Integration Over Modular Assembly in Diffusion Backbones

**Definition:**
When evaluating frameworks that integrate established components (e.g., LSTMs, FFT, Attention) into diffusion models, reviewers must distinguish between genuine architectural innovation and mere "modular assembly." The core criterion is whether the integration creates emergent properties that address specific limitations of the diffusion process (e.g., Markovian isolation) that neither component could solve alone. Reviewers should scrutinize claims of novelty by checking if the authors have articulated and validated the *synergy* between the temporal memory unit and the denoising trajectory, rather than simply reporting additive gains. The evaluation must also consider whether simpler baselines (e.g., standard LSTM-U-Net) were adequately compared to justify the complexity of the proposed specialized modules (e.g., tLSTM).

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
*   **Synergy Validation:** Do ablation studies show that the combined system performs significantly better than the sum of its parts, or better than standard implementations of the same components?
*   **Baseline Adequacy:** Were strong, relevant baselines (e.g., existing recurrent U-Nets, other frequency-domain diffusion methods) included to prove the proposed modification is superior to standard adaptations?
*   **Theoretical Alignment:** Is there a coherent theoretical argument explaining *why* this specific integration (e.g., gating mechanism modified for timesteps) is suited for diffusion dynamics, beyond empirical trial-and-error?
*   **Generalizability of Design:** Is the proposed module demonstrated to be a reusable building block applicable to other diffusion architectures, or is it over-fitted to a specific backbone?

### Principle 4: Comprehensive Computational Efficiency Profiling and Performance-Cost Trade-off Transparency in 3D Medical Models

**Definition:**
Given the inherent resource intensity of 3D volumetric diffusion models, reviewers must demand comprehensive computational profiling that goes beyond inference speed. The evaluation requires a multi-dimensional efficiency assessment including training time, GPU memory footprint, GFLOPs, and inference latency, benchmarked against both non-diffusion SOTA and recent efficient diffusion baselines under strictly identical hardware and resolution settings. Crucially, the work must transparently discuss the performance-cost trade-off: if the method incurs higher costs, the marginal gain in segmentation accuracy or stability must be explicitly quantified and justified as clinically or practically significant. Hiding efficiency metrics in appendices or omitting key comparisons (e.g., against lightweight diffusion variants) should be flagged as a major deficiency.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
*   **Multi-Dimensional Metrics:** Are at least four key indicators (Training Time, Inference Time, Memory, FLOPs) reported in the main text or easily accessible summary?
*   **Fair Benchmarking Protocol:** Were all baselines re-implemented or evaluated under identical patch sizes, batch sizes, and hardware to ensure valid comparison?
*   **Marginal Utility Analysis:** Does the paper explicitly discuss whether the X% increase in cost yields a Y% improvement in critical metrics (e.g., HD95, boundary precision) that justifies deployment?
*   **Scalability Evidence:** Is there analysis of how computational cost scales with volume size or diffusion steps, informing practical deployment constraints?

### Principle 5: Multi-Center Domain Shift Robustness and Clinical Generalizability Validation in Heterogeneous Medical Datasets

**Definition:**
For medical segmentation research claiming robustness, evaluation on single-center or homogeneous datasets is insufficient. Reviewers must verify that the method is tested on multi-center datasets with confirmed domain shifts (e.g., different scanners, protocols, patient populations). The assessment should look for evidence of heterogeneity (e.g., t-SNE visualizations, statistical distribution analysis) and evaluate whether the model's performance remains stable across these distinct domains compared to baselines. Furthermore, the evaluation should prioritize metrics sensitive to clinical utility (e.g., HD95 for boundary safety, qualitative failure case analysis) over aggregate metrics like mean Dice, ensuring the claimed robustness translates to reliable performance in realistic, variable clinical environments.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
*   **Heterogeneity Verification:** Is the domain shift between datasets quantitatively or visually demonstrated, rather than just asserted?
*   **Cross-Domain Stability:** Does the method maintain its relative advantage over baselines consistently across all centers/domains, or does it degrade disproportionately in specific out-of-distribution scenarios?
*   **Clinical Metric Priority:** Are boundary-sensitive and worst-case metrics (HD95, ASD) reported alongside overlap metrics (Dice/IoU) to assess safety-critical reliability?
*   **Qualitative Failure Analysis:** Are representative failure cases under domain shift analyzed to understand the limits of the model's generalizability?
