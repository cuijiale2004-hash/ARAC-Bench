Given Topic: Reinforcement learning fine-tuning for iterative generative language models is fundamentally constrained by the intractability of exact sequence likelihoods. Prevailing approaches require approximating multiple policy distributions per optimization step to construct importance sampling ratios, incurring substantial computational overhead and severe error amplification that intensifies with sequence length and denoising depth. This dependence on explicit ratio estimation destabilizes training and limits scalability. The core scientific problem is to establish a ratio-free policy optimization paradigm that eliminates reliance on likelihood ratio approximation while preserving theoretical improvement guarantees, thereby enabling stable and computationally efficient reinforcement learning for non-autoregressive generative models.

Given Inspiration:
1. Reverse-KL regularized policy optimization admits a closed-form solution expressible as weighted log-likelihood maximization, naturally circumventing explicit policy ratio computation.
2. Approximation errors in log-likelihood estimation are exponentially amplified when aggregated into policy ratios, necessitating an objective that relies solely on single-point likelihood evaluation.
3. Energy-guided diffusion principles indicate that generative trajectories can be steered by energy functions derived from reward signals without requiring explicit partition functions.
4. Effective learning from negative examples should extend beyond passive down-weighting to active suppression, ensuring the model explicitly unlearns low-advantage generations.
5. Iterative refinement processes inherently produce intermediate candidate solutions that constitute rich supervisory signals typically discarded by training frameworks that utilize only final outputs.
6. Advantage-weighted regression methods in off-policy reinforcement learning demonstrate that policy improvement can be achieved through weighted objectives without importance sampling corrections.
