Given Topic: The field concerns developing autonomous agents capable of multi-turn tool orchestration to solve complex, stateful tasks. Current mainstream approaches rely heavily on supervised fine-tuning with scarce, labor-intensive annotated trajectories or synthetic data, which inherently limits generalization to unseen scenarios. Conversely, direct reinforcement learning in complex tool-use environments encounters severe cold-start difficulties and training instability due to vast action spaces, sparse binary rewards, and long-horizon interactions where a single error precipitates complete task failure. The core problem is enabling stable, data-efficient acquisition of robust multi-step reasoning and tool-interaction capabilities under extreme data scarcity without dependence on expert demonstrations.

Given Inspiration:
1. Instead of solely optimizing the agent policy against a static environment, the environment itself can be dynamically adjusted to serve as a pedagogical scaffold that evolves with the learner's capability.
2. Learning complex sequential decision-making can be stabilized by decomposing the objective into a structured curriculum that progresses from syntactic mastery to abstract reasoning.
3. Failed trajectories contain richer learning signals than successful ones if the environment provides actionable, diagnostic feedback rather than sparse terminal outcomes.
4. Dense reward signals that measure incremental progress toward task completion offer more effective credit assignment than terminal binary rewards in long-horizon interactions.
5. The exploration-exploitation dilemma in sparse-reward settings can be mitigated by temporarily enriching environmental feedback during the learning process and gradually removing scaffolding to ensure final-policy robustness.
