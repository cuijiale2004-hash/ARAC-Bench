Given Topic: Research on multi-stage training paradigms for autoregressive sequence models, investigating why reinforcement optimization following generative pre-training enables generalization on complex reasoning tasks where standard next-token prediction stagnates. Current approaches face prohibitive sample complexity when learning from natural data mixtures dominated by short direct responses alongside rare elaborate demonstrations, leaving a critical gap in understanding how reward-based fine-tuning overcomes pre-training limitations and induces increased test-time computation. The work addresses the fundamental optimization dynamics by which reward-weighted objectives leverage heterogeneous demonstration structure to transform rare long-form supervision into accessible learning signals.

Given Inspiration:
1. Natural training corpora exhibit extreme imbalance between concise direct answers and verbose reasoning traces, yet both encode identical underlying tasks.
2. Empirical evidence that reinforcement post-training precipitates sudden accuracy gains concurrent with response length expansion, suggesting length growth is a mechanistic driver of capability acquisition.
3. Theoretical hardness results indicating that direct input-output supervision for complex logical functions requires statistically prohibitive samples, whereas stepwise intermediate traces render the same functions tractable.
4. The insight that reward-based verification operates as an adaptive resampling mechanism, amplifying low-probability successful elaborate behaviors present in the pre-training distribution.
5. The recognition that autoregressive architectures face an estimation barrier rather than an approximation barrier, possessing sufficient representational capacity yet failing to acquire complex mappings without sequential scaffolding.
