Given Topic: The field of deep learning optimization is hindered by the theoretical intractability of highly non-convex loss landscapes, which obstructs precise prediction and control of training dynamics across diverse architectures and optimizers. While empirical training exhibits remarkably regular, convex-like geometric properties, existing frameworks lack rigorous, non-asymptotic tools to translate these observations into principled learning rate design and loss forecasting. Consequently, the relationships among training horizon, model scale, optimal learning rate, and converged loss remain fragmented, impeding systematic resource allocation. This work targets the fundamental challenge of quantifying emergent convex-like regularity to establish unified predictive laws that jointly govern loss evolution and learning rate scaling in large-scale model training.

Given Inspiration:
1. Classical convex optimization theory establishes rigorous upper bounds between learning rate sequences and loss trajectories, providing a foundational perspective that analytical control over step-size policies can predict optimization outcomes without exhaustive empirical exploration.
2. Empirical observations across deep learning regimes reveal that training dynamics rapidly exhibit benign geometric properties during optimization, suggesting that convex analytic principles can be extended beyond traditional convex settings.
3. Current scaling paradigms separately address loss convergence or learning rate scheduling, leaving a critical gap in understanding their coupled dependence on training duration and model capacity, which motivates a coalesced predictive law.
4. The realization that learning rate schedule qualifications can be assessed through abstract analytical conditions without model training suggests that schedule efficacy can be decoupled from task-specific empirical search via theoretical inspection.
5. The persistent similarity of loss curve morphologies across distinct model families, tasks, and optimization algorithms indicates underlying universal geometric regularities, prompting a model-agnostic, data-driven abstraction of optimization dynamics.
