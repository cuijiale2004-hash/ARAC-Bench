Given Topic: Research in multimodal large language models has significantly advanced video understanding; however, efficiently compressing high-dimensional visual tokens while preserving intricate spatiotemporal interactions remains a critical bottleneck. Mainstream approaches predominantly rely on simplistic spatial downsampling strategies, such as uniform pooling or interpolation, which inherently neglect heterogeneous temporal dynamics and cross-frame dependencies essential for video reasoning. Moreover, existing training-free enhancement strategies are primarily designed for image-language paradigms and prove inadequate when adapted to modern video-language architectures. Consequently, a pivotal scientific issue lies in developing a training-free mechanism that can selectively retain semantically salient visual information and capture multi-grained temporal dynamics, thereby optimizing visual token representations for long-form and general video understanding without increasing computational overhead or requiring architectural modifications.

Given Inspiration:
1. Real-world video content exhibits heterogeneous temporal dynamics spanning multiple granularities, from rapid micro-motions to gradual scene evolutions, motivating multi-scale temporal modeling rather than uniform frame sampling.
2. Empirical observations indicate a strong positive correlation between visual token feature magnitudes and their semantic saliency, suggesting that intrinsic feature statistics can serve as reliable indicators for spatial information importance.
3. Existing visual token compression paradigms uniformly treat all spatial regions and temporal segments, causing disproportionate information loss in semantically rich regions while overlooking cross-frame contextual dependencies.
4. Hierarchical signal decomposition strategies, widely utilized in multi-scale image analysis, provide a structural precedent for decomposing temporal sequences into layered granularities to capture both local dynamics and global contextual coherence.
5. The prohibitive computational cost of fine-tuning large video-language models necessitates purely inference-time, parameter-free enhancement mechanisms that reorganize and reweight visual tokens without modifying underlying network architectures.
