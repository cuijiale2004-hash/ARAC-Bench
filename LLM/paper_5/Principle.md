## Principle 1: Causal Contribution Verification of the Design-Logic Guidance Mechanism at Full Scale in Multidisciplinary Reasoning Data Synthesis

**Definition:**
When a data synthesis paper claims a novel core mechanism (here, "Design Logic" as reusable meta-knowledge guiding question generation), reviewers must verify that this mechanism itself causes the observed benefits, rather than serving as a sophisticated indexing device or an incidental byproduct of upstream data curation. In this review, the pivotal challenge was that the "w/o Design Logic" ablation on a 300K subset showed only minor performance drops, leading multiple reviewers to question whether simple exemplar retrieval alone would suffice. The decisive evidence came from a full-scale (3.04M) ablation revealing a five-point gap on GPQA-Diamond, demonstrating that the effect size of a guidance mechanism can be severely compressed at small scales. Additionally, because all ablation variants share upstream filtering and high-quality corpora, even "naive" variants are inherently strong, so small gaps at small scale cannot refute the mechanism's value. Reviewers should also require direct verification that the generated outputs actually adhere to the claimed guidance mechanism (e.g., the 84.69% logic-consistency rate), establishing that the mechanism is operative rather than decorative. Finally, the mechanism's worth should be assessed not only by endpoint metrics but by the unique controllability it affords over difficulty, diversity, and question type—properties that pure exemplar imitation lacks. This principle discriminates genuine methodological innovation from repackaged retrieval-and-imitation pipelines.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Scale-matched ablations:** Are the key ablations of the core mechanism conducted at the full data scale, or are conclusions drawn only from small subsets where mechanism effects may be diluted or masked?
- **Confound acknowledgment:** Does the analysis recognize that ablation variants inherit benefits from shared pipeline components (data filtering, corpus quality), and does it interpret small observed gaps accordingly rather than overclaiming or underclaiming?
- **Mechanism adherence auditing:** Is there independent verification (e.g., by a stronger external LLM) that the synthesized artifacts actually follow the intended guidance structure, rather than assuming the prompt guarantees compliance?
- **Beyond-endpoint utility:** Does the work demonstrate the mechanism's unique affordances—explicit and reusable control over difficulty, diversity, and question type—that simpler baselines cannot provide even when their final metrics are close?
- **Effect localization consistency:** Are the mechanism's gains most pronounced on the hardest, most reasoning-intensive benchmarks (e.g., GPQA-Diamond), consistent with the mechanism's stated purpose of enabling complex multi-step reasoning?

## Principle 2: Independent Validation of LLM-Generated Quality Labels and Multi-Dimensional Characterization (Difficulty, Diversity, Disciplinary Coverage) of Synthetic Reasoning Data

**Definition:**
Synthetic reasoning dataset papers rely heavily on LLM-assigned metadata—difficulty levels, discipline labels, question types—and on LLM-based judgments of answerability and correctness. Reviewers must assess whether these labels are validated against independent or ground-truth standards, because unvalidated LLM self-judgment renders headline claims (e.g., "our data is more difficult") circular and self-certifying. Strong work audits label accuracy on random samples using a stronger independent verifier (e.g., GPT-5.1 checking Qwen3-assigned labels, reporting 90–97% appropriateness rates), and calibrates the labeling procedure on well-understood public benchmarks (showing GSM8K is predominantly "Easy" while GPQA-Diamond is predominantly "Hard") so that relative difficulty claims are anchored to accepted references. It further characterizes the data along multiple quantitative dimensions—difficulty distributions, several complementary embedding-space diversity metrics, and disciplinary distribution—rather than asserting quality via a single statistic. Reviewers should check whether few-shot labeling prompts are justified as format-standardizers rather than accuracy-determiners, ideally supported by in-context learning literature. Cross-checking consistency between independently generated artifacts (reference answers versus long-CoT responses) provides an additional reliability signal, provided the open-endedness of many disciplines is properly taken into account. This principle separates rigorous dataset science from unverifiable self-assessment.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Independent label auditing:** Is a stronger or different model (or, ideally, human annotation) used to verify discipline, difficulty, and question-type labels on a substantial random sample, with accuracy rates explicitly reported?
- **Procedural calibration:** Is the identical labeling pipeline applied to commonly used benchmarks to demonstrate that it produces sensible, widely accepted relative distributions (e.g., GSM8K vs. GPQA-Diamond difficulty profiles)?
- **Prompt-sensitivity justification:** Does the work argue—empirically or via citation—that few-shot exemplars standardize output format rather than determine label content, addressing concerns about fragile prompt engineering?
- **Multi-metric characterization:** Are difficulty, diversity (multiple complementary distance-based metrics), and disciplinary coverage each quantified against baseline datasets, rather than claimed anecdotally or with a single metric?
- **Cross-artifact consistency:** Are independently generated components (reference answers vs. CoT responses) checked for agreement, with agreement rates interpreted in light of the open-ended nature of humanities, social science, and applied-domain questions?

## Principle 3: Confound-Controlled Baseline Comparisons for Synthetic Data Efficacy: Equalized Size, Regenerated Responses, Difficulty-Matched Controls, and Complete Training-Regime References

**Definition:**
The central empirical claim of a synthetic data paper—"models trained on our data outperform models trained on baseline datasets"—is credible only if confounding variables are systematically eliminated. Reviewers must check that dataset sizes are equalized across compared methods, since larger synthetic corpora trivially win. They must check that response quality is held constant: if the proposed dataset uses frontier-model long-CoT responses while baselines retain simple native responses, gains may stem from the response generator rather than the questions; regenerating baseline responses with the identical teacher model and settings isolates the question-quality effect. A particularly probing control is the difficulty-matched upsampling test: mixing and upsampling hard questions from baseline datasets to match the proposed data's difficulty distribution reveals whether the advantage reduces to difficulty alone or genuinely stems from diversity and disciplinary coverage. Complete training-regime references—raw base model, official instruction-tuned model, and continued SFT on instruction-tuned models—establish the floor, the ceiling, and the remaining headroom of the data's utility. Evaluation should span both near-saturated benchmarks (where ceiling effects and known label errors must be acknowledged) and unsaturated hard benchmarks (where sensitivity to data quality actually resides), plus out-of-focus domains (e.g., open-ended mathematics) to probe generality. This principle distinguishes convincing causal attribution of data quality from leaderboard artifacts and favorable comparisons.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Size and budget equalization:** Are all compared datasets sampled to identical instance counts and trained under identical SFT hyperparameters, with any mixing of data sources justified as a deliberate controlled variable?
- **Response-source control:** Are baseline responses regenerated with the same teacher model and generation settings, so that performance differences are attributable solely to question quality?
- **Difficulty-confound test:** Does the work test whether matching the difficulty distribution via upsampling hard baseline questions closes the gap, and does it correctly interpret any residual gap as arising from diversity and disciplinary coverage?
- **Training-regime completeness:** Are base-model (no SFT) results, official instruction-tuned model results, and continued-SFT-on-instruct results all provided to frame the data's marginal utility for already-strong models?
- **Saturation awareness and breadth:** Does the evaluation cover near-ceiling and unsaturated benchmarks, acknowledge ceiling/label-error effects when interpreting scaling behavior, and include out-of-focus domains (e.g., GSM8K/MATH-500 for a non-math-centric dataset) with honest interpretation?

## Principle 4: Reproducibility, Openness of Reusable Meta-Resources, and Transparency of Proprietary Dependencies and Computational Costs in LLM-Driven Synthesis Pipelines

**Definition:**
Large-scale synthesis pipelines often depend on proprietary assets (a 150M-item question bank, proprietary book corpora) and frontier LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B), which creates legitimate community concerns about reproducibility and practical accessibility. Reviewers must evaluate whether the work's transferable contribution—here, the reusable Design Logic library with difficulty, type, and discipline labels—is genuinely released so that others can apply it to their own raw corpora, thereby decoupling the methodological contribution from the proprietary inputs. They should assess whether the authors convincingly argue that the pipeline works on arbitrary, including low-quality or fully public, question banks through a documented filtering pipeline, and whether at least some data sources are public (e.g., FineFineWeb). Transparency about computational cost (e.g., ~64,512 H20 GPU hours) and stage-wise data retention and filtering ratios (e.g., a 2.6% reasoning-density pass rate for web text, <0.03% deduplication removal) is essential for assessing the quality–cost trade-off and the feasibility of adoption by other groups. Reviewers should also weigh the "one-time community cost" justification: incurring high synthesis cost once in order to release reusable data and meta-resources can be a net community benefit, but only when release commitments are concrete and verifiable (e.g., sample data and the full logic library in supplementary material). This principle separates reproducible, community-enabling resource papers from non-replicable engineering demonstrations that showcase results without enabling follow-up work.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Decoupling of method from proprietary inputs:** Is there a credible argument or evidence that the pipeline applies to public question banks and corpora of arbitrary initial quality, so the method is not contingent on privileged data access?
- **Release of reusable artifacts:** Are the core meta-resources (the design logic library with its labels) and representative samples of the final data concretely released or firmly committed, enabling direct reuse and validation?
- **Dependency transparency:** Are the roles of frontier LLMs at each pipeline stage made explicit, with justification of why they are needed and an honest statement of compatibility with smaller models?
- **Cost and yield reporting:** Are total compute (GPU hours) and per-stage filtering/retention ratios disclosed, clarifying whether filtering is quality-driven or budget-driven and what headroom remains for scaling?
- **Documentation completeness:** Are all processing steps, prompts, and hyperparameters provided in sufficient detail for independent reimplementation, exceeding the disclosure norms typical of industrial post-training recipes?

## Principle 5: Error Accumulation and Noise Control Analysis in Multi-Stage Synthesis Pipelines, with Honest Scope Delimitation Between Question Synthesis Quality and Response Correctness

**Definition:**
Multi-stage LLM pipelines (labeling → filtering → logic extraction → retrieval → generation → response synthesis) risk compounding errors, and reviewers must require a stage-wise account of where noise enters and how it is contained. A key analytical distinction is whether early-stage errors (e.g., discipline mislabels) merely distort metadata distributions while leaving the logical content quality of final question–response pairs intact, versus errors that propagate into actual flaws in the artifacts themselves. Funnel-style designs with filtering, deduplication, decontamination, and coarse-to-fine retrieval should be examined as explicit noise-control mechanisms rather than dismissed as incidental engineering complexity. Reviewers should require direct audits of content validity—question answerability and completeness (96.7%), design-logic adherence (84.69%)—performed by an independent verifier on random samples. Equally important is honest scope delimitation: when a paper's core contribution is question synthesis, it should clearly state that response generation is secondary and regenerable by stronger future models, while still demonstrating that its responses are consistent enough (71.48% reference-answer agreement, contextualized by open-ended disciplines) to support effective SFT. Claims about benchmark-leakage safety (13-gram decontamination) and rare near-duplicate generation further substantiate noise control and should be checked. This principle distinguishes trustworthy pipeline science from fragile constructions whose final metrics may conceal accumulated noise.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Stage-wise error accounting:** Does the work identify which stages can introduce errors and classify their downstream impact (metadata distribution vs. final content quality), rather than treating the pipeline as an undifferentiated black box?
- **Explicit noise-control mechanisms:** Are filtering, deduplication, decontamination, and coarse-to-fine matching presented and justified as deliberate error-containment devices, with retention/removal statistics reported?
- **Independent content audits:** Are answerability, logic adherence, and label appropriateness verified on random samples by an independent strong model, with realistic rates reported rather than perfect scores?
- **Scope honesty:** Does the paper clearly delimit its core claim (question synthesis quality) from secondary components (response correctness), avoid overclaiming, and position itself as compatible with—rather than a replacement for—downstream quality-control strategies?
- **Downstream triangulation:** Is the absence of harmful noise accumulation corroborated by downstream evidence (stable data-scaling curves, disproportionate gains on hard expert-level benchmarks, no hallucination or degradation symptoms after SFT)?
