Given Topic: Offline reinforcement learning seeks to learn effective policies from static datasets without further environment interaction, which is essential in domains where data collection is costly or hazardous. A persistent challenge is distributional shift, wherein learned policies query out-of-distribution state-action pairs, leading to severe value overestimation and degraded performance. Existing methods predominantly depend on global value or policy models trained over entire datasets, which are ill-suited to tasks exhibiting inherent compositional structure. In many control problems, the state space encompasses diverse subtasks with distinct local transition dynamics and value patterns; knowledge acquired in one region may not transfer to others, and geometrically proximate states may correspond to semantically different behaviors with divergent long-horizon returns. Under limited offline data and absent exploration, fitting a single shared global approximator fails to capture these localized variations, thereby compromising the accuracy of value estimation and the optimality of extracted policies.

Given Inspiration:
1. The compositional nature of complex control tasks, wherein the state space comprises multiple subtasks with heterogeneous local dynamics and value patterns that resist unified global approximation.
2. The observation that geometric proximity in state space does not guarantee semantic or behavioral similarity, necessitating context-aware value estimation that distinguishes locally coherent behavioral clusters.
3. The limitations of global offline value learning under data scarcity and distributional shift, where insufficient coverage prevents a single approximator from accurately modeling diverse regional behaviors.
4. The insight that adaptive inference mechanisms can condition value estimation on locally relevant experiential context, enabling dynamic localization without explicit subtask labels or predefined decomposition.
5. The theoretical perspective that value functions exhibit localized structural regularities within restricted state neighborhoods, suggesting that neighborhood-specific functional adaptation can reduce approximation error and improve policy optimality.
