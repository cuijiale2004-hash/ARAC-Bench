Given Topic: Research in time-series classification under partial observability, where operational constraints restrict inference to truncated sequence prefixes. Mainstream approaches assume full-sequence availability during both training and deployment, causing severe generalization failure when class-discriminative patterns emerge after the observed window. Knowledge distillation from full-sequence models is a natural countermeasure, yet prevailing methods presuppose that teacher and student process identically distributed inputs. When the student operates on incomplete and ambiguous prefixes, direct alignment with full-context supervision signals introduces a severe representational mismatch that overwhelms the student. The central scientific challenge is to devise a knowledge transfer paradigm that respects the incomplete nature of partial observations, provides diverse and adaptive teacher guidance, and faithfully encodes the statistical structure of full-length temporal knowledge without relying on deterministic point-to-point feature matching.

Given Inspiration:
1. Inverse problem formulation: Student representations derived from partial inputs can be conceptualized as degraded measurements of an underlying ideal full-context representation, naturally framing the distillation objective as a conditional restoration task.
2. Iterative generative restoration: Diffusion-based generative priors provide a principled mechanism for progressively recovering plausible clean signals from corrupted or incomplete observations through stochastic denoising.
3. Distributional knowledge modeling: Teacher knowledge can be treated as a probability distribution over plausible target representations rather than a deterministic point estimate, enabling richer and more adaptive supervision.
4. Controlled stochastic diversity: Controlled randomness within a learned prior manifold yields multiple valid completions of incomplete student states, mitigating the brittleness of single-view teacher supervision.
5. Dynamic target progression: Optimal supervision should evolve with the student’s current capability, providing targets that are progressively refined rather than statically fixed throughout training.
6. Collective knowledge fidelity: Aggregating information from a collection of plausible teacher signals, rather than relying on a single corresponding signal, better reveals the statistical structure and decision boundaries of the teacher’s feature space.
