Given Topic: In the domain of persistent large language model agents operating in continuous task streams, existing systems suffer from catastrophic neglect of accumulated interaction history, approaching each query in isolation and repeatedly committing similar errors. Current memory mechanisms predominantly retain raw behavioral trajectories or successful procedural templates, which inherently lack transferable reasoning patterns and systematically discard the counterfactual knowledge embedded in unsuccessful experiences. Concurrently, test-time scaling strategies for interactive agents remain agnostic to historical learning, failing to exploit the bidirectional synergy between memory curation and inference-time computation. The central scientific challenge is to construct a memory framework that abstracts generalizable reasoning strategies from both positive and negative experiences, and to establish how depth-wise experiential scaling per task can be harmonized with memory evolution to enable autonomous, ground-truth-free self-improvement.

Given Inspiration:
1. Human learning theory suggests that durable expertise arises from extracting generalizable principles from both successful and unsuccessful experiences, rather than from rote memorization of correct procedures.
2. Contrastive learning perspectives indicate that juxtaposing multiple solution attempts can surface consistent reasoning patterns while suppressing spurious or idiosyncratic behaviors.
3. Metacognitive self-reflection demonstrates that iterative re-examination of one's own reasoning traces yields valuable corrective signals that are absent from final outputs alone.
4. Closed-loop cybernetic systems imply that effective memory must operate through continuous cycles of retrieval, application, and consolidation rather than as static archival storage.
5. Inference-time scaling principles suggest that deeper exploration within individual tasks generates more informative experiential distributions than superficial coverage across many tasks.
6. Knowledge transfer research indicates that abstract strategic representations generalize more robustly across diverse scenarios than concrete action sequences or domain-specific workflows.
