Given Topic: Reinforcement learning alignment of diffusion language models remains hindered by the intractability of exact sequence log-likelihoods, which prevents direct application of standard policy gradient estimators. Current approaches resort to one-sided variational approximations as surrogates for the true likelihood, yet these approximations incur systematic gradient bias. Specifically, reliance on lower-bound surrogates alone restricts valid policy updates to non-negative reward configurations and fails to reliably suppress low-reward trajectories, ultimately compromising alignment fidelity. The core scientific challenge is to establish a principled, tractable likelihood estimation framework for policy gradient computation that robustly accommodates both positive and negative reward signals without introducing prohibitive bias.

Given Inspiration:
1. The theoretical deficiency that single-sided variational lower bounds do not guarantee a decrease in true log-likelihood when penalizing undesirable trajectories, suggesting the necessity of complementary upper-bound constraints to sandwich the true policy probability.
2. The mathematical insight from generalized variational inference that tractable evidence upper bounds can be constructed via importance-weighted divergences, furnishing a dual counterpart to conventional lower-bound objectives.
3. The distributional observation that inference-time generation follows structured partial-masking patterns, motivating training-time corruption strategies to align with these patterns rather than uniform random masking to stabilize gradient estimation.
4. The bias-variance trade-off principle in Monte Carlo policy gradients, indicating that an interpolation between optimistic and pessimistic likelihood surrogates can suppress gradient variance and stabilize optimization dynamics.
5. The optimization-theoretic asymmetry that likelihood maximization and minimization benefit from contrasting bound properties, inspiring differentiated bound selection for advantageous versus disadvantageous trajectories.
6. The empirical intuition that conservative likelihood approximations alone produce unstable penalties for low-reward samples, motivating a hybrid confidence-aware weighting mechanism that modulates correction strength per token.
