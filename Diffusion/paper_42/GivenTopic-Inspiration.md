Given Topic: Diffusion models have emerged as a dominant paradigm in generative modeling, yet a fundamental theoretical gap persists between the training objective, which theoretically admits closed-form memorization solutions, and empirical observations that trained networks consistently produce novel, diverse samples. Existing theoretical analyses remain fragmented, relying on oversimplified parametric assumptions or phenomenological inductive biases that fail to jointly characterize memorization and generalization within a unified framework. This disconnect poses critical challenges for privacy auditing, model interpretability, and trustworthy deployment. The core research problem is to establish a principled theoretical framework that rigorously distinguishes the conditions and mechanisms that drive memorization from those that enable generalization, thereby reconciling theoretical expectations with observed empirical behavior in deep generative models.

Given Inspiration:
1. Representation-centric view of generative modeling: The work is inspired by the perspective that internal network representations are not incidental byproducts but central structures governing whether a model memorizes training data or learns underlying distributions.
2. Duality between distribution learning and representation learning: Motivated by empirical evidence that generative models concurrently learn semantically meaningful features, the work posits that characterizing generalization requires bridging the training objective with the geometry of the learned representation space.
3. Geometric signatures of memorization versus generalization: The work assumes that distinct generative behaviors manifest in characteristic activation geometries, where memorized content produces localized, concentrated representational codes and generalized content yields distributed, statistically balanced codes.
4. Local data density and statistical sufficiency: Drawing on statistical learning principles, the work is motivated by the intuition that networks resort to instance-specific encoding when local data is scarce, but extract underlying distributional statistics when local sampling is abundant.
5. Low-dimensional manifold structure of real data: Grounded in the view that natural data concentrates around structured modes or manifolds, the work assumes that the geometry of these underlying data clusters fundamentally shapes the network's learned internal representations.
6. Optimization dynamics and structural inductive biases: Inspired by the observation that training algorithms exhibit preferences for specific geometric configurations in parameter space, the work considers how optimization biases influence whether networks encode instance-level details versus population-level statistics.
