Given Topic: Event-based multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have emerged to address the limitations of frame-based systems in high-speed and low-light scenarios. However, prevailing approaches predominantly convert asynchronous event streams into dense, image-like representations, thereby disregarding the intrinsic spatiotemporal sparsity of neuromorphic data. This paradigm incurs substantial computational redundancy, severely constraining inference throughput and limiting the temporal extent of processable sequences. Additionally, publicly available instruction datasets exhibit restricted scene diversity, limited temporal coverage, and inconsistent annotation quality, which impede generalizable model training. The central scientific problem is to develop computationally efficient architectures that natively leverage the sparse, high-temporal-resolution nature of event streams to enable scalable understanding of long-duration sequences without degrading reasoning fidelity.

Given Inspiration:
1. The asynchronous spatiotemporal sparsity inherent to neuromorphic event data represents a structural property that should be exploited for computational efficiency rather than eliminated via dense image-like conversion.
2. Temporal redundancy at microsecond resolution suggests that adjacent event intervals with high correlation or low motion activity can be adaptively merged without significant information loss.
3. Spatial information density in event streams is highly non-uniform, where empty or sparse sensor regions carry minimal semantic relevance compared to activated areas.
4. Conventional visual token reduction strategies designed for regular grid-structured frames are fundamentally incompatible with the irregular, point-cloud-like distribution of event tokens.
5. A progressive curriculum exposing models to incrementally longer temporal horizons can stabilize training dynamics and foster robust cross-modal alignment for extended sequences.
6. The local event density within spatial neighborhoods serves as a natural indicator of information salience, which can guide selective allocation of computational resources.
7. Semantic coherence between adjacent temporal windows provides a criterion for compressible temporal segments, distinct from rigid uniform binning.
8. Comprehensive event-based understanding requires large-scale instruction data spanning diverse scenes and a wide spectrum of temporal durations from milliseconds to seconds.
