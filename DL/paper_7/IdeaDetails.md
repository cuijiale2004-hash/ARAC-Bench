1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points
Modern neural networks trained in the overparametrized regime exhibit a striking yet paradoxical property: basins of attraction in the loss landscape are often connected by low-loss paths, implying that the landscape is far less rugged than once assumed and that minima which appear isolated are, in fact, linked by paths of nearly constant loss. This phenomenon, known as mode connectivity, suggests that distinct solutions found by standard optimization algorithms exist within a single broad "valley" of solutions. At the same time, however, optimization dynamics display a seemingly contradictory behavior. Standard training with stochastic gradient descent (SGD), with or without momentum, converges to a well-defined minimum and rarely explores regions of parameter space corresponding to these connecting paths. Optimization dynamics generally remain confined to a single convex basin and rarely explore intermediate points, raising the question of why models do not diffuse into other regions of parameter space that share low loss. Existing perspectives that focus solely on energetic barriers (derived from gradients of the energy or loss) fail to resolve this paradox, as they cannot explain confinement when the loss profile is nearly flat.

2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions
The core research motivation is to resolve the paradox between energetic connectivity and dynamical confinement by recognizing the role of entropic forces generated by curvature variations along connecting paths. The central scientific questions are: How can regions of parameter space that are energetically connected become effectively disconnected in the presence of optimization noise? What geometric features of the loss landscape generate these barriers? And how do entropic forces interact with stochastic optimization dynamics to confine solutions to specific basins?

3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy
The overall core idea is that although the loss may be nearly flat along paths connecting minima, the curvature of the landscape typically increases away from found minima, producing effective barriers that bias stochastic dynamics back toward the endpoints. These barriers emerge from the interaction between fluctuations induced by SGD noise and the Hessian spectrum along low-energy paths. The design philosophy draws on statistical physics, where the state of a system is governed not only by energetic forces but also by entropic forces arising from thermal fluctuations. In deep learning, stochasticity from finite learning rates and minibatch sampling induces an effective temperature, making entropic contributions non-negligible. Optimization is biased toward broader, flatter regions of the landscape because they occupy a larger volume in parameter space (higher entropy), even if they are not lower in loss. Thus, regions that are energetically connected become effectively disconnected by entropic barriers.

4. Core Innovation Points
1. We show empirically that the curvature along minimum-loss paths between minima increases away from the endpoints.
2. We argue that such a "bump" in the curvature leads to an entropic barrier, and that such entropic barriers lead to confinement of solutions even when the loss is low along a path in parameter space.
3. We show that despite the existence of low-loss connecting paths between solutions, entropic forces confine models to specific regions of parameter space.
4. We show that entropic barriers between minima persist longer than energetic barriers, when considering models that shared the first k epochs of training, suggesting that both energetic and entropic forces are responsible for the final region of parameter space that a model ends up in.

5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution
The technical solution proceeds in five main steps: First, training a collection of image-classification models on CIFAR-10 using Wide ResNet and ResNet architectures, each initialized from different random seeds to obtain diverse distinct minima. Second, constructing low-loss connecting paths (Minimum Energy Paths, MEPs) between every pair of minima using the AutoNEB algorithm. Third, measuring the curvature along the obtained paths using complementary Hessian-based statistics (maximum eigenvalue, trace, and SVD of the score matrix) to characterize how flatness varies between endpoints and the interior. Fourth, studying optimization dynamics constrained to the MEP by projecting SGD updates onto the nearest path segment using a novel k-step projected SGD algorithm, which isolates how stochasticity interacts with curvature to reveal entropic forces. Fifth, analyzing linearly connected minima via splitting epoch experiments to compare the persistence of energetic versus entropic barriers over the course of training.

6. Detailed Module Design

6.1 Theoretical Toy Model Module
To illustrate the mechanism of entropic forces, a simple toy model is constructed using a Brownian particle evolving in a two-dimensional potential.
- Potential Definition: $V(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} g(y)x^2$, where $g(y) > 0$.
- Dynamics: $\dot{x} = -\nabla V (x) + \xi(t)$, where $\xi$ is white delta correlated Gaussian noise.
- Coordinate Roles: $y$ corresponds to soft modes (directions with nearly flat curvature), and $x$ represents stiff modes (directions associated with large eigenvalues of the Hessian).
- Fast Variable Averaging: For fixed $y$, the distribution of $x$ is Gaussian with variance $\langle x^2\rangle = Tg(y)^{-1}$. When $x$ relaxes much faster than $y$, averaging over the fast variable $x$ yields an effective dynamics for $y$:
$\dot{y} = - \partial_y g(y) \langle x^2\rangle + \xi = -T \frac{1}{g(y)}\frac{dg(y)}{dy} + \xi$.
- Effective Potential: This equation is rewritten as gradient-flow dynamics generated by an effective potential $V_{eff}(y)$:
$\dot{y} = -\frac{dV_{eff}(y)}{dy} + \xi$, with $V_{eff}(y) = T \ln g(y)$.
- Stationary Distribution: The dynamics converge to $P (y) \propto \exp[-\frac{V_{eff}(y)}{T}]$.
The force is proportional to the negative derivative of $g(y)$, driving the system towards smaller values of $g(y)$ (flatter directions). These forces are entropic as they are proportional to the effective temperature $T$ and vanish without noise.

6.2 Minimum Energy Path (MEP) Construction Module
Low-loss connecting paths are identified using the Automatic Nudged Elastic Band (AutoNEB) algorithm.
- Initialization: $k$ intermediate pivots are initialized along the straight-line path between two minima.
- Optimization: Pivots are optimized such that they evolve as if connected by elastic springs, while minimizing the loss orthogonal to the path.
- Adaptive Refinement: AutoNEB dynamically adds new pivots whenever the loss along a segment exceeds a predefined threshold, ensuring a smooth, low-loss path. The optimization does not change the length of segments; inserting a pivot results in shorter segments.
- Parameterization: The relative position along the MEP is reported in terms of pivot index, normalized by the total number of pivots. Note this does not reflect actual metric distance due to non-uniform pivot density and length.
- Refinement Cycles: MEPs are computed using a sequence of refinement cycles with decreasing learning rates: four cycles with parameters (0.1, 10), (5×10−2, 5), (10−2, 5), and (10−3, 5), where tuples denote (learning rate, number of epochs).

6.3 Curvature Measurement Module
Curvature is measured using three independent summary statistics of the Hessian spectrum $H \equiv \nabla^2_\theta L(\theta)$:
1. Maximum Eigenvalue: Estimated using the power iteration method.
2. Trace of the Hessian: Estimated using the connection to the Fisher Information Matrix near a minimum. The trace is computed by summing diagonal elements of the outer product of the score.
3. Spectrum via SVD: Compute the Fisher matrix on a small random subset of the training dataset of size $E$, and perform SVD on the resulting score matrix (shape $N \times (CE)$).
- Note on Reparameterization: While symmetries allow re-parameterization without changing the network function, for SGD optimization dynamics the Hessian governs the dynamics. Flat directions induced by symmetry do not induce a gradient ($\nabla_\alpha L(T_\alpha \theta) = 0$), making the Hessian the relevant measure for dynamics.

6.4 Dynamics Measurement Module (k-step Projected SGD)
To measure entropic forces in a controlled setting, a modified version of SGD is used.
- Conflict Consideration: Limiting observation to models on a line/MEP vs. ensuring entropic forces (arising from noisy multi-step dynamics) remain relevant.
- Algorithm Logic: Trade off by taking multiple SGD steps before projecting parameters back to the linear path or MEP. The parameter $k$ trades off the effect of entropic forces (large $k$) vs. proximity to the low-loss path (small $k$).
- Effective Time: Defined as the product of optimizer updates and learning rate, $t_{eff} = (\text{optimizer updates}) \times \eta$.

6.5 Linear Mode Connectivity Analysis Module
Following Frankle et al. (2020), networks are trained with a shared data order up to a "splitting epoch" $k$. After epoch $k$, networks see independent orderings and move away from siblings until convergence.
- Metric: Instability (relative change, max/min) in loss or curvature along the linear path between siblings.

7. All Mathematical Formulas and Symbol Definitions

Toy Model Dynamics:
$\dot{x} = -\nabla V (x) + \xi(t), \quad V (x, y) = \frac{1}{2} g(y)x^2, \quad g(y) > 0$

Noise Correlation:
$\langle \xi_i(t)\xi_j(t')\rangle = 2T\delta_{ij}\delta (t- t')$

Effective Temperature:
$T \propto \eta/B$ (where $\eta$ is learning rate, $B$ is batch size)

Fast Variable Variance:
$\langle x^2\rangle = Tg(y)^{-1}$

Effective Slow Dynamics:
$\dot{y} = - \partial_y g(y) \langle x^2\rangle+ \xi = -T \frac{1}{g(y)}\frac{dg(y)}{dy}+ \xi$

Effective Potential Dynamics:
$\dot{y} = -\frac{dV_{eff}(y)}{dy}+ \xi, \quad V_{eff}(y) = T \ln g(y), \quad \langle\xi(t) \xi(t')\rangle = 2T \delta(t- t')$

Stationary Distribution:
$P (y) \propto \exp\left[-\frac{V_{eff}(y)}{T}\right]$

Hessian Definition:
$H \equiv \nabla^2_\theta L(\theta)$

Power Iteration Update:
$v^{(n+1)} = \frac{Hv^{(n)}}{\|Hv^{(n)}\|}, \quad \text{where } Hv = \sum_\beta \frac{\partial^2 L(\theta)}{\partial\theta_\alpha \partial\theta_\beta} v_\beta$

Maximum Eigenvalue Approximation:
$\lambda_{max} \approx \|Hv^{(n)}\|$

Fisher Information Matrix:
$F(\theta^\star) \equiv \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D} \left[ s_\theta(x, y)s^\top_\theta (x, y) \right]_{\theta^\star}$

Score Definition:
$s_\theta(x, y) \equiv \nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(y | x)$

Loss Function (Negative Log-Likelihood):
$L(\theta) = -\sum_{(x,y)\in D} \log p_\theta(y | x)$

Derivative Identity at Minimum:
$\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(y|x)} [\log(p_\theta(y|x))] = 0$

Fisher-Hessian Equivalence Derivation:
$\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(y|x)} [\nabla_\theta \log(p_\theta(y|x))] = \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(y|x)} [\nabla^2_\theta p_\theta(y|x)] - \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(y|x)} [\nabla_\theta \log(p_\theta(y|x))\nabla_\theta \log(p_\theta(y|x))]$

Fisher at Minimum (Eq 8):
$F(\theta^\star) \equiv \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D} \left[ \nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(y | x)\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(y | x)^\top \right]_{\theta^\star}$

Effective Time Definition:
$t_{eff} = (\text{optimizer updates}) \times \eta$

8. Algorithm Pseudocode

Algorithm 1 k-step projected SGD
Input: A model $f_\theta(x)$, a loss function $L(\theta, x, y)$, an integer $k$, path pivots $\theta_0, \theta_1, \ldots \theta_N$, SGD learning rate $\eta$, SGD batch size $B$.
while not converged do
    for $i = 1$ to $k$ do
        Draw a batch $b \leftarrow \{x_j , y_j\}_{j=1}^B \sim D_{train}$
        $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta L(\theta, b)$
    end for
    Project $\theta$ onto the closest segment (across $n$) connecting $\theta_n$ and $\theta_{n+1}$.
end while
