Given Topic: In the field of multimodal reasoning, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has demonstrated substantial potential for enhancing vision-language models. However, a critical bottleneck lies in the cold-start phase preceding reinforcement learning. Current mainstream approaches predominantly rely on imitation-based warm-up strategies that jointly optimize reasoning content, output format, and task solutions within a single training stage. This entanglement tends to induce overfitting to training distributions, impair out-of-distribution generalization, and constrain the exploratory capacity of subsequent reinforcement learning. Furthermore, existing data construction for cold-start typically depends on costly human annotation or external teacher models with significant capability gaps, limiting scalability and transferability. The core scientific issue is how to design a cold-start paradigm that disentangles shallow surface-form alignment from deep reasoning acquisition, thereby improving generalization, stabilizing downstream training, and enabling autonomous high-quality data generation without external supervision.

Given Inspiration:
1. Mainstream cold-start strategies suffer from a structural generalization bottleneck: they tightly couple format compliance with reasoning content, causing the initialized policy to overfit to surface patterns and compromise transferability to novel task formulations.
2. The hierarchical cognition hypothesis motivates decoupling learning objectives by depth—reserving shallow, transferable structural regularities for the initialization phase while delegating deep logical reasoning to subsequent optimization stages.
3. The inherent limitation of external teacher distillation suggests that data synthesized by significantly more capable models may diverge from the student's representational capacity, rendering introspective, self-generated comparative pairs more aligned for effective initialization.
4. A theoretical perspective on generalization measurement treats it as a harmonic balance between performance gains on seen and unseen task distributions, privileging out-of-distribution improvement as the critical indicator of initialization quality.
5. The alignment mismatch between imitation learning and reward-driven optimization indicates that initialization objectives should favor learning from relative comparative signals rather than exact-sequence replication to better prime the policy for exploratory refinement.
