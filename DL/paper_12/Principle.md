**Principle 1: Hardware-Aligned Energy Claim Validation and Communication Sparsity Quantification in Neuromorphic Training Algorithms**

**Definition:**  
For methods proposing energy-efficient training on neuromorphic hardware, reviewers must scrutinize whether efficiency claims are grounded in hardware-relevant metrics or rely solely on abstract proxies. The evaluation should assess if the authors distinguish between data-movement reduction, arithmetic operation counts, and actual on-chip energy consumption, particularly when physical hardware access is limited to simulation. In the context of spike-based predictive coding, communication sparsity is a necessary but insufficient proxy for total system energy, as it ignores interconnect topology, memory hierarchies, and neuron state update costs. Reviewers should verify whether claims are framed with appropriate precision—using terms like "data movement reduction" rather than uncalibrated "energy savings"—and whether the algorithm maps to concrete hardware instructions rather than remaining an abstract idealization. Furthermore, the analysis must account for whether baseline comparisons are fair, pitting the proposed sparse method against both standard dense predictive coding and surrogate-gradient backpropagation under matched architectural and experimental conditions. This principle ensures that hardware-aligned contributions are evaluated by standards that reflect real deployment constraints rather than optimistic extrapolations from simplified metrics.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Metric Rigor:** Are efficiency claims framed precisely (e.g., bits-per-neuron, communication sparsity) without overstating as calibrated energy (Joules) unless measured on physical hardware?
- **Simulator Fidelity:** Does the algorithm map to specific hardware instruction sets (e.g., Loihi 2 microcode) with validated simulator compatibility, or remain a high-level conceptual mapping?
- **Holistic Cost Accounting:** Does the analysis account for NoC hops, memory hierarchies, spike routing, and accumulator overflow, or ignore these dominant hardware costs?
- **Baseline Comparisons:** Are efficiency comparisons fair against both standard dense floating-point PC and surrogate-gradient SNN training under identical architectural constraints?


**Principle 2: Depth Scaling and Architectural Generalization in Predictive Coding-Based Spiking Networks**

**Definition:**  
When evaluating local learning rules like Predictive Coding adapted for spiking neural networks, it is critical to determine whether empirical limitations in network depth arise from the novel spike-based communication protocol or from intrinsic constraints of the underlying PC framework. Reviewers must assess whether authors clearly attribute scalability challenges and provide a credible, empirically grounded pathway toward deeper architectures such as convolutional networks or residual backbones. Because standard predictive coding historically struggles with depth due to ill-conditioned inference dynamics, a spike-native formulation that faithfully discretizes PC dynamics will naturally inherit these limitations; the burden lies on authors to demonstrate where their contribution ends and where orthogonal stabilization techniques must begin. A rigorous evaluation should therefore examine the fidelity of the spiking approximation as a function of depth, including whether layer-wise deviation between discrete and continuous states compounds in deeper networks. This principle demands that authors do not conflate the communication efficiency gains of their protocol with the fundamental algorithmic scalability of the learning rule, and that they outline specific future experiments—such as iso-error curve analysis—needed to validate deep-network deployment.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Attribution Clarity:** Does the work explicitly separate limitations of the spiking discretization (e.g., quantization error accumulation) from inherent PC inference instabilities (e.g., ill-conditioned dynamics in deep networks)?
- **Empirical Depth Progression:** Does the evaluation extend beyond shallow MLPs to convolutional and potentially deeper architectures, or justify why such scaling is deferred?
- **Orthogonality Argument:** Does the paper convincingly argue that emerging stabilization techniques are orthogonal to the communication protocol and transferable, and does it identify what a future scaling study must verify?
- **Ablation of Approximation Fidelity:** Is there analysis of how layer-wise deviation between spiking and continuous PC states evolves with depth, particularly regarding iso-error curves for spike-based communication windows?


**Principle 3: Benchmark Diversity and End-to-End Spiking Fidelity for Neuromorphic Deployment Claims**

**Definition:**  
Research proposing spike-native training frameworks must substantiate generality through rigorous benchmark selection that reflects both static and temporal processing demands. Reviewers should examine whether evaluations progress beyond trivial datasets to moderately complex benchmarks and, where neuromorphic deployment is claimed, whether spatiotemporal or event-driven datasets are included. Equally important is the end-to-end integrity of the system: reported accuracies must reflect purely spiking networks with local plasticity rather than hybrid pipelines that rely on frozen representations fed to external non-spiking classifiers. The experimental design must also feature appropriate baselines, including standard backpropagation, dense predictive coding, and state-of-the-art surrogate-gradient SNN training, all under matched architectural constraints. If event-driven computation is claimed, reviewers should expect explicit demonstrations of temporal sparsity and latency-accuracy trade-offs, ensuring that the method exploits the time domain rather than merely tolerating it. This principle guards against overfitting evaluation to simple benchmarks and ensures that efficiency claims generalize across the input complexities encountered in real-world neuromorphic applications.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Benchmark Complexity:** Does the experimental scope progress beyond trivial datasets (e.g., MNIST) to moderately complex static datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10) and, where appropriate, spatiotemporal or event-camera benchmarks?
- **End-to-End Integrity:** Are classification results produced by a purely spiking network with local plasticity, or do they rely on frozen representations fed to external ANN classifiers?
- **Baseline Appropriateness:** Are comparisons made against relevant state-of-the-art SNN training paradigms (ANN-to-SNN conversion, surrogate-gradient BPTT, other PC-SNN hybrids) using matched architectures?
- **Temporal Validation:** If event-driven computation is claimed, is there explicit demonstration of temporal sparsity (e.g., silent steady-states, spike bursts on input changes) and latency-accuracy trade-offs?


**Principle 4: Theoretical Distinctness and Novelty Attribution in Spike-Based Predictive Coding Formulations**

**Definition:**  
Given that spiking neural networks, predictive coding, and adaptive threshold mechanisms are individually well-studied, reviewers must evaluate whether the specific integration constitutes a novel methodological contribution or an incremental combination of existing components. Special attention should be paid to distinguishing the approach from superficially similar paradigms such as binary neural networks—which quantize weights rather than dynamic communication—and teacher-student distillation, which relies on static external targets rather than dynamic internal error variables. The work must precisely articulate the mechanistic differences from backpropagation, moving beyond vague assertions of "locality" to explain how bidirectional state relaxation and prospective configuration yield distinct weight-space trajectories compared to unidirectional gradient flow. Furthermore, the paper should provide formal or empirical evidence that the spiking formulation is a faithful discretization of standard predictive coding dynamics, with explicit bounds on quantization error or numerical convergence guarantees. This principle ensures that contributions are assessed on the rigor of their theoretical synthesis and their clear demarcation from prior art, rather than on the novelty of individual subcomponents in isolation.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Component Novelty vs. Integration:** Does the paper clearly credit prior art for individual components (ternary spikes, dynamic thresholds) while rigorously justifying the novel mathematical derivation that unifies them into a coherent local learning rule?
- **Distinction from Distillation:** Is the relationship to teacher-student distillation clarified, specifically regarding the dynamic internal nature of PC targets versus static external teachers?
- **Backpropagation Differentiation:** Does the work precisely articulate the mechanistic differences from backpropagation (e.g., bidirectional state relaxation vs. unidirectional gradient flow, prospective configuration vs. chain-rule weight updates) rather than relying on vague "locality" claims?
- **Equivalence and Convergence Guarantees:** Are there formal proofs or rigorous empirical demonstrations that the spiking formulation converges to standard PC dynamics, with explicit bounds on quantization error?


**Principle 5: Algorithmic Operational Completeness and Hyperparameter Transparency in Event-Driven Local Learning Rules**

**Definition:**  
For event-driven local learning algorithms intended for neuromorphic deployment, the completeness of pseudocode and operational details is paramount, as ambiguous specifications prevent reproducibility and hardware implementation. Reviewers must assess whether all computational pathways—including synaptic weight updates, initialization sequences, and threshold scheduling—are explicitly defined, rather than omitted or relegated to vague textual descriptions. The translation of ternary spikes into synaptic updates must be mechanistically clear, specifying how accumulated spike counts interact with fixed-precision arithmetic, overflow safeguards, and local memory constraints compatible with target hardware such as neuromorphic instruction sets. Beyond algorithmic completeness, hyperparameter sensitivity must be thoroughly characterized through systematic ablation studies and accompanied by actionable selection heuristics grounded in empirical data. This includes theoretical motivation for scheduling strategies—such as proofs of convergence for cyclic threshold decays—and practical rules-of-thumb that relate scheduler magnitude to activation clipping bounds and trade off cycle length against decay factors. This principle upholds standards of transparency and reproducibility, ensuring that local learning rules can be independently verified, implemented, and optimized by other researchers in the field.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Pathway Completeness:** Does the algorithm explicitly specify how ternary spikes translate into synaptic updates (e.g., accumulated spike counts, fixed-precision multiplication, overflow handling) compatible with target hardware?
- **Notational Clarity:** Are all symbols rigorously defined, ideally with a notation table, and are missing algorithm steps (e.g., weight updates omitted from pseudocode) corrected and clarified?
- **Scheduler Motivation:** Are threshold scheduling strategies theoretically motivated (e.g., convergence proofs) and empirically validated through ablation studies across cycle length, decay factors, and phase lengths?
- **Reproducibility Heuristics:** Does the work provide concrete rules-of-thumb for hyperparameter selection (e.g., initializing magnitude relative to activation clipping bounds, trading cycle length for decay factor) supported by grid-search data?
