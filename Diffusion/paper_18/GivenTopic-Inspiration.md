Given Topic: Conditional diffusion-based generative modeling has demonstrated remarkable capability in high-dimensional data synthesis. Current conditional sampling paradigms predominantly employ static guidance weights to modulate conditioning strength throughout the denoising trajectory. This creates a fundamental tension: intensifying guidance improves perceptual fidelity and semantic alignment, yet concurrently induces distributional drift away from the true conditional density. Existing post-hoc correction strategies are computationally prohibitive for large-scale applications. The core scientific challenge is to devise a principled mechanism that adaptively calibrates guidance strength as a function of the conditioning signal and temporal progression of the diffusion process, thereby reconciling sample quality with strict distributional fidelity. Additionally, extending such adaptive guidance to incorporate external reward criteria without compromising distributional integrity remains an unresolved problem.

5. The observation that approximation errors vary across the denoising timeline, suggesting that guidance strength should be temporally heterogeneous rather than temporally uniform.
6. The understanding that conditioning signals differ in semantic complexity, motivating conditioning-dependent guidance that adapts to individual inputs rather than applying universal constants.
7. The perspective that external reward functions induce a tilted target distribution, providing a principled way to bias generation toward high-reward regions while maintaining distributional constraints.
8. The empirical observation that excessive static guidance drives samples toward the boundaries of the data manifold, degrading overall distributional coverage.
