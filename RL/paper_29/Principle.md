**Principle 1: Theoretical Grounding and Approximation Validity of Information-Theoretic Pseudo-Label Selectors**

**Definition:**  
In label-free test-time reinforcement learning, the mechanism for aggregating noisy rollouts into a pseudo-label must not be treated as an arbitrary heuristic. High-quality research is expected to derive its selection criterion from a principled information-theoretic or statistical objective—such as an InfoMax or invariance principle—and to rigorously justify any closed-form approximation (e.g., second-order Taylor expansion, uniform priors) used to make it computationally tractable. The work must demonstrate that the chosen surrogate (e.g., harmonic mean) is not merely empirically convenient but is a valid optimization target, and it should generalize beyond a single setting (e.g., via tunable parameters, multiple views, or non-uniform priors) to avoid being a narrowly fitted trick.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Principled Derivation**: Is the pseudo-label selector derived from a formal objective (e.g., View-Invariant InfoMax) rather than from intuition alone? Are the assumptions (e.g., balanced confidence, non-degeneracy) explicitly stated in the main text?
- **Approximation Fidelity**: Does the work empirically validate that the closed-form surrogate (e.g., harmonic mean) matches or exceeds the performance of directly optimizing the original objective (e.g., full KL-based InfoMax)?
- **Generalization of Framework**: Does the theory extend to broader regimes such as tunable penalty weights, more than two views, or non-uniform view priors, proving the method is a general class rather than a fixed heuristic?
- **Discriminatory Power**: Does the work map its specific instantiation onto a well-known family (e.g., Generalized Means) to explain why its particular parameter choice is optimal for the target domain?

**Principle 2: Semantic Invariance Stress Testing and Robustness to View-Dependent Perturbations**

**Definition:**  
Multi-view consistency methods rely on the core assumption that reformulated questions are semantically equivalent to the original. Because paraphrases are generated by the model itself—or by auxiliary models of varying capability—they may introduce semantic drift, wording biases, or shallow restatements. High-quality research must therefore subject its view-generation pipeline to stress tests that deliberately degrade paraphrase quality, and it must prove that the consistency-based selector does not falsely down-weight valid answers when views are imperfect. External validation of semantic preservation and qualitative failure analysis are essential to establish that the second view is meaningful rather than noisy.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Sensitivity to View Quality**: Does the work include controlled experiments varying the reframer quality (e.g., model scale as a proxy for noise, from 0.6B to 14B) to show robustness under low-fidelity paraphrasing?
- **Semantic Preservation Validation**: Is there independent verification (e.g., oracle LLM judges, consistency checks) that reframed questions preserve the original problem’s logic and correct answer?
- **False Penalty Analysis**: Does the method maintain or improve performance on high-confidence correct subsets even when using noisy reframers, proving it does not discard valid solutions due to minor drift?
- **Diversity vs. Triviality**: Does the work distinguish between meaningful semantic reformulations and trivial restatements (e.g., via diversity rewards or JS divergence), ensuring views are sufficiently distinct to challenge the solver?

**Principle 3: Decomposition of Gains Across Confidence-Correctness Quadrants and Signal Calibration**

**Definition:**  
Aggregate accuracy metrics like Pass@1 are insufficient for evaluating pseudo-label selectors in TTRL because they mask critical failure modes, such as the model being confidently wrong. Reviewers expect a granular, diagnostic breakdown of where improvements originate. This requires stratifying results into confidence-correctness quadrants (confident-correct, confident-incorrect, not-confident-correct, not-confident-incorrect) and reporting signal-quality metrics like F1 score and Spearman correlation against ground truth. A strong contribution must demonstrate that it recovers correct answers in hard regimes—particularly the confident-incorrect quadrant where majority voting amplifies spurious solutions—without degrading performance on easy cases.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Quadrant-Wise Breakdown**: Does the work report pseudo-label accuracy, final model performance, and signal correlation for each confidence-correctness subset separately?
- **Hard Regime Superiority**: Does the method show substantial gains in the confident-incorrect and not-confident-correct quadrants, where baseline selectors typically fail or provide noisy signals?
- **Calibration Metrics**: Are rigorous statistical metrics (e.g., F1, Spearman/Somer’s D correlation) reported to prove the selector’s alignment with ground truth, rather than relying solely on pseudo-label accuracy?
- **No Trade-Offs**: Is there evidence that improvements on difficult subsets do not come at the cost of performance degradation on easy (confident-correct) subsets?

**Principle 4: Stability and Collapse Avoidance in Long-Horizon Self-Supervised Test-Time Training**

**Definition:**  
Test-time RL is notoriously vulnerable to training collapse: once a pseudo-label selector reinforces a systematic error, the policy can enter a positive feedback loop that drives entropy to zero and locks the model into a deterministic incorrect state. High-quality research must demonstrate stable convergence across all experimental settings, diagnose the collapse modes of baseline methods (e.g., majority voting causing reward hacking), and explain mechanistically how the proposed selector interrupts this feedback loop. Robustness to hyperparameters and training configurations is also a key indicator of a reliable method.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Empirical Stability**: Does the work demonstrate zero training failures (no collapse) across diverse benchmarks, model sizes, and datasets, with smooth learning curves?
- **Collapse Diagnosis**: Does it analyze when, where, and why baseline methods collapse (e.g., early training steps, specific model-dataset pairs) and relate this to pseudo-label quality?
- **Hyperparameter Robustness**: Is there a systematic sensitivity analysis (e.g., grid search over reward coefficients, batch sizes) showing that performance remains high and stable across a wide hyperparameter range?
- **Mechanistic Explanation**: Does the work explain how its selector prevents self-reinforcement of spurious answers (e.g., by penalizing view-dependent confidence) rather than merely reporting higher final accuracy?

**Principle 5: Inference-Only Utility and Strict Computational Budget Control in Test-Time Scaling**

**Definition:**  
A pseudo-label selection mechanism should be evaluated both as a generator of training signals and as a standalone inference-time aggregation strategy. Claims of superiority must be validated under strictly matched computational budgets to ensure gains arise from algorithmic efficacy rather than increased rollouts or auxiliary compute. The method should demonstrate consistent benefits across varying inference budgets (e.g., 32 to 256 rollouts) and model scales, and it should isolate the contribution of the selector itself from the broader self-play training loop to prove its independent utility.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Inference-Only Ablations**: Does the work evaluate the selector as a pure test-time aggregation method (without parameter updates), showing Pass@1 improvements over majority voting across multiple rollout budgets?
- **Budget Fairness**: Are comparisons conducted under matched computational budgets (e.g., total number of rollouts or FLOPs), ensuring multi-view methods are not given an unfair advantage over single-view baselines?
- **Cross-Scale Consistency**: Does the method demonstrate favorable scaling across model capacities (e.g., 1.7B to 8B) and families, ruling out size-specific or architecture-specific artifacts?
- **Component Isolation**: Is there an ablation that isolates the pseudo-label selector from the full training framework (e.g., comparing harmonic-mean-as-selector vs. harmonic-mean-as-reward) to verify that the core mechanism itself drives the gains?
