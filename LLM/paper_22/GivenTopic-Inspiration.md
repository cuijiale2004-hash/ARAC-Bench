Given Topic: The research field concerns inference-time reasoning strategies for large language models, specifically the dichotomy between discrete explicit reasoning and continuous latent reasoning. Mainstream explicit approaches are fundamentally constrained by discrete tokenization, which collapses the full predictive distribution into a single trajectory at each step, thereby discarding alternative hypotheses and restricting information bandwidth. Conversely, purely latent continuous approaches preserve richer distributional information but tend to diffuse probability mass across multiple implicit paths, hindering convergence toward a definitive solution and degrading accuracy. Additionally, uncontrolled iterative deliberation in both paradigms induces overthinking, incurring substantial token costs without commensurate accuracy improvements. The central scientific challenge is to develop a training-free paradigm that dynamically balances expansive exploration in continuous space with disciplined convergence in discrete space, while curtailing redundant computation to jointly enhance reasoning accuracy and token efficiency.

3. Paradigm-level deficiencies reveal that single-mode systems either prematurely collapse uncertainty through discrete commitments or fail to converge due to continuous diffusion.
4. Controlled termination principles indicate that bounding total deliberation cycles and permitting early finalization at natural intermediate consolidation points mitigates redundancy.
5. Transition alignment via semantic anchors during modality shifts preserves coherent reasoning patterns and facilitates smooth phase transitions between divergent and convergent cognition.
