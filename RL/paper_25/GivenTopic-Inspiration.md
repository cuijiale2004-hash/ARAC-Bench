Given Topic: In the domain of enhancing multi-step reasoning capabilities of language models, current mainstream training paradigms face significant bottlenecks when addressing complex problems. Outcome-based reinforcement learning approaches suffer from sparse reward signals and vanishing policy gradients when correct solutions are rarely sampled within practical rollout budgets, rendering difficult reasoning tasks intractable. Concurrently, imitation learning via demonstration-based fine-tuning enforces rigid, token-level alignment with expert trajectories, leading to overfitting on lengthy reasoning chains and poor generalization beyond training distributions, particularly for less capable models. The core scientific issue centers on developing a training framework that bridges the gap between these paradigms—providing dense, step-wise supervision to facilitate learning on challenging problems without succumbing to the instability of sparse rewards or the rigidity of full-sequence imitation.

Given Inspiration:
1. The deficiency of outcome-based reinforcement learning on complex tasks: when policy rollouts fail to produce any correct solutions, advantage estimates collapse and learning stalls, indicating a fundamental need for non-sparse, process-dense reward signals.
2. The rigidity of supervised fine-tuning on long-form demonstrations: token-level imitation enforces excessive fidelity to surface-form expert trajectories, limiting generalization and causing overfitting, especially for models with limited capacity.
3. The sequential decision-making perspective on reasoning: treating complex problem-solving as a discrete sequence of high-level logical actions rather than monolithic text generation, inspired by classical decomposition methods in control and planning.
4. The cognitive science notion of deliberation-before-action: separating internal reflective thought from external executable decisions allows an agent to develop flexible reasoning strategies while still conforming to expert behavioral strategy at the action level.
5. The apprenticeship learning insight that partial trajectory alignment is sufficient: students can learn sophisticated behaviors by matching expert decisions at intermediate stages without replicating the exact token sequence of the entire demonstration.
6. The educational psychology of step-wise skill acquisition: mastering complex reasoning is better supported by granular, step-level guidance and intermediate state restoration than by exposure to complete but opaque final solutions.
