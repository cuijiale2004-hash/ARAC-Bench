**Principle 1: Causal Disentanglement of Learned Exploration from In-Context Adaptation in Meta-RL Agent Training**

**Definition:**  
In Meta-RL frameworks for LLM agents that optimize cross-episode returns, reviewers must assess whether the reported gains are causally attributable to the emergence of exploratory behaviors in early episodes, as opposed to merely exploiting in-context adaptation from past trajectories or benefiting from extended context windows. The core challenge is distinguishing "learning to explore"—where the agent actively modifies its initial behavior to gather task-relevant information—from "learning to leverage feedback"—where the agent simply retries with memory of past failures. This requires evidence that early-episode policies are qualitatively or quantitatively optimized for information acquisition rather than immediate success, and that this information specifically enables superior later-episode performance. Without such disentanglement, claims of induced exploration remain confounded by the mere availability of inter-episode memory or additional inference-time compute. Reviewers should insist on experiments that isolate the contribution of the credit-assignment mechanism from the availability of historical context.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Ablation of First-Episode Origin:** Does the work include experiments where the first episode is generated by a random policy, a base model, or the trained agent, with subsequent episodes handled by the trained Meta-RL agent? If performance drops when the first episode is not generated by the trained agent, this supports the claim that the agent learned to explore.
- **Separation of Memory from Credit Assignment:** Are standard RL baselines evaluated with equivalent inter-episode memory (history and/or reflections) to isolate the marginal effect of the cross-episode objective?
- **Behavioral Evidence of Information Gathering:** Is there qualitative or quantitative evidence that early-episode actions are structurally different from later-episode actions (e.g., more risk-taking, broader state coverage) in a way that specifically benefits subsequent attempts?
- **Rejection of "Second Chance" Confounding:** Does the work explicitly rule out the possibility that gains come solely from having multiple attempts at the same task instance, independent of learned exploratory strategy?

**Principle 2: Empirical Quality and Evolutionary Dynamics of Learned Self-Reflection for Policy Adaptation**

**Definition:**  
When an LLM agent's inner-loop adaptation is implemented through self-generated textual reflection, the evaluation must rigorously examine whether the reflection mechanism is genuinely learned and improved through outer-loop optimization, rather than being a static byproduct of the base model's pre-trained capabilities. The quality, specificity, and actionability of reflections must be shown to evolve with training, and their causal impact on subsequent episode returns must be empirically isolated from general policy improvements. Reviewers should scrutinize qualitative examples for failure modes such as hallucinated strategies, overly generic summaries, or misleading feedback loops that could propagate errors across episodes. Furthermore, the work must demonstrate that any observed performance gains are not achievable by simply prepending raw trajectory histories without reflective synthesis. This principle ensures that reflection is treated as a trainable, functional component of the Meta-RL algorithm rather than a superficial prompt augmentation.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Reflection Content Analysis:** Does the work provide qualitative examples of reflections before and after training, categorizing them into informative (actionable, specific) versus generic (vague, high-level) types?
- **Ablation of Reflection vs. Raw History:** Are there controlled ablations comparing (i) reflection + history, (ii) reflection-only, and (iii) history-only, to prove that the learned synthesis of experience provides value beyond raw context?
- **Failure Mode Disclosure:** Does the work analyze cases where reflections are misleading, hallucinated, or ineffective, and discuss how the training objective mitigates or fails to mitigate these cases?
- **Causal Attribution:** Is there evidence that performance improvements are driven by the *quality* of the reflections themselves co-evolving with the policy, rather than by the policy improving independently while reflections remain static?

**Principle 3: Fair Inter-Episode Baseline Alignment and Control of Cross-Episode Information Access**

**Definition:**  
Fair evaluation of cross-episode Meta-RL demands that baseline single-episode RL methods be granted equivalent access to inter-episode memory buffers, including raw histories and any reflective summaries, to isolate the marginal contribution of the multi-episode objective. The experimental design must control for total environment interactions, gradient update batch sizes, and the length of context available to the agent at any decision point, preventing confounds from unequal test-time compute or training data budgets. Additionally, the sequential dependency inherent in generating episodes within a trial often introduces substantial wall-clock overhead; this efficiency trade-off must be quantified and discussed transparently. Reviewers should verify that performance improvements are not artifacts of longer effective context horizons or additional inference-time tokens, but rather stem from the principled credit assignment across episodes. Only through such controlled comparisons can claims of Meta-RL superiority be considered valid.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **RL Baseline with Equivalent Memory:** Are standard RL baselines (e.g., PPO, GRPO, GiGPO) evaluated both with and without inter-episode memory to isolate the effect of the Meta-RL objective from the effect of cross-episode context?
- **Matched Training Budgets:** Is the total number of trajectories, environment steps, and gradient updates carefully matched between RL and Meta-RL conditions?
- **Computational Overhead Transparency:** Is the sequential generation cost of dependent episodes quantified and reported (e.g., wall-clock time, throughput comparisons), and are potential mitigation strategies discussed?
- **Control for Context Length:** Does the experimental design rule out the possibility that Meta-RL gains are simply due to the agent having access to a longer effective context window at test time?

**Principle 4: Statistical Reproducibility and Architectural Generalization in Multi-Episode RL Training**

**Definition:**  
Given the high variance inherent in RL training and the sensitivity of LLM agents to initialization and hyperparameters, empirical claims in this domain require rigorous statistical validation across multiple independent random seeds and, where possible, across diverse base model architectures. Single-seed results or evaluations restricted to a single model family are generally insufficient to establish that Meta-RL reliably induces exploration rather than exploiting idiosyncratic training dynamics. The work should report means and standard deviations, perform appropriate significance testing, and explore sensitivity to critical hyperparameters such as the trajectory discount factor and episode count. Furthermore, generalization claims—to harder task variants or out-of-distribution environments—must be supported by consistent trends across seeds and models rather than isolated point estimates. This principle safeguards against overfitting the experimental setup and ensures that the findings represent robust, reproducible phenomena in LLM agent training.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Multi-Seed Reporting:** Are results reported with means and standard deviations across at least three independent random seeds, and are statistical significance annotations provided for comparisons?
- **Cross-Model Validation:** Is the method validated on more than one LLM architecture or model family to demonstrate that the Meta-RL framework is not overly tailored to a specific base model?
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Are key hyperparameters (e.g., trajectory discount factor γ_traj, number of episodes per trial, batch size) ablated and their impact on stability and final performance reported?
- **Generalization Robustness:** Do claims of generalization to harder or out-of-distribution tasks hold across multiple seeds and exhibit consistent effect sizes, rather than relying on single-run point estimates?

**Principle 5: Mechanistic Behavioral Validation of Environment-Specific Exploration Strategies**

**Definition:**  
Aggregate metrics such as trajectory entropy or pass@k improvements are insufficient to substantiate claims of learned exploration without accompanying mechanistic evidence of how the agent interacts with the environment to acquire useful information. Reviewers should expect qualitative and quantitative behavioral analyses—such as visualizations of action heatmaps, analysis of information-gathering moves in partially observable environments, or comparisons against base-model behaviors—that demonstrate environment-specific exploratory strategies. The suitability of the chosen task domains must also be scrutinized: environments where success is primarily achieved through repeated trial-and-error without requiring active information acquisition may not genuinely test the hypothesis of learned exploration. Furthermore, diversity metrics should be grounded in task-relevant state-action coverage rather than easily hackable distributional properties. This principle ensures that "exploration" is operationalized as a meaningful, observable behavioral pattern rather than an abstract statistical artifact.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Qualitative Trajectory Visualization:** Does the work provide visual or textual examples of trajectories (e.g., click maps in MineSweeper, action sequences in Sokoban) comparing base model, RL, and Meta-RL agents to reveal distinct behavioral patterns?
- **Task-Relevant Diversity Metrics:** Are diversity measures explicitly tied to meaningful environment interaction patterns (e.g., information-gathering actions in partially observable spaces) rather than relying solely on entropy over action strings, which can be artificially inflated?
- **Domain Suitability Assessment:** Does the work justify why the chosen environments genuinely require active exploration as opposed to deterministic re-trying, and does it acknowledge limitations where exploration claims may be weaker?
- **Information-Gain Quantification:** Is there evidence linking early-episode behaviors to measurable information gain or reduced uncertainty that directly facilitates success in subsequent episodes?
