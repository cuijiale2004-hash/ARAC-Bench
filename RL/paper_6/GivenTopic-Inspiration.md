Given Topic: Enhancing reasoning capabilities of large language models in code generation and broader logical reasoning tasks through reinforcement learning paradigms represents a critical research frontier. Current mainstream reinforcement learning methods predominantly optimize for direct solution generation using execution-based or rule-based rewards. While effective for improving task completion rates, these paradigms intrinsically lack mechanisms to incentivize introspection, reflective judgment, or critical evaluation of candidate solutions. Consequently, models trained under such frameworks may exhibit limited deliberative reasoning and struggle to internalize self-correction behaviors. The core scientific issue is how to integrate explicit critique-oriented learning signals into reinforcement learning frameworks to cultivate models' critical thinking and reflective reasoning abilities, thereby overcoming the limitations of purely solution-centric optimization and enhancing both task-specific performance and transferable general reasoning capabilities.

Given Inspiration:
1. Recent findings that explicitly cultivating evaluation capabilities in language models—teaching them to assess solution validity rather than solely generate answers—yields substantial gains in reasoning performance.
2. The structural deficiency of conventional reinforcement learning frameworks that optimize exclusively for terminal task success, thereby omitting explicit incentives for introspection, error detection, and reflective correction.
3. The cognitive science perspective that effective reasoning necessitates a dual process of proposition and critical appraisal, motivating the integration of judgment-oriented learning signals alongside generative objectives.
4. The recognition that binary, verifiable correctness judgments on problem-solution pairs constitute a distinct form of supervision that remains underutilized in end-to-end reinforcement learning regimes.
5. The observation that critique-oriented training in specialized domains can enhance transferable general reasoning, suggesting that evaluative competence constitutes a domain-agnostic reasoning primitive.
6. The limitation of existing critique methodologies that depend on static teacher-provided evaluation traces, inspiring the pursuit of an autonomous exploration mechanism where models learn to judge through self-directed interaction with verifiable outcomes.
