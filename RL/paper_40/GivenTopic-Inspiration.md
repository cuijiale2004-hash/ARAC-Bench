Given Topic: The research concerns end-to-end reinforcement learning for multi-turn tool-integrated reasoning. Current approaches encounter severe training instability when extending tool-augmented reasoning beyond single-turn interactions, primarily due to compounding distributional shifts introduced by external feedback loops. Prevailing stabilization strategies often rely on supervised cold-start initialization, which constrains models to predefined behavioral patterns and precludes the autonomous emergence of novel reasoning strategies. The central problem is to devise a training paradigm that maintains stable learning dynamics in long-horizon, feedback-driven interactions while preserving the exploratory benefits of training directly from pretrained base models without supervised fine-tuning.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that external tool feedback induces distributional drift, causing language models to generate extremely low-probability tokens that compound across successive interaction turns.
2. The theoretical insight that policy gradient norms contain terms inversely correlated with token probabilities, offering a mechanistic explanation for gradient explosions in multi-turn settings.
3. The identification of a pathological response pattern—turns that yield neither executable actions nor conclusive answers—as a reliable diagnostic indicator for trajectories undergoing distributional collapse.
4. The perspective that trajectories containing initially successful reasoning should be insulated from later turn failures, suggesting the need for mechanisms that isolate credit assignment across turns.
5. The motivation to preserve the exploratory capacity of unsupervised initialization by preventing premature convergence to single-turn solutions, thereby permitting the autonomous discovery of complex reasoning behaviors such as progressive verification and self-correction.
