Given Topic: Preference-based reinforcement learning enables agents to learn from human pairwise preferences instead of hand-crafted rewards, yet its practical deployment is severely hindered by the high cost of querying human feedback. In offline settings, existing methods suffer from poor query efficiency due to two fundamental limitations: exploration strategies that prioritize reward estimation in task-irrelevant regions rather than information critical to policy improvement, and the propensity to overoptimize imperfect learned rewards, particularly in out-of-distribution regions. The core scientific challenge lies in developing principled mechanisms to identify maximally informative queries within a fixed offline dataset while robustly preventing value overestimation induced by noisy reward learning, thereby achieving near-optimal policy performance with minimal human supervision.

Given Inspiration:
1. Policy-centric information gain: The insight that query efficiency is governed by reducing uncertainty about which policy is optimal, rather than achieving uniform accuracy over the entire reward landscape.
2. Pessimism under latent rewards: The recognition that conservative estimation principles from standard offline reinforcement learning require re-examination when rewards are inferred from sparse, noisy human comparisons.
3. Uncertainty-aware credit assignment: The perspective that disagreement among learned value estimates reveals where preference feedback is most ambiguous, guiding targeted information acquisition.
4. Diameter minimization of hypothesis sets: The mathematical view that the worst-case policy disagreement within a confidence set bounds learning complexity, motivating queries that maximally shrink this set.
5. Horizon as a robustness knob: The observation that effective planning horizons can modulate sensitivity to noise in learned objectives, suggesting dynamic horizon adjustment as a defense against overoptimization.
