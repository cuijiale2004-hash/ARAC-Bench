Given Topic: The research lies at the intersection of generative language modeling and efficient inference for large-scale language models. Current autoregressive decoding paradigms suffer from inherent sequential dependencies that severely constrain parallelization during generation, leading to substantial latency bottlenecks. Although diffusion-based language models offer a theoretical alternative for parallel token generation, they typically forfeit key autoregressive efficiencies—most notably sequential context caching—and often exhibit inferior latency, inflexible sequence lengths, and prohibitive adaptation costs when scaling to large pretrained models. Furthermore, prior attempts to reconcile autoregressive and diffusion principles remain largely confined to small-scale settings, leaving their viability for modern large language model deployment uncertain. The core scientific problem is therefore to identify a principled adaptation strategy that preserves the representational strengths of pretrained autoregressive models while unlocking substantial parallelization during decoding, without incurring excessive retraining overhead or sacrificing generation quality.

Given Inspiration:
1. The fundamental latency bottleneck inherent to strictly sequential, single-token generation motivates reconceptualizing the atomic unit of decoding to enable greater parallelization.
2. The tension between global autoregressive coherence and local diffusion-based refinement suggests that these paradigms can be unified by enforcing causal dependencies only at coarse time scales while permitting parallelism within local windows.
3. The substantial investment in pretrained autoregressive representations implies that transitioning to parallel generation should leverage existing model weights through data-efficient adaptation rather than training from scratch.
4. The computational redundancy inherent in iteratively refining partial outputs suggests that inference acceleration requires multi-granular context reuse, spanning both completed historical segments and actively generated local segments.
5. The empirical observation that fixed structural granularity during training constrains flexible inference indicates that decoding strategies should decouple training-time grouping from runtime parallelization granularity.
6. The effectiveness of discrete denoising objectives in modeling complex distributions suggests that local token prediction under partial observation can preserve global sequence quality when appropriately constrained.
