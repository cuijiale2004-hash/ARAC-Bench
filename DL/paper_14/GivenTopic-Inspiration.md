Given Topic: Robust causal generalization in deep learning under empirical risk minimization. Current data-driven models indiscriminately exploit spurious correlations, leading to brittle out-of-distribution performance. Prevailing causal learning paradigms typically rely on multi-environment training data, explicit invariance penalties, or specialized regularization, which imposes burdensome practical requirements. Existing theoretical understanding remains insufficient regarding whether standard training procedures alone can suppress non-causal effects without external supervision or architectural interventions. The core scientific problem is to identify the intrinsic data conditions and optimization-induced mechanisms under which deep learning models trained on observational data can autonomously prioritize causally relevant features over strongly correlated but non-causal descendants.

Given Inspiration:
1. The practical limitations of prevailing invariance learning and distributionally robust optimization frameworks, which require multi-environment partitions, explicit regularizers, or group annotations, motivating exploration of whether standard training dynamics alone can induce causal feature selection.
2. The statistical observation that causal feature-target relationships in pooled observational data often exhibit greater stability and effective correlation strength than spurious associations, which tend to destructively interfere or vary across latent subpopulations.
3. The theoretical understanding that gradient descent in overparameterized architectures exhibits implicit directional bias and margin-maximizing tendencies, suggesting that optimization geometry may naturally amplify dominant signals while suppressing competing correlates.
4. The empirical phenomenon that deep networks preferentially amplify the most predictive features during early learning stages, implying that overwhelming causal predictiveness could trigger autonomous suppression of weaker, non-causal alternatives throughout training.
5. The mathematical realization that classical linear estimators retain constant fractions of spurious components even under strong causal dominance, indicating that achieving cause-only prediction requires leveraging nonlinear architectural properties and non-equilibrium optimization trajectories rather than relying solely on data statistics.
