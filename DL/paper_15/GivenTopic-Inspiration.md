Current approaches predominantly rely on shallow architectures or localized plasticity rules, which inherently lack the capacity to model long-range temporal dependencies and maintain cross-timestep semantic stability. Furthermore, the intrinsic temporal dynamics of spiking neurons alone prove insufficient for complex video processing, while the absence of progressive temporal aggregation mechanisms—unlike conventional architectures—exacerbates feature instability across time. The core problem is to overcome the bottleneck of semantically unstable, temporally inconsistent representations in deep unsupervised spiking networks, enabling effective temporal modeling beyond intrinsic neuronal dynamics.]

2. The empirical observation that improved semantic extraction in converging models coincides with increased feature stability across timesteps, implying that temporally consistent representations are a hallmark of high-level understanding.
3. The architectural limitation that spiking neural networks retain full temporal resolution throughout the network without hierarchical temporal aggregation, unlike conventional video architectures that progressively downsample time, resulting in insufficient temporal abstraction.
4. The signal-processing insight that semantic content in video possesses long-range temporal correlations, whereas low-level nuisances decay rapidly, meaning that future-state prediction inherently privileges stable semantics over transient noise.
5. The paradigm deficiency that existing unsupervised methods for spiking networks are predominantly limited to shallow topologies or localized plasticity rules, lacking mechanisms to capture extended temporal dependencies in large-scale video data.
6. The experimental realization that explicitly forcing feature invariance across timesteps harms downstream performance, indicating that temporal consistency should be an emergent property of meaningful predictive objectives rather than a hard constraint.
