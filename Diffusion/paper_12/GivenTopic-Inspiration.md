Given Topic: The research addresses the reconciliation of visual perception and image synthesis within computer vision. Existing discriminative approaches for visual understanding predominantly optimize decision boundaries, which constrains their generalization to unseen distributions and neglects fine-grained structural details. In contrast, mainstream generative methods focus on data distribution modeling but suffer from deficient high-level semantic reasoning, limiting their efficacy in complex scene interpretation. The longstanding technological divergence between these paradigms further obstructs cross-paradigm knowledge integration. The fundamental scientific challenge is to establish a unified framework in which discriminative perception and generative synthesis mutually reinforce their respective capabilities without degrading individual performance.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical observation that generative learning processes encode distributional visual knowledge transferable to discriminative tasks, while discriminative semantic features conversely benefit generative fidelity, suggesting an intrinsic representational commonality between the two paradigms.
2. The statistical insight that sequential intermediate states of generative trajectories can be interpreted as samples approximating conditional data distributions, enabling the distillation of generative likelihood information to refine discriminative confidence calibration.
3. The semantic feedback hypothesis that high-level understanding derived from perception tasks can serve as dynamic conditioning signals to guide generative sampling trajectories toward semantically consistent outputs.
4. The multi-objective optimization perspective that simultaneous gradient updates for perception and generation may intrinsically conflict, necessitating a symmetric reconciliation mechanism that preserves non-interfering components while dampening antagonistic directions.
