Given Topic: The research concerns inference-time scaling for generative diffusion and flow-based models, specifically the adaptation of pre-trained models to new target distributions without costly retraining. Current guidance-based techniques, while computationally efficient, introduce uncontrolled bias that undermines sampling accuracy, particularly in scientific applications demanding high fidelity. Conversely, principled particle-based correction methods, though statistically grounded, suffer from exponential weight degeneracy and escalating computational costs, leading to ensemble collapse during sequential simulation. The field therefore confronts a fundamental bottleneck in achieving scalable, training-free inference-time adaptation that simultaneously preserves statistical correctness, computational tractability, and dynamic stability.

2. Existing particle ensemble methods exhibit a characteristic instability wherein importance weights concentrate exponentially onto a vanishing subset of samples, fundamentally eroding statistical power throughout sequential evolution.
3. Current adaptation paradigms impose an unfavorable dichotomy between lightweight but biased heuristic guidance and asymptotically correct but unstable particle reweighting, indicating a missing middle ground for principled control.
4. Stochastic control theory and variance reduction principles suggest that proactive trajectory-level regulation can absorb sources of ensemble variability preemptively, rather than merely compensating for their effects through retrospective statistical correction.
