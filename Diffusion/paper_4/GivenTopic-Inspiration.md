Given Topic: Reinforcement learning has emerged as a critical post-training paradigm for enhancing reasoning in language models, yet its principled extension to non-autoregressive sequence generation remains largely unresolved. Conventional algorithms presuppose a left-to-right, token-level factorization of sequence likelihood, enabling per-token importance sampling and localized policy gradients. However, for generative processes that produce entire sequences through iterative, global refinement rather than sequential next-token prediction, such factorization is either ill-defined or computationally prohibitive. Existing remedies rely on heuristic token-level surrogates that introduce theoretical inconsistencies and optimization instability. The central scientific problem is therefore to formulate a reinforcement learning objective that respects the holistic, sequence-level nature of non-autoregressive generation while maintaining computational tractability for large-scale model training.

Given Inspiration:
1. The prevailing reinforcement learning frameworks for language generation are structurally tethered to autoregressive, token-wise action spaces; applying them to non-autoregressive, iterative refinement constitutes a fundamental category error that calls for reorienting the optimization paradigm around the model's holistic generation mechanics rather than constructing incompatible token-level proxies.
2. Variational inference principles offer mathematically rigorous lower bounds for intractable exact likelihoods in complex generative models, suggesting that principled sequence-level probability estimation can circumvent the need for autoregressive conditional decomposition.
3. From a credit assignment standpoint, distributing a sequence-level reward signal across individual tokens via local importance ratios is conceptually mismatched; a globally coherent action space is needed to align policy gradient structure with the unitary nature of sequence evaluation.
4. The linear growth of cumulative log-likelihood disparities with sequence length implies that raw, unnormalized sequence ratios will exhibit exponential variance; this motivates the use of length-normalized surrogates to stabilize importance weights.
5. Statistical theory indicates that divergence estimators based on exponential transforms of log-likelihood differences suffer from severe variance and biased gradients in long-sequence regimes, whereas quadratic-form estimators provide a more robust theoretical foundation for policy regularization.
