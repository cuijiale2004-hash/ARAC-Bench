Given Topic: In the domain of large-scale multi-modal generation, prevailing continuous generative paradigms impose prohibitive inference costs due to their reliance on extensive sequential sampling steps. Existing few-step acceleration methods present an unsatisfactory trade-off: distillation-based approaches typically require frozen teacher networks or iterative refinement procedures and exhibit severe quality degradation at extremely low step counts, whereas adversarial variants introduce auxiliary discriminators and score-estimation modules that amplify memory overhead, training instability, and architectural complexity. Consequently, scaling efficient one-step generation to billion-parameter models remains largely intractable. The fundamental problem is to establish a streamlined, self-contained training framework capable of realizing high-fidelity one-step synthesis on large models without dependence on external auxiliary networks or frozen teachers, while maintaining output diversity and training stability.

Given Inspiration:
1. The duality of generative trajectories: conceptualizing a mirrored, self-referential path that pairs the standard noise-to-data evolution with a counterpart originating from the model's own synthesized distribution.
2. Self-adversarial signaling: generating an internal discrepancy measure by contrasting real and self-generated distributions without employing external discriminators or adversarial networks.
3. Velocity field alignment: reformulating distribution matching as the minimization of differences between instantaneous velocity predictions along paired trajectories to straighten the generative flow.
4. Score-velocity equivalence: exploiting the analytical correspondence between score functions and flow velocities under linear transport to translate divergence-based objectives into tractable regression targets.
5. Unified any-step capability: designing a single training objective that simultaneously preserves multi-step reconstruction fidelity and optimizes for few-step inference, avoiding separate student-teacher distillation pipelines.
