Given Topic: Conditional semantic textual similarity aims to measure semantic proximity between text pairs under explicit natural language conditions, addressing the ambiguity inherent in traditional semantic similarity tasks. Current mainstream approaches predominantly rely on discriminative paradigms and struggle to leverage advances in generative language modeling and reinforcement learning. Although reinforcement learning is theoretically appealing for directly optimizing non-differentiable ranking metrics and guiding conditional reasoning, naively applying holistic ranking rewards leads to optimization collapse and ineffective credit assignment due to overly complex and coarse-grained signals. The core scientific issue lies in developing a stable optimization framework that enables generative models to acquire fine-grained conditional scoring and ranking semantics without succumbing to unstable reward propagation.

Given Inspiration:
1. Curriculum learning in reward complexity: foundational scoring capabilities should be established through simple absolute judgment signals before introducing nuanced relative ordering objectives.
2. Localized credit assignment for ranking: global batch-level ranking signals are too coarse for precise optimization; decomposing comparisons into independent localized subsets enables differentiated learning signals for individual predictions.
3. Hybrid reward composition: stable policy optimization requires anchoring relative ranking objectives with absolute accuracy signals to mitigate collapse from sparse or complex ranking supervision.
4. Direct metric alignment: training objectives should directly correlate with non-differentiable ranking evaluation criteria rather than relying on differentiable surrogate losses that only approximate ranking quality.
