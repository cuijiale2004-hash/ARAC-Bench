Given Topic: Transformer-based large language models face escalating memory bottlenecks during autoregressive inference, particularly as sequence lengths increase, primarily due to the linear growth of key-value cache storage. While cross-layer cache sharing strategies offer promising memory reduction potential by reusing intermediate representations across depths, they consistently exhibit inferior modeling fidelity compared to within-layer compression techniques. The fundamental challenge lies in understanding the heterogeneous information distribution across network layers for keys and values, and developing principled reconstruction mechanisms that preserve representational capacity while substantially reducing cache memory footprint. This research direction seeks to reconcile the tension between inference efficiency and model performance in long-context scenarios by investigating asymmetric information flow patterns and designing compatible fusion paradigms that maintain positional encoding properties without computational recomputation.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical observation that keys and values in upper layers exhibit distinct information provenance patterns, suggesting that unified cross-layer sharing paradigms may be fundamentally suboptimal.
2. The mathematical insight that positional encoding compatibility can be preserved in reconstructed representations through structural symmetry constraints on learnable transformations, eliminating the need for explicit recomputation.
3. The training dynamics perspective that facilitating stronger gradient signals to shallow foundational layers enhances the learning of basis representations critical for hierarchical feature extraction.
4. The systems-level consideration that memory bandwidth limitations necessitate explicit balancing between representational expressiveness and input-output overhead in cache reconstruction mechanisms.
5. The hypothesis that feature-selective aggregation of multi-layer caches provides superior representational fidelity compared to direct cache replication for maintaining distinct functional roles across depths.
