Given Topic: Image captioning is a fundamental task bridging visual and linguistic domains, playing a critical role in multimodal pre-training. Existing methods predominantly rely on supervised fine-tuning using costly, non-scalable annotations from humans or proprietary systems. This paradigm inherently encourages models to memorize fixed ground-truth responses, thereby limiting generality and suppressing the diversity of valid descriptions in open-ended scenarios. Furthermore, applying reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards to subjective generation tasks remains challenging because conventional reward signals—whether reference-based metrics or discriminative evaluator models—are susceptible to bias exploitation and struggle to reliably assess complex, free-form captions. The central scientific issue is to develop a scalable training framework that overcomes memorization limitations and establishes an objective, robust reward mechanism for dense and accurate image captioning.

Given Inspiration:
1. A caption’s functional utility—its capacity to enable a text-only reasoning agent to correctly answer questions about the corresponding image—can serve as an objective, intrinsic proxy for caption quality without requiring stylistic reference alignment.
2. Supervised fine-tuning in open-ended visual description creates a memorization bottleneck by compelling the model to replicate singular annotated outputs rather than acquire underlying conceptual mappings between vision and language.
3. Paradigms that employ verifiable rewards in structured domains demonstrate that shifting away from imitation-based training can foster more general and diverse solution spaces, suggesting similar potential for subjective generative tasks.
4. End-to-end discriminative evaluation of free-form descriptive text is fundamentally vulnerable to reward hacking due to evaluator biases, motivating a decoupled principle that separates content generation from independent content verification.
5. The inherent multiplicity of valid descriptions for any single image implies that reward signals should prioritize informational completeness and factual adequacy over lexical conformity to reference captions.
