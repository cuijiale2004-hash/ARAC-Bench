Given Topic:
Machine unlearning for large language models seeks to eliminate undesired knowledge while preserving general capabilities, yet existing approaches suffer from critical deficiencies. Current methods frequently induce catastrophic forgetting, wherein aggressive optimization on targeted data severely degrades model utility. Furthermore, these approaches exhibit pronounced metric imbalance, prioritizing unlearning efficacy, utility retention, or privacy protection in isolation rather than achieving harmonious trade-offs. A more fundamental limitation lies in their adversarial fragility: subtle perturbations in latent representations or parameter configurations enable attackers to recover forgotten information through fine-tuning or circumvent safety constraints via crafted prompts. Consequently, the central scientific challenge is to establish a robust unlearning paradigm that simultaneously balances competing objectives—forgetting strength, downstream utility, and privacy—while maintaining resilience against both relearning and jailbreak attacks without precipitating model collapse.

2. Sharpness-aware optimization principles indicate that penalizing local loss surface sensitivity around parameter configurations can expand the minimal parameter change required for successful knowledge recovery.
3. Adversarial training frameworks demonstrate that explicitly optimizing against worst-case perturbations within bounded neighborhoods promotes local smoothness and enhances robustness.
4. Intermediate layer representations encode distinguishable behavioral traces, motivating the use of robust discriminative boundaries to guide the forgetting process without explicit output-level penalties.
5. The inherent conflict between retaining general knowledge and forgetting specific information manifests as competing gradient directions, suggesting that decoupling these opposing optimization forces can mitigate catastrophic collapse.
