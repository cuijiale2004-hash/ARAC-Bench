Given Topic: Deep learning theory and representation learning. Despite the remarkable empirical success of deep neural networks across diverse domains, a fundamental theoretical understanding of how these models process and preserve underlying data structures remains elusive. Current mainstream methods achieve high predictive performance, yet the mechanisms by which high-dimensional nonlinear transformations maintain or destroy the intrinsic geometric organization of data—particularly clustering and subspace structures—are poorly understood. The field lacks rigorous guarantees regarding whether and under what conditions latent representations retain the structural properties of the input data. This gap obscures model interpretability, complicates the design of theoretically grounded architectures, and hinders understanding of why certain data modalities inherently benefit from deep hierarchical processing. The core problem is to formally characterize the conditions under which network layers preserve subspace clustering structure without requiring explicit regularization or architectural constraints.

3. Spectral perturbation theory, which provides tools to analyze how linear and nonlinear transformations affect the stability of principal subspaces and their associated projectors.
