Given Topic: Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art generation quality but suffer from high inference costs due to the large number of function evaluations required for iterative denoising. While probability-flow ordinary differential equation formulations enable accelerated sampling via classical numerical solvers, existing approaches face a fundamental dilemma: the discretization behavior varies significantly across different prediction types and integration domains, yet no principled mechanism exists to reconcile these discrepancies in the low-step regime. Furthermore, learned acceleration methods typically demand expensive teacher trajectories or final samples generated at high function evaluation counts, and their parameters are often tightly coupled to specific backbone architectures. The core problem is how to develop a generalized sampling framework that adaptively bridges heterogeneous prediction paradigms and integration strategies without reliance on costly teacher-based supervision, thereby enabling high-fidelity generation under extreme evaluation budgets.

1. The discrete-time inconsistency among noise, velocity, and data prediction paradigms in diffusion models, which are mathematically equivalent in continuous time yet produce divergent numerical updates under discretization.

5. The distinct numerical stability characteristics associated with linear and logarithmic integration domains, which imply that domain selection critically influences approximation error in accelerated sampling.
6. The inherent algebraic coupling among noise, data, and velocity representations in diffusion dynamics, suggesting that isolated treatment of a single prediction target may limit approximation flexibility.
