Given Topic: Research on multimodal large language models for fine-grained visual perception and understanding encompasses detection, segmentation, and visual grounding. Current mainstream approaches predominantly rely on serializing structured visual targets, such as object locations, into textual coordinate strings. This paradigm suffers from inherent format inconsistency, numerical discontinuity upon tokenization, and fundamental semantic misalignment between linguistic symbols and visual content. Consequently, these methods exhibit limited efficacy on dense prediction tasks and vulnerability to hallucination in spatial localization. The central scientific challenge lies in enabling unified generation of both textual and structured visual outputs within multimodal language models, while establishing precise semantic alignment and spatial correspondence between linguistic reasoning and visual perception.

1. The fundamental incompatibility between continuous spatial information and discrete textual tokenization, which fractures numerical semantics and representation coherence in multimodal systems.
2. The dual semantic-spatial encoding property of image patch embeddings, suggesting their potential as referential primitives that naturally couple high-level concepts with pixel-level locations.
3. The ambiguity and spatial indeterminacy inherent in dataset-level visual vocabularies, motivating the need for query-specific visual references that preserve unique object correspondence.
4. The paradigm of unified multimodal autoregressive generation, wherein heterogeneous outputs should coexist within a single sequential prediction framework rather than being isolated in separate modalities.
5. The inadequacy of coarse geometric descriptors for dense visual tasks, necessitating fine-grained, patch-level grounding mechanisms to bridge language understanding and pixel-precise perception.
