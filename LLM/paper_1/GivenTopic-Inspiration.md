Given Topic: The research concerns optimization schedule design in large language model pre-training, where conventional practice prioritizes the minimization of intermediate pre-training loss through decay-based strategies. Recent empirical evidence suggests, however, that strong performance at this intermediate stage does not reliably predict downstream efficacy after supervised fine-tuning, revealing a potential misalignment between stage-wise optimization criteria and end-to-end deployment objectives. The core problem is to identify pre-training configurations that preserve downstream adaptability—particularly under modern multi-stage and over-training paradigms—rather than exclusively pursuing minimal pre-training loss, and to elucidate the geometric properties governing this transfer behavior.

Given Inspiration:
1. The prevailing practice of employing decay-based optimization schedules during large-scale pre-training to minimize intermediate-stage perplexity, and the growing suspicion that this criterion is decoupled from downstream supervised fine-tuning efficacy.
2. Empirical findings in transfer learning that correlate the geometric flatness of converged loss basins with improved adaptability to downstream tasks under distribution shift.
3. Evidence from staged training pipelines demonstrating that the checkpoint with minimal pre-training loss does not necessarily yield maximal performance after task-specific fine-tuning.
4. Optimization-theoretic perspectives on how terminal-phase convergence dynamics shape the local curvature of the loss landscape, thereby governing a model's plasticity for subsequent adaptation.
5. Practical bottlenecks in modern training workflows—including mid-training continuation and over-training regimes—where rigid decay schedules necessitate heuristic learning rate restarts and impede flexible pipeline design.
6. The fundamental tension between greedy optimization of an intermediate objective (pre-training loss) and the global objective of maximizing end-to-end performance across the entire training and deployment pipeline.
