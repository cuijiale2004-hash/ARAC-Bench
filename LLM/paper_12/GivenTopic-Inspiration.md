Given Topic: Research in large reasoning models has increasingly focused on allocating test-time computation to improve complex problem-solving capabilities. Mainstream approaches typically rely on either scaling parallel reasoning paths or injecting task-agnostic auxiliary tokens to stimulate extended thinking. However, these methods often overlook an emergent phenomenon prevalent in reasoning traces: the spontaneous repetition of the problem statement at the onset of the reasoning process. Existing paradigms tend to treat such repetition as a superficial training artifact or inefficient verbosity, lacking a principled understanding of its functional role. Consequently, there is a critical gap in determining whether this initial restatement constitutes redundant computation or serves as an intrinsic cognitive mechanism for grounding subsequent reasoning. The core scientific issue lies in formally characterizing the probabilistic and mechanistic implications of this behavior, and understanding how to harness it—beyond heuristic prompting—to enhance reasoning fidelity and focus without arbitrary computational overhead.

Given Inspiration:
1. The emergent phenomenon wherein reasoning models spontaneously initiate their internal chains by restating the problem statement, suggesting an intrinsic computational tendency rather than random redundancy.
2. The observation that explicit external instructions to re-read inputs can improve performance, which raises the question of whether spontaneous repetition serves an analogous, self-directed grounding function.
3. The tension between viewing uncontrolled repetition as a failure mode and the potential for such repetition to act as a learned, task-specific cognitive strategy for maintaining reasoning fidelity.
4. The hypothesis that initial repetition may function as an attentional refocusing mechanism, serving to anchor subsequent computation to salient problem details and mitigate drift during extended inference.
5. The mathematical intuition that suppressing a naturally occurring generation pattern imposes a measurable probabilistic cost, suggesting that the pattern is not arbitrary but deeply coupled with the model's reasoning distribution.
6. Insights from mechanistic analysis suggesting that mid-layer representational spaces govern reasoning aggregation, implying that the locus of effective cognitive grounding may reside in intermediate computational stages rather than superficial input reprocessing.
