Given Topic: Post-training refinement of large language models typically employs supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations followed by reinforcement learning from self-generated trajectories. However, the sequential application of these paradigms often induces policy instability: static imitation of off-policy expert data can abruptly disrupt established response patterns and cause overfitting, while isolated on-policy exploration frequently suffers from inefficient search and local convergence. The core problem is how to unify off-policy knowledge transfer and on-policy self-exploration into a single, dynamically balanced optimization framework that preserves model stability and selectively absorbs external expertise without brittle stage-wise transitions.

Given Inspiration:
1. Empirical analyses of existing paradigms reveal that off-policy expert imitation follows a progression from initial policy disruption through readaptation to eventual overfitting, indicating that expert influence requires temporal modulation rather than static application.
2. Viewing supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning through a unified off-policy versus on-policy lens suggests integrating imitation not as a separate pre-stage but as an auxiliary objective within continuous on-policy optimization.
3. The distributional divergence between expert demonstrations and the learner's established policy determines whether imitation yields beneficial guidance or destructive pattern interference, necessitating adaptive influence control.
4. Conventional importance sampling for off-policy data integration tends to collapse policy entropy by excessively reinforcing high-confidence tokens, exposing the need for balanced weighting that sustains exploratory diversity.
5. Information-theoretic uncertainty measures indicate that tokens at intermediate probability values constitute a natural learning sweet spot where off-policy updates are maximally informative yet minimally destabilizing.
6. Observations that stylistic and structural changes from expert data vary by task domain suggest that integration mechanisms must selectively absorb reasoning patterns rather than enforce uniform imitation.
7. The inadequacy of binary or fixed-coefficient mixing schedules in resolving distributional conflicts motivates smooth, adaptive transitions that gradually shift the optimization focus from external supervision to autonomous exploration.
