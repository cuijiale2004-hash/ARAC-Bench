Given Topic: Diffusion models have become the dominant paradigm in image generation, yet they remain fundamentally limited in interpreting and executing continuous numeric instructions for adjusting semantic attributes. Existing systems primarily rely on discrete textual conditioning, which is inherently ill-suited to represent gradable aesthetic concepts—such as brightness, realism, or detail—as continuous spectra. Moreover, prevailing preference alignment strategies typically pursue a global, undifferentiated aesthetic optimum, thereby neglecting the compositional and context-dependent nature of human aesthetic judgment. Consequently, current methods lack mechanisms to disentangle individual aesthetic factors and modulate their intensities with precision. The core scientific problem addressed is how to enable generative models to decompose aesthetic attributes into continuous, quantifiable dimensions and afford users fine-grained, independent control over attribute intensity while preserving semantic fidelity and generation quality.
