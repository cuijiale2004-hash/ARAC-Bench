Given Topic: Universal multimodal embedding seeks to produce task-specific representations that satisfy complex user instructions. Contemporary approaches predominantly employ multimodal foundation models as passive encoders, disregarding their inherent generative and reasoning capacities. As instructions increase in complexity—demanding compositional reasoning, fine-grained grounding, and nuanced contextual interpretation—this encoder-only paradigm struggles to yield sufficiently discriminative embeddings. The central scientific challenge concerns how to harness the generative potential of these models to explicitly interpret multimodal instructions prior to representation extraction, thereby mitigating instruction ambiguity and retrieval asymmetry while maintaining computational practicality.

Given Inspiration:
1. Chain-of-thought reasoning in generative models: The established effectiveness of step-by-step intermediate reasoning in improving complex problem-solving accuracy suggests that similar explicit cognitive processes may enhance the quality of learned representations.
2. Encoder-centric paradigm limitation: Prevailing methods restrict multimodal foundation models to passive encoding functions, failing to exploit their inherent generative and reasoning capabilities developed during pretraining.
3. Query-target semantic gap: Text retrieval research demonstrates that raw queries often inadequately capture user intent, motivating the need for interpretive expansion to bridge semantic distance between complex instructions and target content.
4. Autoregressive state conditioning: The property that hidden representations in generative models are contextually dependent on previously generated tokens implies a natural mechanism by which reasoning content can shape subsequent embedding geometry.
5. Cognitive stage separation: The architectural principle that high-level interpretive reasoning and low-level feature embedding can be decomposed into distinct operational stages, enabling modular optimization of each component.
