Given Topic: The research addresses exploration inefficiencies in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for large language model reasoning. Mainstream methods suffer from premature convergence, entropy collapse, and poorly calibrated policies that maintain high confidence irrespective of correctness. Conventional exploration strategies, including count-based and prediction-based approaches, rely on effective trajectory embeddings or auxiliary modules, which are fundamentally limited in representing complex reasoning paths and often lead to collapsed state representations. The central challenge is developing a scalable exploration mechanism that avoids explicit visitation counting and external representation learning, while simultaneously ensuring that the model's confidence remains properly calibrated against its actual accuracy throughout optimization.

Given Inspiration:
1. Developmental psychology suggests that learning is driven by intrinsic curiosity toward novel situations rather than by external summaries of experience; agents should analogously explore unfamiliar reasoning patterns while confidently exploiting familiar ones.
2. An actor's self-generated uncertainty regarding its own outputs provides a natural curiosity signal, wherein responses that appear surprising under the current policy indicate underexplored regions of the distribution.
3. A critic's epistemic uncertainty, reflected in the dispersion of its value estimates, encodes the degree of state-action coverage, with higher variance signaling regions warranting further exploration.
4. Theoretical foundations in sequential decision-making establish that predictive uncertainty can serve as an implicit surrogate for visitation-based novelty bonuses, grounding exploration in principled uncertainty quantification.
5. Calibration principles imply that overconfident erroneous behavior should be suppressed, whereas successful but uncertain behavior should be reinforced, aligning the agent's exploratory drive with its confidence landscape.
