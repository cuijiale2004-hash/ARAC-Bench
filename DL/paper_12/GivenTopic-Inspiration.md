Given Topic: Research in neuromorphic computing and spiking neural network training seeks to reconcile biological plausibility with hardware efficiency. Current mainstream approaches either rely on global error backpropagation with dense continuous-valued communication, which is incompatible with event-driven neuromorphic architectures, or adopt local learning rules that underperform on complex discriminative tasks. While predictive coding offers a biologically grounded alternative with local synaptic updates, its standard iterative inference necessitates dense floating-point message passing between layers, creating substantial communication overhead that negates the energy advantages of spiking hardware. Furthermore, existing attempts to integrate predictive coding with spiking networks often depend on hybrid non-spiking components, predefined runtime scaling, or retain dense floating-point operations during training. The core scientific challenge is to develop a training paradigm that preserves the locality and iterative error-correction principles of predictive coding while enabling truly sparse, event-driven communication compatible with neuromorphic hardware constraints, without compromising task accuracy.

Given Inspiration:
1. Biological action potentials: Biological neurons communicate via sparse, all-or-none electrical impulses and perform robust computation with low-precision, noisy signals, suggesting that artificial learning systems need not rely on dense continuous-valued gradients.
2. Predictive coding theory: Neuroscientific evidence that cortical circuits function as prediction machines, minimizing free energy through local error correction rather than global backpropagation of loss derivatives.
3. Neuromorphic hardware constraints: Brain-inspired silicon architectures achieve efficiency through event-driven, co-located memory and computation, but dense communication during training creates a data-movement bottleneck that dominates energy consumption.
4. Event-driven processing: Efficient natural and engineered systems remain inactive during steady states and respond selectively to salient changes, motivating learning protocols where synaptic and state updates occur only upon significant deviations.
5. Locality of synaptic plasticity: Biological learning rules depend predominantly on pre- and post-synaptic activity, indicating that scalable on-chip training should avoid non-local error transport across multiple layers.
6. Deficiencies in existing SNN training paradigms: Current high-accuracy spiking network training either relies on offline conversion or surrogate gradients with dense backward signals, highlighting a need for fully spiking, online local learning.
7. Threshold-based integration: Biological membranes accumulate inputs until crossing firing thresholds, suggesting that incremental corrections in artificial neuronal states can be communicated as discrete sparse events rather than dense vectors.
