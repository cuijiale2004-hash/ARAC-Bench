**1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points**

The ambition of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is to endow machines with human-like abilities to perceive, interpret, and reason about the dense visual world. To date, renowned state-of-the-art models have made remarkable strides, excelling in generating holistic descriptions and answering general questions about an entire image. However, this global-level perception struggles with the dense understanding of cluttered environments, intricate object details, and the complex interplay between multiple entities. 

To address the limitation of global perception, several previous works argue for a paradigm shift to region-level MLLMs. Specifically, they equip MLLMs with promptable and fine-grained interactions to achieve targeted region-level understanding, using boxes or masks. This mechanism transforms the model from a passive observer of the entire scene into an active participant capable of deep, localized analysis. However, effectively balancing global scene context with fine-grained local details remains challenging, which serves as a fundamental trade-off in region-level MLLMs. Conventional methods that employ pooled local features suffer from insufficient details, while recent models mainly focus on the ability to generate a descriptive caption for a single region, and thus model architectures are generally optimized to understand a given region in isolation. This design often neglects crucial global context, e.g., misidentifying a frog-shaped slipper as a real frog because the surrounding bedroom context is ignored. Existing methods struggle to master both; methods like DAM excel at local perception but lack a holistic view of the global context, while earlier works incorporating the global image tend to lose crucial local details by pooling region features into single vectors.

**2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions**

The core research motivation is to introduce Grasp Any Region (GAR) for comprehensive and detailed region understanding that simultaneously preserves and leverages the global context. Empowered by an effective technique, GAR aims to support precise perception by leveraging necessary global contexts, model interactions between multiple prompts, and naturally achieve advanced compositional reasoning to answer specific free-form questions about any region, shifting the paradigm from passive description to active dialogue.

The scientific questions addressed are: 
1) How to effectively balance global scene context with fine-grained local details in region-level MLLMs without suffering from contextual blindness or insufficient details? 
2) How to move beyond the prevailing single-prompt paradigm to model relationships between an arbitrary number of visual prompts rather than treating every region of interest as an isolated entity? 
3) How to enable advanced compositional reasoning capabilities that require synthesizing information from local prompts, global context, and the relationships between multiple regions?

**3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy**

The overall core idea is to propose Grasp Any Region (GAR), a family of MLLMs for comprehensive region-level visual understanding. GAR is designed specifically to solve the dilemma of balancing global context and local details. Empowered by an effective RoI-aligned feature replay technique, GAR supports (1) precise perception by leveraging necessary global contexts, and (2) modeling interactions between multiple prompts. Together, it then naturally achieves (3) advanced compositional reasoning to answer specific free-form questions about any region.

The design philosophy is guided by a central principle: to achieve a fine-grained understanding of the prompted region while simultaneously preserving and leveraging the global context of the entire scene. The task definition requires overcoming the contextual blindness inherent in models that analyze prompted regions in isolation. This myopic focus can lead to fundamental reasoning errors, such as misidentifying a frog-shaped slipper as a real frog because the surrounding bedroom context is ignored. Therefore, the architectural design must ensure that effectively encoding global contexts becomes equally crucial as local detailed features.

**4. Core Innovation Points (List all innovations in the original text completely)**

1. **RoI-aligned Feature Replay Technique**: A novel technique that processes the full, uncropped image (together with the mask prompt) with AnyRes, producing a global feature map that is rich in contextual information. Subsequently, RoI-Align is employed to gather relevant features directly from the global feature map. Because the features are extracted from a feature map that was computed over the entire image, they are inherently context-aware, providing sufficient local details while maintaining global information simultaneously. This replay of context-rich features allows GAR to simultaneously “zoom in” on detail without “losing sight” of the bigger picture.

2. **Interactions between Multiple Prompts**: GAR moves beyond the prevailing single-prompt paradigm, which treats every region of interest as an isolated entity. It manages to model relationships between an arbitrary number of prompts, allowing the model to perceive, understand, and describe the relationship between multiple regions.

3. **Advanced Compositional Reasoning Capabilities**: Empowered with the aforementioned features, GAR is naturally equipped with advanced compositional reasoning capabilities, allowing it to answer any specific free-form questions. This shifts the paradigm from passive description to active dialogue and requires the model to synthesize information from local prompts, global context, and the relationships between multiple prompts to arrive at logical conclusions.

4. **GAR-Bench**: A comprehensive benchmark suite designed to systematically evaluate the region-level comprehension capabilities of MLLMs beyond simply describing a single region. It not only provides a more accurate evaluation of single-region comprehension by constructing multiple-choice questions, but also, more importantly, measures interaction and complex reasoning across multiple regions. It includes a multi-prompt captioning task (GAR-Bench-Cap) and a multifaceted visual question answering task (GAR-Bench-VQA). GAR-Bench-Cap contains a caption protocol to measure the correctness of descriptions for the relation between visual prompts. GAR-Bench-VQA evaluates both the basic understanding capability for specific regions (color, shape, material, texture/pattern) and advanced compositional reasoning abilities for multiple regions (Position, Non-Entity Recognition, Relation).

5. **Multi-stage Training Data Pipeline (GAR-2.5M)**: A multi-stage process to generate a large-scale, high-quality dataset that enhances model capabilities from basic object recognition with single region to complex relational reasoning with multiple regions. Round 1 enhances recognition capability by integrating a subset of ImageNet-21K and validating with an LLM to create a refined fine-grained dataset of 456K samples. Round 2 supports multiple prompts by incorporating the Panoptic Scene Graph (PSG) dataset and using Qwen2.5-72B as LLM-Merger to generate 144K rich object descriptions, 144K question-answering pairs, and 126K multiple-choice questions, constructing a relation dataset with 414K samples.

**5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution**

The overall technical solution centers on the GAR model architecture designed to overcome the contextual blindness of isolated region analysis. The model takes an image and a set of binary masks as input. Two new components are introduced into the architecture: (1) a simple yet effective prompt encoding scheme, and (2) a novel RoI-aligned feature replay technique. 

The input binary mask, which specifies the region(s) of interest, is first processed by a simple convolutional block to produce a mask embedding. This zero-initialized mask embedding is then added to ViT’s patch embeddings. The full, uncropped image (with the encoded mask prompt) is then processed by AnyRes, producing a global feature map that is rich in contextual information. Based on the input mask, a corresponding bounding box for the region of interest is derived, and RoI-Align is employed to gather the relevant feature vectors directly from the global feature map. Because the features are extracted from a feature map that was computed over the entire image, they are inherently context-aware. Both the global context features and the detailed local features are then fed into an LLM to accurately infer complex relationships and interactions between multiple objects within the image. 

To train this model, a multi-stage data pipeline constructs the GAR-2.5M dataset, transitioning the model from basic recognition to complex relational reasoning. Finally, GAR-Bench is introduced to systematically evaluate region-level comprehension capabilities, including multi-prompt interaction and advanced compositional reasoning.

**6. Detailed Module Design (If any, complete mechanisms of each layer/sub-module)**

- **Prompt Encoding and Integration Module**: 
  To integrate spatial guidance into the vision backbone, a lightweight prompt encoding mechanism is introduced. The input binary mask, which specifies the region(s) of interest, is first processed by a simple convolutional block (LeCun et al., 1989) to produce a mask embedding. This zero-initialized (Zhang et al., 2023) mask embedding is then added to ViT’s (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) patch embeddings.

- **RoI-aligned Feature Replay Module**: 
  To simultaneously provide sufficient local details and maintain necessary global context, the RoI-aligned feature replay technique is proposed. Specifically, the model first encodes the full, uncropped image (together with the mask prompt) with AnyRes (Liu et al., 2024), producing a global feature map that is rich in contextual information. Based on the input mask, a corresponding bounding box for the region of interest is then derived. Subsequently, RoI-Align (He et al., 2017) is employed to gather the relevant feature vectors directly from the global feature map. Because the features are extracted from a feature map that was computed over the entire image, they are inherently context-aware, providing sufficient local details while maintaining global information simultaneously. This replay of context-rich features allows GAR to simultaneously “zoom in” on detail without “losing sight” of the bigger picture.

- **Training Data Pipeline Module (GAR-2.5M)**:
  - **Round 1: Enhance Recognition Capability**: Starting from the Describe Anything-1.5M dataset, deficiencies in its fine-grained recognition capability are observed, limiting the quality of generated captions for more complex scenarios. To address this, images and masks provided by Sun et al. (2024), a subset of ImageNet-21K (Deng et al., 2009) renowned for its detailed and extensive category labels, are integrated. The seed captioner is employed to generate descriptions, and then an LLM is utilized to validate these generated captions against the ground-truth categories, resulting in a refined fine-grained dataset of 456K samples. Both datasets are utilized to train a fine-grained captioner.
  - **Round 2: Supporting Multiple Prompts**: To further enable understanding multiple prompts, the Panoptic Scene Graph (PSG) dataset (Yang et al., 2022), which is rich in relational information, is incorporated. The fine-grained captioner is first queried to generate a detailed description for each region. Subsequently, Qwen2.5-72B (Team, 2024) is regarded as the LLM-Merger, together with the original annotations provided by the PSG dataset, to generate: (1) 144K rich object descriptions that explicitly integrate relational context, (2) 144K question-answering pairs designed to probe the understanding of complex relationships, and (3) 126K multiple-choice questions. A relation dataset with 414K samples in total is constructed during this stage.

- **GAR-Bench Module**:
  - **GAR-Bench-Cap**: Goes beyond isolated object descriptions and measures the ability to perform compositional scene understanding. It contains two sub-tasks: (1) simply describe the relationship, and (2) generate detailed captions including necessary relationships. For the “simple” protocol, models are directly asked with “what is the relationship between <Prompt1> and <Prompt2>” and are required to answer the question simply. For the “detailed” protocol, the model is not evaluated on its ability to describe each independently, but rather on its capacity to generate an accurate description of their relation. The models need to perform spatial reasoning, action recognition, and semantic integration across disparate image regions, thereby quantifying its ability to interpret a scene as a cohesive whole rather than a collection of independent parts.
  - **GAR-Bench-VQA**: Designed to shift the evaluation from static description to dynamic, interactive dialogue. It is divided into two distinct but complementary sub-tasks: “perception” and “reasoning”.
    - **Perception**: Evaluates the model’s foundational ability to recognize basic visual attributes of a single object, serving as a litmus test for its core visual acuity. Specifically, for a given visual prompt, the model is asked targeted questions about its intrinsic visual properties, specifically focusing on color, shape, material, and texture/pattern.
    - **Reasoning**: Designed to probe higher-order cognitive abilities, composed of several sub-tasks:
      - **Position**: Evaluates the model’s grasp of spatial arrangement and ordinal logic within a global context. A model is presented with a mask on a single object within a larger group and asked to identify its precise position in a complex, grid-like structure. Answering correctly requires the model to not only recognize the masked object but also to process the entire scene structure.
      - **Non-Entity Recognition**: Designed to test the capability to leverage sufficient global context. For instance, the given prompt might highlight a reflection in a mirror, the shadow of a person, a face depicted on a television screen, and so on. The model is then queried to determine if the prompted region corresponds to a physical entity. Success in this task demonstrates that the model is performing sophisticated context-aware reasoning rather than simple pattern matching on the masked pixels alone.
      - **Relation**: Measures the capacity for complex compositional reasoning across multiple prompts. The model is presented with several visual prompts and must deduce the intricate spatial or logical relationship between them. A key challenge is the inclusion of redundant prompts. To arrive at the correct answer, the model must ignore the potentially distracting information. It requires the model to build a mental “scene graph”, which is essential for comprehending complex object assemblies and interactions in cluttered, real-world environments.

**7. All Mathematical Formulas and Symbol Definitions (If any, replicate exactly as in the original text)**

The task of grasping any region is a hierarchical challenge from basic perception to complex, compositional reasoning about specific visual regions. Specifically, given an image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, where $H \times W$ indicates the resolution, and a set of $N$ binary visual prompts, e.g., masks $\{M_i\}_{i=1}^N$, where $M_i \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$, the objective is to generate a precise text response $R$ that demonstrates a multi-layered comprehension of the scene, e.g., detailed attributes description and relational caption, based on the given text instruction $T$:

$$R = \text{RegionModel}(I, \{M_i\}_{i=1}^N, T)$$
