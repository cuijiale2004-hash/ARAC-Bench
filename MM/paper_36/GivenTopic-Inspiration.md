Given Topic: Domain generalization aims to enable models trained on source domains to generalize reliably to unseen target domains. Existing approaches predominantly rely on invariant feature representation learning, data augmentation, regularization, or meta-learning strategies. While effective to some extent, these methods are primarily confined to feature-level invariance and often struggle to capture higher-level cross-domain semantic commonalities, limiting their robustness under complex distribution shifts. Furthermore, current paradigms lack explicit exploitation of structured decision-making processes underlying generalizable predictions. This work investigates whether integrating structured, class-relevant reasoning chains as complementary supervision can bridge domain gaps. It addresses the fundamental challenges in distilling and optimizing such reasoning signals within multimodal language models, seeking to establish a process-level invariance mechanism alongside traditional feature-level alignment for improved out-of-domain generalization.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that semantic descriptions and reasoning trajectories for identical categories remain notably consistent across domains with drastically different visual styles, implying that structured reasoning captures domain-invariant regularities that low-level visual features fail to preserve.
2. The capability of multimodal language models to produce explicit, step-wise semantic decomposition suggests that integrating process-level reasoning into decision-making could complement traditional feature-level invariance strategies.
3. Complex reasoning-chain supervision introduces a significant optimization burden compared to direct label prediction, as the model must first align lengthy intermediate sequences before arriving at the correct output, necessitating an auxiliary stabilizing objective.
4. Reasoning patterns generated by powerful external systems tend to be linguistically rich and contextually detailed, whereas student models naturally produce sparser, label-centric explanations, creating a fundamental mismatch between supervision style and internal reasoning tendencies.
5. Introducing a simpler, label-focused supervisory signal alongside complex reasoning chains could stabilize the optimization landscape and prevent training from overfitting to high-entropy intermediate linguistic patterns.
6. Encouraging the model to produce and iteratively refine its own reasoning explanations, while filtering for semantic correctness, could gradually align the supervision distribution with the model's intrinsic reasoning style without sacrificing discriminative information.
7. The principle that higher-level semantic abstractions inherently exhibit lower cross-domain discrepancy than perceptual visual embeddings suggests that structured reasoning operates at a transferable layer of representation particularly suited for robust generalization.
