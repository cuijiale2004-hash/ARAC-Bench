Given Topic: Mask-based diffusion language models represent a prominent paradigm in text generation, progressively recovering masked tokens to synthesize coherent sequences. Nevertheless, prevailing approaches adhere to a rigid assumption that unmasked tokens are immutable once revealed, precluding any subsequent revision even when expanded contextual information exposes earlier predictions as erroneous. This irreversible commitment mechanism engenders persistent error propagation along the generation trajectory, constraining the model's capacity for self-correction. The central scientific challenge resides in developing a principled mechanism that enables iterative reassessment and refinement of previously generated tokens within the diffusion framework, leveraging progressively enriched context to identify and rectify inferior predictions while strictly preserving the monotonic reduction of noise throughout the reverse process.

Given Inspiration:
1. The cognitive phenomenon of human self-reflection, wherein provisional judgments are continuously re-evaluated and revised in light of newly acquired contextual evidence rather than being permanently fixed.
2. The structural limitation of existing generation paradigms that enforce irreversible intermediate decisions, suggesting that text synthesis should be conceptualized as an iterative refinement procedure rather than an accumulation of immutable commitments.
3. The metacognitive principle that internally estimated prediction reliability can function as a discriminative signal to flag uncertain or erroneous intermediate outputs for subsequent reconsideration.
4. The systems-engineering perspective that progressive information accumulation in sequential decoding naturally warrants conditional revisability of earlier states as later structural constraints become manifest.
5. The physical intuition that diffusion-based generative processes must observe monotonic noise attenuation, implying that token revision must constitute an intrinsic component of the forward denoising dynamics rather than an extraneous post-hoc correction.
