Given Topic: The field of end-to-end object detection, particularly transformer-based set prediction methods. Current approaches either depend on hand-crafted components such as predefined anchors and post-processing heuristics, or suffer from slow convergence and training instability arising from query initialization lacking explicit spatial priors. Existing denoising training strategies merely serve as auxiliary optimization aids rather than fundamentally reformulating query generation as a probabilistic process. Furthermore, adapting generative denoising frameworks to structured set prediction remains considerably less explored than pixel-level applications. The core problem is how to reformulate the generation of spatial object priors as a conditional denoising process, thereby eliminating reliance on handcrafted inductive biases while ensuring training stability and inference efficiency.

Given Inspiration:
1. The probabilistic framework of denoising diffusion, which learns to reverse a gradual noising process, providing inspiration to treat object spatial localization as a conditional generative process recoverable from noise.
2. The paradigm shift of viewing detection as structured output generation rather than pure discrimination, motivating the generation of object candidates from learnable distributions conditioned on image content.
3. The bottleneck that existing end-to-end detectors rely on query embeddings initialized without explicit spatial semantics, suggesting the need for a generative approach to synthesize spatial priors progressively.
4. The observation that diffusion processes on low-dimensional latent spaces require significantly fewer sampling steps than high-dimensional pixel spaces, offering a pathway for efficient generative inference in detection.
5. The gap between the widespread success of generative denoising in pixel-level vision tasks and its underexploration in unordered set prediction tasks, highlighting an opportunity to bridge generative modeling with structured detection.
