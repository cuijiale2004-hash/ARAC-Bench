Given Topic: Post-training of large language models via reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing task-specific capabilities. However, the effectiveness of subsequent reinforcement learning training is highly sensitive to the initialization quality of the base model, necessitating a preliminary supervised cold-start phase to inject essential reasoning patterns. Current practice optimizes the cold-start phase through standard supervised fine-tuning objectives that prioritize demonstration imitation and evaluation metric maximization. Nevertheless, this conventional approach suffers from a fundamental misalignment: the checkpoint with the highest evaluation performance often deviates excessively from the base model's original distribution, leading to distributional forgetting prior to traditional overfitting and consequently degrading the model's exploration potential for subsequent reinforcement learning. The core scientific issue lies in identifying proper optimization criteria and control mechanisms for the cold-start phase that can harmonize the acquisition of new reasoning patterns with the preservation of the base model's foundational distribution, thereby establishing superior initialization states for reinforcement learning post-training.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that reinforcement learning potential degrades prior to traditional overfitting during cold-start training suggests that evaluation-centric optimization is fundamentally misaligned with the goal of preparing model initialization for subsequent reinforcement learning.
2. Empirical dynamics of output diversity metrics reveal characteristic turning points during cold-start training, indicating that maximal response diversity may serve as a more reliable indicator of reinforcement learning readiness than task accuracy.
3. The heterogeneity in distributional drift across different tokens and sequence prefixes implies that uniform optimization strategies are insufficient, motivating context-adaptive mechanisms to balance pattern acquisition with base distribution preservation.
4. Information-theoretic principles regarding the decomposition of sequence-level uncertainty into prefix-conditioned components suggest that prefix confidence should modulate the degree of distribution preservation during adaptation.
5. The asymmetric data scale between lightweight cold-start supervision and large-scale reinforcement learning suggests that cold-start objectives should function as distributional steering mechanisms rather than pursuing complete demonstration memorization.
