**Principle 1: Principled Theoretical Justification for Token-Level Log-Likelihood Attribution in Cross-Model Adapter Transfer**

**Definition:**  
Research proposing token-level selective transfer for PEFT adapters must establish a rigorous, non-heuristic theoretical foundation explaining why the proposed signal—such as token-wise contrastive excess or log-likelihood ratio—reliably isolates transferable task knowledge. The justification should anchor the metric in established statistical frameworks (e.g., Neyman–Pearson optimality, information theory) and empirically demonstrate that high-scoring tokens are genuinely informative rather than artifacts of model confidence or noise. This principle ensures that the token-selection mechanism is not merely an engineering trick but a principled attribution method that localizes adapter-injected knowledge, distinguishing it fundamentally from sequence-level distillation or global logit matching.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Theoretical Derivation:** Is the token-level score explicitly linked to established statistical tests (e.g., log-likelihood ratio) or information-theoretic quantities, with clear proofs or derivations provided?
- **Contrastive Subgroup Validation:** Does the work empirically contrast top-ranked tokens against bottom-ranked and random subsets to validate that concentrated knowledge resides in the selected tokens?
- **Cross-Expert Consistency:** Is evidence provided that important tokens identified by the score are consistently selected across different source model families and architectures?
- **Causal Distinction from Baselines:** Does the work clearly articulate why token-level attribution avoids the pitfalls of global imitation, especially when the source adapter is weak or biased?

**Principle 2: Accuracy and Robustness of Cross-Tokenizer Signal Propagation Under Vocabulary Heterogeneity**

**Definition:**  
For cross-backbone adapter transplantation where source and target models employ different tokenizers, the evaluation must rigorously assess the algorithm that propagates token-level importance masks across disparate vocabularies. A trustworthy method cannot assume one-to-one token correspondence; it must deterministically or probabilistically handle many-to-many, many-to-one, and one-to-many mappings while preserving the semantic integrity of the transferred signal. This principle demands quantitative evidence that alignment errors are negligible and that non-trivial mappings contribute positively to transfer quality, ensuring the method's practicality in real-world deployment across heterogeneous model ecosystems.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Quantitative Alignment Coverage:** Are alignment statistics (e.g., percentages of one-to-one, many-to-many, and exception cases) reported for cross-family transfers?
- **Ablation on Mapping Types:** Does the work include ablations showing performance when non-trivial alignments are excluded versus included?
- **Error Bounds or Deterministic Guarantees:** Is the alignment algorithm proven or empirically shown to be exception-free or bounded in mismatch rate on actual transfer tasks?
- **Semantic Preservation:** Is there evidence that propagated fractional or binary mask scores retain discriminative power after vocabulary transformation?

**Principle 3: Comprehensive Generalization Validation Across Architectures, Scales, and Data Modalities**

**Definition:**  
A token-level transfer framework must demonstrate robust generalization across the full spectrum of realistic deployment scenarios, including variations in model architecture (e.g., dense to mixture-of-experts), parameter scale (e.g., 7B to 70B), model family, and data modality. Crucially, the method should not be tightly coupled to high-quality synthetic data generated by the source model; it must remain effective when using external, non-synthetic, or low-quality datasets. Additionally, the approach should be resilient to suboptimal source adapters, avoiding negative transfer when the source LoRA underperforms the target baseline. This principle separates truly generalizable transfer mechanisms from brittle, setting-specific improvements.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Cross-Architecture and Scale Transfer:** Are experiments conducted across distinct architectures (e.g., dense → MoE) and frontier-scale models (≥70B parameters)?
- **Data-Mode Independence:** Does the method maintain performance when using external real-world data instead of synthetic teacher-generated data?
- **Source Adapter Quality Robustness:** Are results reported for cases where the source LoRA is weaker than the target vanilla model, proving selective token retrieval avoids harmful imitation?
- **Prompt and Generation Robustness:** Is sensitivity to synthetic data prompt design, diversity filtering, and data quality explicitly tested?

**Principle 4: Empirical Rigor in Baseline Selection, Efficiency Benchmarking, and Hyperparameter Sensitivity**

**Definition:**  
Claims of efficiency and superiority in adapter transplantation research must be supported by rigorous empirical benchmarking against appropriate baselines, fair computational cost comparisons, and exhaustive sensitivity analyses. Reviewers must assess whether baseline choices are methodologically sound (excluding incompatible methods with clear justification), whether wall-clock or sample-level overhead claims are empirically validated (e.g., comparing discriminator-free versus discriminator-based pipelines), and whether key hyperparameters—especially token-selection thresholds—are evaluated across a broad spectrum rather than cherry-picked. Statistical variance and significance testing are essential to ensure that reported gains are reproducible and not artifacts of random seed or narrow tuning.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Baseline Justification and Fairness:** Are compared baselines selected based on problem compatibility (e.g., cross-family applicability, data-free constraints), and are excluded methods explained?
- **End-to-End Efficiency Metrics:** Are training and inference overheads measured with actual runtime or FLOPs, breaking down contributions from each stage?
- **Hyperparameter Spectrum Analysis:** Is the token-selection ratio (or equivalent threshold) evaluated across a wide, continuous range (e.g., 10%–90%) to reveal stability regions?
- **Statistical Reliability:** Are results accompanied by standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests across multiple random seeds?

**Principle 5: Disentanglement of Token-Selective Transfer from Synthetic Data Dependence and Source Model Strength**

**Definition:**  
The work must clearly establish its conceptual novelty by disentangling its core mechanism—token-level selective transfer—from adjacent paradigms such as sequence-level synthetic data distillation or auxiliary discriminator-based filtering. It should articulate how selective token extraction redefines the knowledge transfer objective itself, shifting focus from replicating full model outputs to identifying and aligning internal expert-amateur divergence. The evaluation should verify that the method's efficacy stems from the token-selection logic rather than from synthetic data engineering or increased model capacity, ensuring that the contribution is a fundamental rethinking of how adapter knowledge is localized and transplanted across heterogeneous backbones.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Conceptual Distinction from Prior Art:** Does the work clearly differentiate its token-level signal from sequence-level transfer and explain why this represents a conceptual advance rather than a simplification?
- **Mechanistic Ablation:** Are components such as sample filtering and token selection ablated independently to isolate the contribution of the token-level mechanism?
- **Output Decoupling Validation:** Does the method work when synthetic labels are not generated by the source teacher, proving efficacy is not tied to teacher-generated sequence fidelity?
- **Qualitative and Quantitative Interpretability:** Are qualitative examples or visualization analyses provided to show which tokens are selected and why they carry structural or semantic importance?
