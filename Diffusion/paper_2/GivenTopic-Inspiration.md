Given Topic: Ultra-High-Definition image restoration aims to recover high-quality images from degraded 4K/8K inputs. While diffusion models offer powerful generative priors, their direct application to ultra-high-resolution restoration encounters substantial challenges. Existing restoration networks, though architecturally advanced, remain limited by the fundamentally ill-posed nature of the task and insufficient exploitation of generative priors. When scaling diffusion-based methods to ultra-high resolutions, patch-based inference inevitably introduces global inconsistencies and boundary artifacts, while the inherent stochasticity of diffusion processes produces hallucinated textures in smooth regions. Furthermore, the latent compression mechanisms underlying these models inevitably discard fine-grained high-frequency information, creating bottlenecks that constrain detail fidelity. Therefore, the core problem lies in developing a principled adaptation strategy that can harness pre-trained diffusion priors for ultra-high-definition restoration while simultaneously enforcing global structural coherence across patches, suppressing local detail hallucinations, and alleviating information loss introduced by latent compression—all without altering the pre-trained generative backbone.

1. The observation that phase spectra in the frequency domain predominantly encode structural geometry, whereas amplitude spectra govern texture style, suggesting that phase-level regularization can enforce global structural alignment without compromising local texture diversity.

3. The insight that unconstrained generative diffusion in local patches amplifies stochasticity, producing unrealistic high-frequency artifacts in smooth regions, which necessitates global contextual guidance to constrain local detail generation.
4. The inherent information bottleneck in perceptual compression frameworks, which discard fine-grained high-frequency details during encoding, motivating supplementary pathways to recover lost structural and textural information during decoding.
5. The complementary properties of frequency-domain analysis and deep semantic features, indicating that synergistic regularization across spectral and feature representations can simultaneously stabilize global coherence and enhance local fidelity.
