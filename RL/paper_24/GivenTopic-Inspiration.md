Given Topic: The research addresses online reinforcement learning fine-tuning from offline datasets within the domain of continuous control, specifically when leveraging highly expressive generative policy classes. Existing online methods are predominantly designed for simple unimodal action distributions and fail to accommodate modern policies parameterized by deep iterative generative chains. The fundamental pain point is that direct optimization of value functions through such chained parameterizations leads to unstable gradient propagation and computationally intractable credit assignment. Moreover, standard policy extraction via slow parametric updates prevents immediate adaptation to evolving critic estimates. The core scientific challenge is to achieve stable, sample-efficient value maximization and exploration for expressive policy classes without requiring direct end-to-end differentiation through their complex generative parameterizations.

Given Inspiration:
1. End-to-end value gradient propagation through deep iterative generative chains is inherently unstable, suggesting that value maximization must be decoupled from the primary generative parameterization.
2. Supervised behavior modeling offers stable training for highly expressive function approximators, indicating that the prior behavior distribution should be maintained via imitation objectives rather than replaced by reinforcement learning losses.
3. Local action refinement constrained to a small neighborhood around sampled actions enables effective policy improvement while preserving the structure of the prior behavior distribution.
4. Decision-time implicit value maximization via candidate evaluation permits immediate adaptation to value function changes without requiring slow parametric alignment.
5. State-dependent exploratory noise can be injected through a lightweight auxiliary stochastic component rather than by perturbing the complex primary generative architecture.
6. Maintaining proximity to the offline behavior distribution during online adaptation prevents catastrophic deviation and preserves fine-grained behavioral capabilities when transferring from offline pretraining.
7. Decomposing control into a stable behavioral prior and a constrained local adjustment mechanism allows the system to preserve complex behavior distributions while accommodating online corrective signals.
8. Implicit policy extraction via sampling and ranking circumvents explicit backpropagation through iterative generative chains, enabling stable value-directed action selection.
