Given Topic: In the field of deep learning optimization and loss landscape geometry, current research has established that distinct solutions in overparameterized networks are often connected by low-loss paths, suggesting a globally connected valley. However, a fundamental paradox remains: stochastic optimization dynamics consistently confine themselves to localized basins despite the existence of these connecting paths. Existing methods fail to explain why noise-driven dynamics do not diffuse across these low-loss regions. This work addresses the core scientific issue of understanding the mechanisms that effectively partition the parameter space into disjoint dynamical basins, even when energetic barriers are absent, and how late-time localization emerges during training.

Given Inspiration:
1. Statistical physics principles of entropic forces generated by the interaction between stochastic fluctuations and spatially varying geometric constraints in energy landscapes.
2. The thermodynamic perspective that noisy dynamics minimize free energy rather than pure energy, creating an inherent trade-off between loss reduction and entropy maximization.
3. Geometric observations that curvature variations along continuous low-loss paths can induce effective biases that guide stochastic trajectories toward broader regions.
4. The conceptualization of optimization noise as an effective temperature that couples with local landscape geometry to produce emergent dynamical confinement.
5. The principle that flatter regions of a landscape possess greater accessible volume, biasing stochastic exploration toward geometrically favorable configurations independent of energy gradients.
