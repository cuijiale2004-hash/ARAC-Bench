Given Topic: Generative modeling for sequential discrete data remains dominated by left-to-right sequential decoding paradigms, which inherently restrict parallel generation and preclude post-hoc revision of synthesized content. Alternative paradigms based on iterative denoising in discrete state spaces have been proposed to circumvent these limitations; however, their scaling behavior is poorly understood, with preliminary evidence suggesting inferior data and compute efficiency compared to sequential counterparts. Existing scaling analyses for these iterative approaches may further be complicated by fixed or suboptimal training hyperparameters. This work addresses the need for a principled characterization of scaling laws for discrete iterative denoising language models across diverse corruption strategies, focusing on the interplay between model expressivity, dataset scale, and training configuration to determine compute-optimal and data-optimal scaling regimes.

Given Inspiration:
1. The inherent constraints of prevailing sequential decoding paradigms, which preclude parallel token synthesis and retrospective content refinement, motivate the exploration of iterative refinement-based generative alternatives.
2. The conceptual hierarchy of task difficulty, wherein corruption processes with progressively diminished structural inductive bias impose greater modeling challenges, suggests that scaling dynamics should vary systematically with the degree of generative structure.
3. The treatment of signal-to-noise ratio as a fundamental descriptor in continuous-state diffusion theory, offering a temporally invariant perspective on corruption processes, inspires an analogous reparameterization for discrete state spaces.
4. The principle of zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across model scales, which posits that stable training dynamics can be maintained without exhaustive per-model tuning, facilitates systematic large-scale scaling analyses.
5. The decomposition of training into distinct pre-training and convergence enhancement phases, as observed in contemporary large-scale training recipes, motivates the isolation of scaling behavior from final learning rate annealing effects.
6. Empirical observations regarding the scaling of optimal batch sizes with training data volume rather than model capacity suggest that batch size should be treated as a critical variable in scaling law estimation rather than a fixed constant.
