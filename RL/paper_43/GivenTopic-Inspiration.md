Given Topic: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has emerged as a core paradigm for enhancing the complex reasoning capabilities of large language models. While effective during training, standard approaches fail to provide verification signals during test-time inference when ground-truth answers are unavailable. Existing methods that equip models with self-verification capabilities typically require sequential generation of reasoning solutions and verification texts under separate prompts, significantly increasing per-sample inference overhead and reducing overall efficiency. The core challenge lies in developing a unified framework that jointly optimizes reasoning generation and self-evaluation capabilities without introducing substantial additional computational costs during either training or inference.

Given Inspiration:
1. Theoretical analysis of the reinforcement learning objective for verification reveals that its optimal closed-form solution can be reduced to a simple relationship involving the probability distribution of the terminal generation token.
2. Empirical observation that the reference model's predicted log-probability for certain special tokens remains stable and approximately constant across diverse problem-solution pairs.
3. The insight that a model's self-assessment of solution correctness can be implicitly captured by the next-token probability distribution at the final step of the generation process.
4. Existing approaches that unify reasoning and verification require sequential generation under separate conditions, motivating the derivation of evaluation signals directly from the generation trajectory without additional decoding steps.
5. The observation that sequence-level reward signals in reasoning tasks exhibit strong length bias, indicating the necessity of compact, localized reward representations.
