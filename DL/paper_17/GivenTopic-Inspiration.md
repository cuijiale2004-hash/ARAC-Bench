Given Topic: Neural audio generation reconstructs high-fidelity waveforms from compressed acoustic representations, serving as a critical backend for speech and music synthesis. Current methodologies face a fundamental dilemma between optimization stability and sampling efficiency. Methods enabling rapid inference often suffer from prolonged training convergence and mode collapse risks, whereas approaches with stable training dynamics typically depend on costly multi-step iterative sampling. Additionally, the unique acoustic properties—including extensive silent intervals and perceptually critical low-energy spectral regions—pose significant challenges to conventional generative objectives. The central scientific challenge is to establish a unified training paradigm that simultaneously ensures stable generative capability acquisition, facilitates high-fidelity few-step inference, and adequately accounts for the perceptual and structural idiosyncrasies of audio signals.

Given Inspiration:
1. The recognition that generative frameworks with stable optimization dynamics and discriminative refinement mechanisms possess complementary strengths motivates their sequential synergy rather than isolated application.
2. Audio signals contain extensive zero-energy regions where rate-based estimation is ill-posed; directly predicting the terminal state offers a more coherent objective for such sparse distributions.
3. Human auditory perception is disproportionately sensitive to distortions in low-energy spectral regions, indicating that reconstruction objectives should inversely weight errors by spectral energy to align with perceptual salience.
4. Audio exhibits hierarchical structures across different time-frequency granularities, suggesting that simultaneous modeling at multiple spectral resolutions can better capture its complex acoustic patterns.
5. Continuous probability flow trajectories learned during generative pre-training can provide robust initialization priors for constructing efficient few-step generators without prohibitive inference costs.
