Given Topic: Medical image segmentation, particularly three-dimensional volumetric delineation, is fundamental to modern diagnosis and treatment planning. Although diffusion-based generative frameworks have exhibited notable robustness in two-dimensional medical imaging tasks, their adaptation to three-dimensional volumetric segmentation remains severely constrained. Current methodologies exhibit a critical failure mode characterized by the collapse of reverse sampling when initialized from high-noise states, resulting in anatomically incoherent predictions. This instability stems from the exponentially enlarged manifold of volumetric data coupled with the vanishing structural cues present under extreme noise conditions. Moreover, standard paradigms iterate through denoising timesteps in isolation, precluding the accumulation and propagation of evidence across the temporal trajectory. Therefore, a pressing scientific challenge is to establish stable initialization strategies for high-dimensional volumetric diffusion under near-Gaussian noise regimes while endowing the sampling process with explicit temporal consistency across sequential steps.
