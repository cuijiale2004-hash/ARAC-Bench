Given Topic: Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) constitute a prominent class of brain-inspired computing models that process temporal information through discrete spike events. However, dominant existing approaches describe neuronal membrane dynamics via first-order ordinary differential equations, which impose a Markovian structure wherein the current state is conditioned solely on its immediate predecessor. This mathematical simplification inherently constrains the network's expressive power and its capacity to model complex, long-range temporal dependencies observed in biological systems. The central scientific problem is to establish a generalized dynamical framework that transcends memoryless evolution, enabling the capture of persistent historical dependencies while preserving computational feasibility and architectural compatibility.

Given Inspiration:
1. Neurophysiological evidence demonstrates that biological neurons exhibit long-range temporal correlations, fractal dendritic morphologies, and history-dependent adaptation that cannot be reconciled with memoryless dynamical assumptions.
2. Experimental observations of spike-frequency adaptation suggest that subthreshold membrane relaxation in cortical neurons obeys power-law temporal decay rather than exponential forgetting.
3. The mathematical theory of non-integer-order calculus provides a rigorous foundation for modeling systems with non-local, memory-dependent evolution, generalizing conventional differential descriptions.
4. Prevailing integer-order neuronal models fundamentally limit representational capacity by restricting state transitions to depend exclusively on instantaneous conditions, neglecting cumulative historical influences.
5. Dynamical systems endowed with long-memory kernels exhibit enhanced stability against perturbations, suggesting inherent robustness advantages for temporally precise information encoding.
