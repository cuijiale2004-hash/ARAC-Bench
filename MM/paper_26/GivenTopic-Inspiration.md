Given Topic: The research lies at the intersection of multimodal large language models and video object segmentation. Current training-based approaches necessitate costly joint optimization of vision-language and segmentation foundation models, which limits scalability and generalization. Conversely, existing training-free methods predominantly address static image domains and rely on brittle heuristics or head-selection strategies that fail to generalize across diverse model architectures and complex spatio-temporal scenarios. A fundamental obstacle is that raw attention mechanisms in multimodal transformers produce noisy, low-resolution activation maps plagued by irrelevant dominant responses and misalignment with target object regions, particularly under complex linguistic queries requiring temporal reasoning. The core scientific issue is how to robustly extract and refine implicit localization signals from pre-trained multimodal transformers to achieve accurate, training-free segmentation of referred objects in video sequences.

Given Inspiration:
1. The inherent coupling between high-level reasoning and spatial localization in multimodal transformers, where attention distributions elicited during language-driven video question answering implicitly encode object-centric visual grounding.
2. The limitation of prevailing paradigms that rely on parameter-efficient fine-tuning or joint training between vision-language and segmentation architectures, which incur substantial computational overhead and compromise cross-dataset generalization.
3. The observation that raw attention maps suffer from persistent background dominance and task-irrelevant activation sinks, motivating the need for a principled contrastive mechanism to disentangle foreground object signals from ubiquitous background responses.
4. The complementary characteristics of temporally extended video attention and spatially concentrated frame attention, suggesting that aggregating multi-granularity visual cues can reconcile temporal reasoning with fine-grained object detail.
5. The insight that aggregated attention trajectories across transformer layers indiscriminately blend informative and noisy signals, revealing that decomposing and selectively recombining these flows can suppress interference while preserving task-relevant localization cues.
6. The engineering perspective that pixel-level segmentation models can be driven by sparse geometric prompts, inspiring the treatment of refined semantic attention distributions as surrogate spatial guidance to bridge high-level reasoning and dense mask generation without architectural retraining.
