Given Topic: Post-training of large language models has emerged as a critical stage for enhancing complex reasoning capabilities. While reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning demonstrably yields superior performance compared to supervised fine-tuning alone, the underlying internal mechanisms driving these improvements remain poorly understood. Existing research on model interpretability typically analyzes internal structures in isolation, without establishing connections between specific training methodologies and their induced structural changes. Consequently, a significant gap persists between external behavioral evaluations of post-training effects and the corresponding internal mechanistic transformations. This necessitates a systematic investigation into how reinforcement learning reshapes internal information pathways during inference, bridging empirical performance gains with interpretable shifts in neuronal circuitry.

Given Inspiration:
1. The persistent disconnect between documented external performance improvements following reinforcement learning post-training and the lack of mechanistic understanding regarding corresponding internal structural changes.
2. Existing interpretability frameworks that attribute importance to internal computational pathways, suggesting principled methods to quantify how individual connections influence model outputs without exhaustive ablation.
3. An information-flow graph perspective of model architectures, enabling systematic tracing of how signals propagate and transform between sequential internal stages.
4. The intuition that training algorithms characterized by distinct data sampling dynamics and feedback mechanisms may fundamentally alter the recruitment and strengthening patterns of internal pathways.
5. The hypothesis that broadening the distribution of encountered reasoning trajectories during training necessitates the engagement of otherwise latent internal circuits, implying a direct link between training exploration and internal pathway activation.
