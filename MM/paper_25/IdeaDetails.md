## 1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points

**Research Background:**
There is a growing interest in Universal Multimodal Embeddings (UME), where models are required to generate task-specific representations. UMEs bridge the gap between general-purpose content similarity and user-specific requirements, unlocking applications such as retrieving documents or images based on nuanced queries, powering personalized recommendation systems, and supporting multimodal search with complex transformations. Recent benchmarks like MMEB aggregate diverse, instruction-driven retrieval tasks—including VQA, grounding, and document retrieval—where both the input and the user instruction determine the retrieval target. Leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as encoders for UME is a promising approach, as MLLMs can generate instruction-aware representations that incorporate long context and nuanced user guidance.

**Existing Pain Points:**
1.  **Overlooking Generative Capacity:** While recent studies show that MLLMs perform well on UME tasks, they treat MLLMs solely as encoders, overlooking their generative capacity obtained from pre-training.
2.  **Ineffectiveness for Complex Instructions:** The encoding paradigm becomes less effective as instructions become more complex and require compositional reasoning. Existing methods (relying on hard negative sampling, additional training stages, external data, etc.) fail to address the central challenge of understanding instructions of varying complexity that require different levels of reasoning.
3.  **Retrieval Asymmetry:** Directly encoding queries and matching them to targets without explicit reasoning leads to sub-optimal fine-grained and accurate retrieval, particularly for complex tasks like VQA, classification, visual grounding, and composed retrieval which require substantial reasoning capabilities.

## 2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions

**Core Research Motivation:**
The research is motivated by the proven effectiveness of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in complex problem solving. Just as intermediate reasoning processes significantly improve the accuracy of language model generation, a similar benefit can be achieved for multimodal representation learning. Since the embeddings produced by MLLMs are inherently conditioned on the sequence of preceding tokens, explicitly augmenting multi-step reasoning and contextual grounding enables the model to achieve more fine-grained and accurate retrieval.

**Scientific Questions:**
How can explicit chain-of-thought reasoning enhance universal multimodal embeddings? Can we leverage the generative capacity of MLLMs rather than restricting them to encoder-only models to better interpret and follow complex task instructions and reduce retrieval asymmetry? How can we integrate reasoning and embedding generation efficiently within a unified model without suffering from conflicting training objectives?

## 3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy

**Overall Core Idea:**
The paper proposes a general Think-Then-Embed (TTE) framework for Universal Multimodal Embeddings (UME), composed of a Reasoner and an Embedder. The key idea is to introduce an explicit thinking stage before embedding, in which the model generates reasoning traces based on the given instruction. The Reasoner MLLM first generates Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR) traces that explain complex queries, followed by an Embedder that produces representations conditioned on both the original query and the intermediate reasoning.

**Design Philosophy:**
1.  **Generative-then-Encoding:** Leverage MLLMs for both generative reasoning and representation learning rather than restricting them to encoder-only models. The embeddings are conditioned on the sequence of preceding tokens, including the reasoning traces.
2.  **Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR):** Formulate reasoning tailored to improve embedding quality, capturing step-by-step reasoning to understand the query or detailed descriptions of referred objects along with surrounding visual context.
3.  **Decoupling Objectives:** When unifying reasoning and embedding, decouple the contrastive and generative objectives to prevent interference. Hidden states generated during reasoning are reused for downstream representation extraction, but specialized parameters (embedding heads) are used so that the embedding training objective does not interfere with reasoning.
4.  **Efficiency via Distillation and Unification:** Reduce dependency on large teacher reasoners by distilling high-quality ECR traces into smaller student reasoners, and explore unified models to produce embeddings in a single forward pass.

## 4. Core Innovation Points

1.  **TTE Framework with Teacher Reasoner:** Proposed a Think-Then-Embed (TTE) framework that by exploiting reasoning traces generated by a powerful reasoner (e.g., Qwen2.5 VL 72B), it can achieve state-of-the-art performance on MMEB V2 with a smaller embedder (Qwen2 7B), surpassing close-sourced models trained with additional data. This provides an effective way for retrieval test-time-scaling.
2.  **Distilled Student Reasoner:** Finetuned a smaller MLLM reasoner using high-quality embedding-centric reasoning traces generated by the teacher reasoner. The resulting SFT-ed reasoner performs effectively across the entire benchmark, achieving the best performance among open-source models with a 7% absolute gain over recently proposed models.
3.  **Unified Reasoner and Embedder (TTEu):** Investigated strategies for integrating the reasoner and embedder into a unified model for improved efficiency. A two-stage autoregressive-then-contrastive training with a dedicated embedding head on top of the reasoner almost halves the overall parameters while not degrading end-to-end TTE retrieval performance.
4.  **Systematic Study of Pluggable Embedding Heads:** Presented the first systematic study of embedding head designs that can be seamlessly plugged into a frozen backbone MLLM, evaluating Attention Pooling, NV-Embed-style pooler, QFormer-style head, and Self-Initialized MHSA heads.

## 5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution

The overall technical solution is the Think-Then-Embed (TTE) framework, which enhances Universal Multimodal Embeddings (UME) by incorporating an explicit reasoning step prior to the encoding step. TTE consists of a reasoner, that generates an Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR) trace, and an embedder, that produces task-specific representations conditioned on both the original input and the reasoning trace generated by the reasoner.

Given a multimodal input $\langle V, T, [\text{Ins}] \rangle$, the embedding $h$ is obtained by:
$h = \text{Pooling}(f_\theta(V, [\text{Ins}], T, \psi))$, where $\psi = g_\omega(V, [\text{Ins}], T)$
Here, $g_\omega$ is the reasoner MLLM and $\psi$ is its embedding-centric reasoning trace. $f_\theta$ is the embedder MLLM.

The framework is implemented in three variants:
1.  **TTE with Teacher Reasoner (TTEt):** Employs a powerful MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-72B) as the teacher reasoner, while keeping the embedder model lightweight (2B or 7B). The ECR traces are used for both training and evaluation.
2.  **TTE with Finetuned Student Reasoner (TTEs):** Finetunes a dedicated, student reasoner initializing it from the same backbone as the embedder (2B or 7B) using ECR traces generated by the teacher reasoner.
3.  **TTE with Unified Reasoner and Embedder (TTEu):** Unifies reasoning and embedding generation within a shared backbone using a two-stage training strategy. Stage 1 fully fine-tunes the backbone as a reasoner. Stage 2 freezes the backbone and trains only the embedding head on top of it.

## 6. Detailed Module Design

### 6.1. Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR) Module
ECR is a form of intermediate reasoning tailored to improve embedding quality. It is formulated in the form of `<CoT> ... </CoT> Final Reasoning`, where the reasoner model first outputs some intermediate CoT, then generates the final reasoning.
*   **For QA-based queries:** The CoT is the standard reasoning process; the reasoning is the answer.
*   **For simple embedding tasks (e.g., visual document):** The CoT is a detailed description of the visual input, and the reasoning is the summary.
*   **Optimal ECR:** Defined as $\psi_i^\star \in \text{argmax}_\psi \log \frac{\phi(h_q^i, h_t^i)}{\sum_j \phi(h_q^i, h_t^j)}$. In practice, manually designed task prompts and formats are used to prompt the reasoner MLLM.

### 6.2. TTE with Teacher Reasoner (TTEt)
Uses a large MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-72B) as $g_\omega$. The reasoner is not involved in online retrieval for existing data points and only needs to be run once for new data points. The embedder $f_\theta$ takes in both the original inputs and the ECR generated by the teacher.

### 6.3. TTE with Finetuned Reasoner (TTEs)
Finetunes a small reasoner using ECR traces from the teacher. The small reasoner is initialized from the same backbone as the embedder (2B or 7B). It maximizes the conditional likelihood of the ECR $\psi$ by optimizing the negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss.

### 6.4. TTE with Unified Reasoner and Embedder (TTEu)
To reduce computational overhead, reasoning and embedding are unified within a shared backbone.
*   **Joint SFT-Contrastive Training (Ablated):** Jointly optimizing for both contrastive and autoregressive objectives. This configuration consistently resulted in performance degradation due to challenges in training curriculum and conflicting gradients.
*   **Two-Stage Autoregressive-then-Contrastive Training:** 
    *   **Stage 1:** Fully fine-tune the backbone as a reasoner on the curated ECR dataset.
    *   **Stage 2:** Freeze the backbone and train only the embedding head on top of it. No contrastive gradient flows into the reasoning MLLM, and vice versa.

### 6.5. Pluggable Embedding Head Designs
Designed to be plugged onto a frozen backbone MLLM to extract embeddings from hidden states.
1.  **Attention Pooling:** Learnable attention queries over backbone hidden states (Depth=1). Uses $n_{\text{queries}}$ learnable query vectors.
2.  **NV-Embed-style Pooler:** Learnable latent context with backbone hidden states as queries.
3.  **QFormer-style Embedding Head:** Patched onto the last $n$ layers of the backbone. Requires training from scratch.
4.  **Self-Initialized MHSA:** Repeat the last $n$ layers of the backbone to formulate $N$ new layers as the embedding head. Initialized from the backbone MLLM's weights. Empirically achieves superior performance compared to QFormer.

### 6.6. Layer Selection for Embedding
Investigates using bottom-$n$ transformer layers.
*   **Attention Pooling (Frozen backbone):** Performance improves as $n$ increases from 1 to 8, then drops. Intermediate layers yield slightly more robust embeddings than the final layer.
*   **LoRA + Last Token:** Performance declines as $n$ increases. The final layer performs best under LoRA fine-tuning.

### 6.7. ECR Dataset Construction
*   **For Contrastive Training:** Blend in "noise" (imperfect ECRs) to improve robustness against incorrect ECR generation. Explicitly instruct the MLLM to rewrite the answers while performing reasoning. Keep 50% non-ground-truth ECR in the training data to prevent label leakage and overfitting.
*   **For SFT (Reasoner):** Do not ask the MLLM to rewrite the ground-truth, as the goal is for the TTE Reasoner to learn to output the exact ground-truth as accurately as possible.

## 7. All Mathematical Formulas and Symbol Definitions

**Standard UME Embedding:**
$h_q = \text{Pooling}(f_\theta(V_q, [\text{Ins}_q], T_q))$
$h_t = \text{Pooling}(f_\theta(V_t, [\text{Ins}_t], T_t))$
Where $f_\theta$ denotes the MLLM embedder and $\text{Pooling}$ refers to the pooling operation for aggregating MLLM’s hidden states into the final embedding. Following previous works, the last token’s hidden state is used for final embedding.

**InfoNCE Loss:**
$L_{\text{InfoNCE}} = - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \log \frac{\phi(h_q^i, h_t^i)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \phi(h_q^i, h_t^j)}$
$\phi(h_q, h_t) = \exp(\frac{1}{\tau} \cos(h_q, h_t))$
Where $\cos$ denotes the cosine similarity function, and $\tau$ is the temperature hyper-parameter.

**TTE Embedding Formulation:**
$h = \text{Pooling}(f_\theta(V, [\text{Ins}], T, \psi))$
$\psi = g_\omega(V, [\text{Ins}], T)$
Where $g_\omega$ and $\psi$ are the reasoner MLLM and its embedding-centric reasoning trace, respectively. $\langle V, T, [\text{Ins}] \rangle$ represents the multimodal input triplet (optional visual input, textual input, pre-defined task instruction).

**Optimal ECR Formulation:**
$\psi^\star_i \in \text{argmax}_\psi \log \frac{\phi(h_q^i, h_t^i)}{\sum_j \phi(h_q^i, h_t^j)}$
Where $\psi^\star_i$ is the optimal ECR for a batch of $(q, t)$ pairs.

**SFT Loss for Reasoner (TTEs):**
$L_{\text{SFT}}(\omega) = - \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \log p_\omega(\psi_t | V, [\text{Ins}], T, \psi_{t'<t})$
Where $\omega$ represents the parameters of the student reasoner, and the loss maximizes the conditional likelihood of the ECR $\psi$ generated by the teacher.

**Joint Contrastive-Autoregressive Loss (Ablated):**
$L_{\text{joint}} = \lambda L_{\text{InfoNCE}} + L_{\text{SFT}}$
Where $\lambda$ is a hyper-parameter controlling the weight of contrastive (InfoNCE) loss.

## 8. Algorithm Pseudocode

The paper does not provide formal algorithm pseudocode blocks. Instead, it provides explicit "Prompt templates" for generating ECR data using teacher MLLMs, which serve as the algorithmic operational logic for the Reasoner module. These are extracted exactly as follows:

**Prompt Template for VQA Tasks (Without Ground-Truth):**
```text
Given an image and a question, explain step-by-step how the answer can be derived from the image. Please follow the below rules:
- Keep the reasoning concise and grounded in visual or factual evidence. Keep it succinct, within 1-2 sentences.
- Wrap your reasoning in <CoT> and </CoT> tags. Then, on the next line, output the final answer, starting with "Answer:".
- Follow the format in the example below.
---
Example: QUESTION: What is the hairstyle of the blond called?
<CoT>The blonde woman’s hair is tied back into a single bunch, which is characteristic of a ponytail.</CoT>
Answer: pony tail
---
Now given the following image and question:
IMAGE: <image>
QUESTION: {query}
Please follow the same format as the example above, providing your reasoning and final answer.
```

**Prompt Template for VQA Tasks (With Ground-Truth and Rephrasing for Contrastive Training):**
```text
Given an image and a question, explain step-by-step how the answer can be derived from the image. Please follow the below rules:
- Keep the reasoning concise and grounded in visual or factual evidence. Keep it succinct, within 1-2 sentences.
- Wrap your reasoning in <CoT> and </CoT> tags. Then, on the next line, output the final answer, starting with "Answer:".
- You are provided with the ground-truth answer for reference. Use it to verify your reasoning but do not mention it explicitly in your explanation.
- Rephrase the final answer so that it preserves the exact meaning of the original but may differ in wording or phrasing. Do not add, remove, or alter factual content.
- Follow the format in the example below.
---
Example: QUESTION: What is the hairstyle of the blond called?\nANSER:
<CoT>The blonde woman’s hair is tied back into a single bunch, which is characteristic of a tied-back hair (ponytail).</CoT>
Answer: tied-back hair, pigtail, pony tail.
---
Now given the following image and question:
IMAGE: <image>
QUESTION: {query}\nANSWER: {answer}
Please follow the same format as the example above, providing your reasoning and final answer.
```

**Prompt Template for i2t Tasks (COCO-style Caption):**
```text
Given an image, describe briefly how a COCO-style caption can be formed from the image. Follow the instructions below:
- First provide a detailed description of the image, then describe what a COCO-style caption should contain. (Hint: it should focus on the most salient objects and their arrangement in the image.)
- Wrap your reasoning in <CoT>...</CoT> (2–3 sentences).
- Then write the final COCO-style caption on the next line as: Answer: <answer>
---
EXAMPLE
<CoT>The image shows a cozy bedroom with a wooden bed, striped bedsheets, a lamp on the nightstand with its light turned on, and several large pillows arranged neatly from head to foot along the bed. The COCO-style caption should contain the most salient object and arrangement: the pillows.</CoT>
Answer: Several pillows are lined up down the length of a bed.
---
Now answer for the following image: IMAGE: <image>
```

**Prompt Template for t2v Tasks:**
```text
Given a caption describing an everyday scene, use your background knowledge and reasoning to expand the caption with more concrete and visual details. Follow the rules below:
- Generate a succinct reasoning process (2–3 sentences) about the visual information that can be inferred from the caption, using commonsense and visual priors.
- Wrap your reasoning in <CoT>...</CoT>. - Then output the final description of the corresponding image on the next line using: Answer: <answer>
---
EXAMPLE CAPTION: A teddy bear shop is equipped with a door guard teddy and a neighbor teddy above.
<CoT>The caption playfully describes a teddy bear shop, suggesting there are teddy bear figures used decoratively. A “door guard teddy” likely refers to a large bear figure placed at the shop entrance, possibly dressed in a costume to resemble a guard. The “neighbor teddy above” suggests another teddy is positioned at or hanging from an upper-story window, adding to the shop’s whimsical appearance.</CoT>
Answer: A teddy bear shop with a large teddy bear dressed as a royal guard standing at the entrance, and another teddy bear playfully hanging from an upstairs window.
---
Now answer for the following caption:
CAPTION: {query}
```
