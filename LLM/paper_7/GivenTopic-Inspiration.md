Given Topic: The deployment of large language model-based agents in novel interactive environments, such as unseen web platforms or unfamiliar tool APIs, faces severe generalization challenges. Existing methods suffer from a fundamental misalignment between pre-trained knowledge and test-time environmental specifics, manifesting in two distinct failure modes: an inability to correctly parse and generate environment-specific syntactic structures, and a lack of understanding of environment-specific semantic state-transition dynamics. Current solutions either rely on costly annotated demonstrations or require computationally expensive training of separate specialized world models, making rapid deployment impractical. The core scientific issue is how to enable efficient, annotation-free adaptation of agent behavior solely through test-time environmental interaction, simultaneously addressing both surface-level format misalignment and deep causal dynamic misunderstanding.

2. Test-time adaptation paradigms from computer vision, where models adjust to distribution shifts at inference through unsupervised objectives derived from the input data itself.
3. The concept that lightweight parametric biases in latent representation spaces can transiently shift model output distributions toward context-specific patterns without permanent modification.
4. The inadequacy of heavyweight parameterized world models, motivating the representation of environment dynamics through declarative natural language within the model's context window.
5. The principle that diverse, goal-oriented behavioral probing uncovers non-obvious causal interactions more effectively than unstructured state coverage.
6. The treatment of raw environmental interaction histories as autonomous self-supervisory signals, eliminating dependency on expert demonstrations.
7. The fundamental distributional divergence between pre-trained language model corpora and specific interactive environment distributions, necessitating localized alignment.
8. The necessity of ephemeral, episode-specific adaptation mechanisms to prevent cross-task interference when grounding agents in novel environments.
