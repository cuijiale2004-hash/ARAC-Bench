# 1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points
**Research Background:**
Recently, diffusion models have attracted significant attention due to their ability to generate high-quality images through an iterative denoising process. However, the sampling process in diffusion models is computationally expensive, requiring hundreds of denoising steps to generate a high-quality image. Additionally, the noise estimation networks in diffusion models are often complex and have a large number of parameters, which limits diffusion models’ practical applications on resource-constrained devices. To address these challenges, post-training quantization (PTQ) is an attractive approach for compressing diffusion models to speed up the sampling process and reduce memory footprint. PTQ is particularly effective due to its ability to quantize deep neural networks without relying on a large amount of training data or necessitating model retraining. Calibration data plays a crucial role in PTQ for diffusion models and is typically generated from various time steps of the denoising process.

**Existing Pain Points:**
1. **Uniform Sample Weighting is Suboptimal:** Most existing PTQ methods (such as PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, TFMQ-DM) uniformly sample data from various time steps in the denoising process to construct a calibration set and consider calibration samples equally important during the quantization process. However, treating all calibration samples equally may not be optimal. An empirical study comparing uniform sample weighting against multiple random weighting schemes on the CIFAR-10 dataset reveals that among 50 different weighting schemes, 18 outperform uniform weighting in terms of FID score, demonstrating that uniform weighting is suboptimal and that better weighting solutions exist. Certain samples may contribute more critically to model performance than others.
2. **Ignoring Frequency Evolution Characteristics:** One notable property in the denoising process of diffusion models is that low-frequency features are primarily recovered in early stages, while high-frequency features are recovered in later stages of the denoising process. None of the previous works on quantization for diffusion models consider this property to enhance the effectiveness of quantized models. Previous works ignore the fact that the quantization of the noise estimation network should focus on different frequency components at different time steps during the quantization process.

# 2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions
**Core Research Motivation:**
Unlike previous works that treat calibration samples equally and ignore the frequency evolution property, this research is motivated by the principle that each calibration sample could have different contributions to the performance of the quantized model, and the quantization process should adaptively focus on different frequency components at different denoising time steps. By automatically learning the optimal weights for calibration samples and the optimal weighting for frequency loss components, the quantized model can better preserve the behavior and generation quality of the full-precision model.

**Scientific Questions:**
1. How to automatically and principledly assign optimal weights to calibration samples for post-training quantization of diffusion models, moving beyond the suboptimal uniform weighting?
2. How to effectively leverage the frequency evolution property of diffusion models (low-frequency recovery at early stages, high-frequency recovery at later stages) during the quantization process?
3. How to jointly optimize the sample weights and frequency weights in a unified framework to maximize the quantized model's performance on a validation set?

# 3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy
**Overall Core Idea:**
We propose a novel meta-learning approach for post-training quantization (PTQ) of diffusion models that jointly optimizes the contributions of calibration samples and the weighting of frequency components at each time step for quantizing noise estimation networks. Specifically, our approach automatically learns to assign optimal weights to calibration samples while selectively focusing on mimicking specific frequency components of data generated by the full-precision noise estimation network at each denoising time step.

**Design Philosophy:**
1. **Meta-learning for Sample Weighting:** Inspired by meta-learning principles, we propose a sample-weighting mechanism that automatically learns a weight for each calibration sample, with the objective that the quantized model trained with the calibration samples and their corresponding weights can achieve good performance on the validation set.
2. **Meta-learning for Frequency Weighting:** We propose a frequency weighting method that leverages meta-learning to automatically assign weights to the frequency loss components, derived from the frequencies of features extracted by the full-precision and quantized noise estimation networks at each time step, such that the learned frequency weights lead to the minimization of the quantized model’s validation loss.
3. **Frequency-Aware Regularization:** We propose a regularization term on frequency weights to encourage the quantized model to focus more on mimicking high-frequency components and pay less attention to low-frequency components of samples generated by the full-precision model as the time step decreases during the denoising process.
4. **Bi-level Optimization Formulation:** We form the optimization of the sample weights and frequency weights as a bi-level optimization problem. The aim of the optimization is to learn sample weights and frequency weights such that the quantized model obtained from the training using calibration samples with those weights achieve a good performance on the validation set, i.e., minimizing the validation loss.

# 4. Core Innovation Points
1. We propose a novel PTQ method that leverages meta-learning to automatically learn to weight the contribution of each calibration sample in PTQ training for diffusion models. Such a weighting mechanism prioritizes important samples, improving the performance of the quantized model.
2. We propose a meta-learning based method to automatically learn to weight components of the frequency loss.
3. We propose a regularization term to encourage the quantized model to focus more on mimicking high-frequency components and pay less attention to low-frequency components of the data generated from the full-precision counterpart as the time step decreases during the denoising process.
4. We form the optimization of the sample weights and frequency weights as a bi-level optimization problem to ensure the learned weights generalize well to the validation set.
5. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first to leverage meta-learning techniques specifically to post-training quantization within the context of diffusion models, and the first to exploit frequency domain information for the quantization of diffusion models to improve quantized diffusion model performance.

# 5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution
The proposed method consists of three main optimization steps: 
1. Updating frequency weight $\lambda$. 
2. Updating sample weight $\omega$ using the validation loss $L_{val}$. 
3. Leveraging both sample and frequency weights to quantize the model by minimizing the final loss $L_{FINAL}$.

The overall architecture and workflow are as follows: We first obtain the calibration set following the approach outlined in the Q-Diffusion method, by selecting generated samples at fixed step intervals across all denoising time steps. Each calibration sample is denoted as $(x_i, t_i)$. A validation set $S_v$ is also constructed. For the sample weighting mechanism, each calibration sample $(x_i, t_i)$ is assigned a learnable weight $\omega_i$. For the frequency weighting mechanism, we utilize the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to decompose the features of the noise estimation network into low and high-frequency subbands. At each time step $t_i$, a learnable frequency weight vector $\lambda_{t_i}$ is assigned to these subbands. The joint optimization of $\omega$ and $\lambda$ is formulated as a bi-level optimization problem, where the outer loop minimizes the validation loss to update $\omega$ and $\lambda$, and the inner loop updates the quantized model parameters on the calibration set. An alternating optimization scheme is employed where one set of parameters is held fixed while the other is updated. Finally, the obtained weights are used to optimize the quantizer parameters of each block.

# 6. Detailed Module Design
**Calibration and Validation Data Module:**
The calibration dataset $S_c$ is generated by selecting generated images at fixed step intervals across all denoising time steps. Each calibration sample is denoted as $(x_i, t_i)$, where $x_i$ is the generated sample with the corresponding time step $t_i$. A validation dataset $S_v$ is used to compute the validation loss, which can be a subset of generated images or real images with added noise.

**Sample Weighting Module:**
Each calibration sample $(x_i, t_i)$ is assigned a learnable weight $\omega_i$, representing its impact on the quantized model’s performance. The set of $N$ weights corresponding to $N$ calibration samples is denoted as $\omega = \{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^N$. These weights are optimized via meta-learning to prioritize important samples.

**Frequency Weighting and DWT Module:**
The Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) is utilized to decompose tensor inputs into frequency subbands. For any tensor input $a$, it is decomposed into four wavelet subbands by applying DWT (classical Haar wavelet):
$DWT(a) = (a_{ll}, a_{lh}, a_{hl}, a_{hh})$.
Among these, $a_{ll}$ refers to the low-frequency component that reflects the basic object structure, while $a_{\{lh,hl,hh\}}$ represent high-frequency components that capture texture details. When quantizing the $l$th layer of the network, we assign all calibration samples $x_i$ of time step $t_i$ with a set of learnable weights $\{\lambda_{t_i,0}, \lambda_{t_i,1}, \lambda_{t_i,2}, \lambda_{t_i,3}\}$, denoting the weights corresponding to $a_{ll}, a_{lh}, a_{hl}, a_{hh}$ at time step $t_i$. Let us denote $\lambda$ as a learnable frequency weight matrix of size $T \times 4$, where the $t_i$th row $\lambda_{t_i} = \{\lambda_{t_i,0}, \lambda_{t_i,1}, \lambda_{t_i,2}, \lambda_{t_i,3}\}$ is a vector of length 4. We normalize the total weight of all frequency components at each time step $t_i$ equal to 1.

**Bi-level Optimization and Alternating Update Module:**
Both the sample weight $\omega$ and the frequency weight $\lambda$ are optimized to maximize the model’s performance on the validation set $S_v$. Given a full-precision model $\theta_{FP}$ and a quantized model $\theta_Q$, the joint optimization objective is formed as a bi-level optimization problem. 
When optimizing the sample weights $\{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^N$, we keep the frequency weight $\lambda$ fixed, and conversely. The model $\hat{\theta}_Q(\omega)$, when optimizing sample weights $\omega$, is approximated by solving the inner objective using a single step of gradient-based methods (e.g., SGD or Adam). We employ an alternating optimization scheme in which one set of parameters is held fixed while the other is updated using the validation loss.

**Final Quantization Module:**
For the $l$th layer/block, once we have obtained the sample weights $\{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^N$ and the frequency weight matrix $\lambda$ corresponding to that layer/block, the model will be quantized over the training set with a combined loss $L_{FINAL}$.

# 7. All Mathematical Formulas and Symbol Definitions
**Symbol Definitions:**
- $x_i, t_i$: The generated calibration sample and its corresponding time step.
- $\omega = \{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^N$: The set of $N$ learnable sample weights.
- $\lambda$: A learnable frequency weight matrix of size $T \times 4$, where the $t_i$th row $\lambda_{t_i} = \{\lambda_{t_i,0}, \lambda_{t_i,1}, \lambda_{t_i,2}, \lambda_{t_i,3}\}$.
- $a_{ll}, a_{lh}, a_{hl}, a_{hh}$: The four wavelet subbands from DWT.
- $S_c, S_v$: The calibration dataset and validation dataset, respectively; $|S|$ denotes the cardinality of the set $S$.
- $\theta_{FP}, \theta_Q$: The full-precision model and quantized model, respectively.
- $l$: The index of the layer/block that we want to calibrate.
- $\gamma$: A hyperparameter for the frequency loss weight.
- $\beta$: A hyperparameter for the regularization loss weight.
- $\eta_Q, \eta_\alpha, \eta_\lambda$: The learning rate of the quantized model, sample weights, and frequency weight matrix, respectively.
- $\oslash$: Denotes element-wise division.

**Mathematical Formulas:**
1. Discrete Wavelet Transform:
$DWT(a) = (a_{ll}, a_{lh}, a_{hl}, a_{hh})$ (1)

2. Frequency weight normalization constraint:
$\sum_{k=0}^3 \lambda_{t_i,k} = 1$

3. Bi-level optimization outer objective:
$\omega^*, \lambda^* = \arg\min_{\omega,\lambda} \frac{1}{|S_v|} \sum_{x_j \in S_v} L_{val}(\hat{\theta}_Q, x_j, \lambda)$ (2)
s.t.: $\hat{\theta}_Q = \arg\min_{\theta_Q} \sum_{x_i \in S_c} \omega_i [L_Q(\theta_Q, x_i, l) + \gamma L_F(\theta_Q, x_i, \lambda, l)]$ (3)

4. Quantization loss $L_Q$:
$L_Q(\theta_Q, x_i, l) = \|\epsilon^{(l)}_{FP}(x_i, t_i) - \epsilon^{(l)}_Q(x_i, t_i)\|^2$ (4)
where $\epsilon^{(l)}_{FP}(x_i, t_i)$ and $\epsilon^{(l)}_Q(x_i, t_i)$ are the outputs of the $l$th block of the full-precision model $\theta_{FP}$ and the quantized model $\theta_Q$ for sample $(x_i, t_i)$, respectively.

5. Weighted frequency difference $L_f$:
$L_f(a, b, \hat{\lambda}) = \hat{\lambda}_0\|a_{ll} - b_{ll}\|^2 + \hat{\lambda}_1\|a_{lh} - b_{lh}\|^2 + \hat{\lambda}_2\|a_{hl} - b_{hl}\|^2 + \hat{\lambda}_3\|a_{hh} - b_{hh}\|^2$ (5)

6. Frequency loss $L_F$:
$L_F(\theta_Q, x_i, \lambda, l) = L_f(\epsilon^{(l)}_Q(x_i, t_i), \epsilon^{(l)}_{FP}(x_i, t_i), \lambda_{t_i})$ (6)

7. Validation loss $L_{val}$:
$L_{val}(\hat{\theta}_Q, x_j, \lambda) = \|\epsilon_{FP}(x_j, t_j) - \epsilon_Q(x_j, t_j)\|^2 + \beta L_{Reg}(\lambda)$ (7)
where $\epsilon$ with a subscript is the final output of the corresponding model of interest.

8. Regularization loss $L_{Reg}$:
$L_{Reg}(\lambda) = \sum_{t=0}^{T-2} \max(0, r_t - r_{t+1})$ (8)
where $r = \lambda_{:,0} \oslash (\lambda_{:,1} + \lambda_{:,2} + \lambda_{:,3})$. As the time step $t$ decreases, the regularization loss $L_{Reg}(\lambda)$ will encourage $\lambda_{t,0}$ to decrease while $\lambda_{t,1} + \lambda_{t,2} + \lambda_{t,3}$ to increase.

9. Single-step approximation for $\hat{\theta}_Q$:
$\hat{\theta}_Q = \theta_Q - \eta_Q \sum_{x_i \in S_c} \nabla_{\theta_Q} \omega_i [L_Q(\theta_Q, x_i, l) + \gamma L_F(\theta_Q, x_i, \lambda, l)]$ (9)

10. Sample weight update rule:
$\omega_i = \omega_i - \eta_\alpha \frac{1}{|S_v|} \sum_{x_j \in S_v} \frac{\partial L_{val}(\hat{\theta}_Q(\omega), x_j, \lambda)}{\partial \omega_i}, \forall i = 1, 2, \ldots, N$ (10)

11. Frequency weight update rule:
$\lambda_{t,k} = \lambda_{t,k} - \eta_\lambda \frac{1}{|S_v|} \sum_{x_j \in S_v} \frac{\partial L_{val}(\hat{\theta}_Q(\lambda), x_j, \lambda)}{\partial \lambda_{t,k}}, \forall t \in \{1, \ldots, T\}, k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ (11)

12. Final optimization objective $L_{FINAL}$:
$L_{FINAL} = \sum_{i=1}^N \omega_i[L_Q(\theta_Q, x_i, l) + \gamma L_F(\theta_Q, x_i, \lambda, l)]$ (12)

13. Forward diffusion process (for real validation set):
$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$ (13)
where $x_0$ is the original clean image, $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{i=1}^t \alpha_i$ represents the cumulative product of noise schedule coefficients, and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is random Gaussian noise.

14. Approximated final sample (alternative frequency loss):
$\hat{x} = (x_i - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t_i}}\epsilon_\theta(x_i, t_i))/\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t_i}}$ (14)

# 8. Algorithm Pseudocode

```algorithm
Algorithm 1 Sample and frequency meta weighting for post-training quantization of diffusion models
1: procedure TRAIN(θ_FP, S_c, S_v)
2:  ▷ θ_FP: full-precision model ◁
3:  ▷ θ_Q: quantized model ◁
4:  ▷ L: number of blocks in the full-precision model ◁
5:  ▷ S_c: calibration dataset ◁
6:  ▷ S_v: validation dataset ◁
7:  ▷ N_f : number of iterations for updating frequency weight λ ◁
8:  ▷ N_s: number of iterations for updating sample weights {ω_i}_{i=1}^N ◁
9:  ▷ N_Q: number of iterations for model weight quantization ◁
10: Uniformly initialize sample weights ω = {ω_i}_{i=1}^N and frequency weight λ
11: Initialize the quantized model θ_Q
12: for l = 1 to L do
13:   while not converged do
14:     ▷ Fix {ω_i}_{i=1}^N and update frequency weight λ ◁
15:     for n_f = 1 to N_f do
16:       Compute θ̂_Q(λ) using Eq. (9)
17:       Compute L_val(θ̂_Q(λ), S_v, λ) using Eq. (7)
18:       Update λ: λ← ADAM(L_val(θ̂_Q(λ), S_v, λ))
19:     ▷ Fix λ and update sample weights {ω_i}_{i=1}^N ◁
20:     for n_s = 1 to N_s do
21:       Compute θ̂_Q(ω) using Eq. (9)
22:       Compute L_val(θ̂_Q(ω), S_v, λ) using Eq. (7)
23:       Update {ω_i}_{i=1}^N: {ω_i}_{i=1}^N ← ADAM(L_val(θ̂_Q(ω), S_v, λ))
24:   ▷ Optimize parameters of the quantized model ◁
25:   for n_q = 1 to N_Q do
26:     Optimize the quantizer parameters of the lth block in model θ_Q by minimizing L_FINAL from Eq. (12) over S_c using weights {ω_i}_{i=1}^N and λ.
27: return quantized model θ_Q
```
