Given Topic: Deep reinforcement learning relies on bootstrapped target computation for value function estimation. The predominant approach employs periodically updated target networks to ensure training stability; however, this introduces significant learning delays due to reliance on outdated estimates. Conversely, utilizing the rapidly changing online network for target computation accelerates credit assignment yet precipitates severe instability and overestimation bias, manifesting as uncontrolled value escalation and divergence. The fundamental challenge lies in reconciling the stability afforded by lagged targets with the sample-efficiency benefits of fresh online estimates. The core scientific issue is how to construct a bootstrapping mechanism that leverages up-to-date value information without succumbing to the moving-target problem and associated estimation biases.

Given Inspiration:
1. The fundamental trade-off in temporal-difference learning between optimization stability and information freshness: periodically lagged target networks suppress divergence at the cost of outdated supervision, while fully online bootstrapping accelerates learning but provokes severe instability.
2. The observation that value estimates derived from rapidly updated networks tend to exhibit systematic positive bias, indicating that naive integration of fresh estimates into regression targets is insufficient without bias mitigation.
3. The conjecture that recently updated and historically lagged network parameters occupy complementary niches in the learning dynamics, such that their joint exploitation can outperform either extreme in isolation.
4. The robust estimation perspective that pessimistic value consolidation—favoring conservative predictions over optimistic ones—can curtail uncontrolled value escalation during iterative policy improvement.
5. Empirical patterns showing that target-free learning intervals can be rendered stable when fresh estimates are restrained by slower-evolving references, motivating conditional fusion strategies.
6. The feedback-loop pathology inherent in self-referential function approximation, where immediate predictions chase their own transient fluctuations unless moderated by an auxiliary stabilizing signal.
