Given Topic:
Research field: Interpretability and decomposition of aligned multimodal representation spaces. Pain points: Current methods for decomposing neural network embeddings into sparse, interpretable components fail to preserve cross-modal alignment. When applied to jointly trained multimodal embeddings, existing approaches tend to learn modality-specific feature dictionaries, wherein semantically equivalent inputs from different modalities activate disjoint sets of latent features. This fragmentation undermines cross-modal transfer, interpretability, and control. Core problem: How to learn a unified dictionary of concepts that is simultaneously sparse, interpretable, and aligned across modalities, ensuring that semantically similar inputs—regardless of modality—activate shared feature dimensions while maintaining reconstruction fidelity.

Given Inspiration:
1. The Linear Representation Hypothesis, which suggests neural network embeddings can be understood as linear combinations of high-level semantic features, providing a foundational premise for linear concept decomposition across modalities.
2. The observed fragmentation of learned feature dictionaries in multimodal embedding spaces, wherein semantically aligned inputs from different modalities activate disjoint latent features, revealing a critical misalignment between reconstruction objectives and cross-modal semantic sharing.
3. A geometric and algebraic insight that any modality-fragmented decomposition of an aligned embedding space necessarily admits an alternative decomposition with strictly improved cross-modal alignment, implying that modality-split behavior is a trainable bias rather than an inherent property of aligned representations.
4. Classical structured sparsity principles from high-dimensional statistics and multi-task learning, particularly the use of mixed norms to enforce joint support across grouped variables, suggesting that paired cross-modal signals should share common sparse activation patterns.
5. The implicit bias of reconstruction-driven dictionary learning toward unimodal feature specialization, highlighting the need for additional regularization mechanisms that explicitly enforce shared concept activation across modalities.
