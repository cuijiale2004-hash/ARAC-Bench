Given Topic: Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems aim to ground language model outputs in retrieved evidence, yet models frequently produce content that contradicts or exceeds the provided sources, undermining trustworthiness. Existing methods to detect such faithfulness failures are hindered by substantial requirements for annotated training data, high inference costs and prompt sensitivity of external model judges, or limited accuracy due to the distributed, polysemantic nature of raw internal neural activations. This motivates the development of lightweight, training-efficient detection mechanisms capable of isolating semantically coherent hallucination-specific signals directly from the internal representations of language models, thereby enabling accurate identification and transparent explanation of unfaithful outputs without reliance on extensive supervision or auxiliary large-scale models.

Given Inspiration:
1. Mechanistic interpretability research indicating that high-dimensional neural activations can be decomposed into sparse, semantically meaningful features via unsupervised dictionary learning.
2. The hypothesis that language models internally encode latent knowledge regarding factual consistency that is not reliably expressed in generated text or surface-level uncertainty metrics.
3. Information-theoretic principles for quantifying the discriminative relevance of individual disentangled representational components toward specific behavioral phenomena.
4. Transparent, additive predictive structures from interpretable machine learning that decompose decisions into contributions of individual features to enable both localized attribution and global model auditing.
