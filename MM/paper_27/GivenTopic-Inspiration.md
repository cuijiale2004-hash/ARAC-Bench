Given Topic: Unified multimodal systems exhibit a persistent internal capability imbalance between visual generation and understanding, where discriminative proficiency typically exceeds generative fidelity. Existing mitigation strategies predominantly rely on external evaluators or supplementary annotated datasets, lacking autonomous mechanisms that exploit internal consistency for self-directed refinement. Furthermore, systematic quantification of such capability misalignment across diverse tasks remains inadequate, and the potential for synergistic interplay between both modalities during post-training is theoretically underexplored. This work investigates how inherent internal disparities can be leveraged to achieve self-improving alignment without extrinsic supervision, while illuminating the coupled optimization dynamics underlying joint capability enhancement.

Given Inspiration:
1. Intrinsic capability imbalance: The observation that unified multimodal systems exhibit consistent internal asymmetry between generation and understanding capabilities, suggesting the stronger branch can guide the weaker without extrinsic supervision.
2. Self-evaluation paradigm: The principle that a model's internal discriminative capacity can serve as an autonomous quality assessor for its own generative outputs, replacing external reward functions.
3. Shared representational dynamics: Mathematical perspectives indicating that distinct but coupled tasks operating over common parameter spaces tend to exhibit aligned optimization trajectories and consistent geometric update patterns.
4. Co-evolutionary optimization: The hypothesis that generation and understanding are not independent optimization objectives but coupled processes whose joint dynamics may yield mutual enhancement during post-training.
5. Curriculum learning by capability progression: The educational principle that learners benefit from progressively harder material, applied here by leveraging iterative capability gains to unlock previously inaccessible training examples.
6. Rejection set recycling: The engineering insight that initially discarded data samples, rather than being permanently abandoned, may become valuable training resources as model competence progressively advances.
