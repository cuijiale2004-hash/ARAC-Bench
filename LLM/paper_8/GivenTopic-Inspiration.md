Given Topic: Large language model agents demonstrate promising capabilities in leveraging pre-trained knowledge for complex decision-making tasks, yet they remain constrained in hard-exploration problems characterized by vast state-action spaces, deceptive local optima, and sparse environmental feedback. Existing approaches predominantly rely on localized trial-and-error mechanisms that inadequately accumulate long-term structured knowledge from exploration histories, resulting in suboptimal state selection and inefficient exploration policies. Meanwhile, reinforcement learning-based methods achieve stronger performance but require prohibitively large numbers of environment interactions, rendering them sample-inefficient. A critical scientific challenge lies in developing a principled framework that enables agents to simultaneously maintain global experiential knowledge for strategic state selection and refine local exploration behaviors from sparse feedback, without relying on handcrafted heuristics or high-variance value estimation. Addressing this requires reconciling long-term memory accumulation with fine-grained local policy adaptation in computationally efficient ways.
