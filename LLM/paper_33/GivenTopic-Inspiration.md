Given Topic: The research addresses knowledge grounding in large language models through the integration of external information retrieval. The field currently faces a fundamental tension between sparse retrieval strategies, which risk insufficient information coverage, and dense long-context processing, which suffers from token inefficiency, positional degradation, and heightened susceptibility to distracting passages. Existing retrieval methods largely rely on static relevance rankings and lack explicit mechanisms to account for the combinatorial interference among retrieved passages or the varying long-context robustness of different language models. The core scientific challenge is to develop an adaptive retrieval paradigm that dynamically balances information coverage against distraction, tailored to the capacity of the target model, without requiring exhaustive textual alignment or combinatorial search over passage subsets.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical observation from prior literature that simply increasing the volume of retrieved context eventually degrades model performance due to distraction, suggesting a non-linear coverage-distraction trade-off.
2. The cognitive interference phenomenon where individually informative passages can mutually obscure or mislead when jointly presented, implying that passage interactions are as critical as individual relevance.
3. The distributional structure of semantic similarity scores between queries and passages, which may encode latent cues about the spatial distribution of useful information across ranked candidates.
4. The heterogeneity in long-context processing capabilities and noise resilience across different language models, indicating that retrieval policies should be model-specific rather than universal.
5. Principles from hierarchical decision-making and action abstraction, where compressing high-dimensional discrete selection spaces into smooth, low-dimensional control spaces can enhance learning efficiency and generalization.
6. The possibility of optimizing retrieval decisions solely through the statistical patterns of embedding similarities, avoiding the computational burden of cross-encoding raw text during the selection phase.
