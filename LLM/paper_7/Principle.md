**Principle 1: Operational Failure-Mode Taxonomy Justifying Distinct Adaptation Pathways for LLM Agents in Novel Environments**

**Definition:**
In test-time adaptation (TTA) for LLM agents, a common paper structure decomposes deployment failures into categories (e.g., syntactic mismatch of observation/action formats vs. semantic mismatch of state-transition dynamics) and then proposes one targeted method per category. Reviewers must rigorously assess whether such a taxonomy is *operationally meaningful* or merely an artificial construct designed to justify packaging two methods into one paper. A high-quality work acknowledges that all failure modes may share a common root cause (e.g., lack of environment-specific prior knowledge) while justifying the decomposition by demonstrating that different failure modes demand *fundamentally different adaptation mechanisms*—for instance, parametric gradient-based updates for distributional/syntactic alignment versus in-context explicit rules for causal/semantic dynamics. The taxonomy should function as a practical decision framework for selecting adaptation mechanisms, not as a rigid theoretical claim. Empirical support must include concrete failure examples for each category across diverse environments, and results should show differential effectiveness consistent with the taxonomy (e.g., dynamics grounding yielding 2%→23% on complex multi-site tasks but small gains on simple sites). This principle proved decisive in this review cycle: the taxonomy critique was a primary driver of the initial score of 4, and reframing it as an operational lens—together with redesigned figures showing end-to-end flows with a persistent running example—directly converted the review to acceptance. This criterion distinguishes principled multi-mechanism designs from "taxonomy inflation," where one problem is split merely to claim multiple contributions.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Root-cause honesty**: Does the paper acknowledge the shared underlying cause of all failure modes while providing a defensible operational reason for separation, rather than presenting an arbitrary dichotomy?
- **Mechanism-failure correspondence**: Is there a principled argument for why failure mode X requires mechanism type Y (gradients vs. context, parametric vs. non-parametric), beyond the mere fact that the authors built one method each?
- **Empirical instantiation**: Are concrete, reproducible examples of each failure mode provided, and do per-environment results confirm that each method helps most where its targeted failure mode dominates?
- **Communication adequacy**: Do figures and narrative convey complete end-to-end flows with running examples that ground abstract definitions, rather than partial pipelines that obscure the method's insight?

**Principle 2: Constraint-Driven Differentiation of Repurposed Mechanisms from Steering Vectors, Trained World Models, and Trajectory Memory in Agentic TTA**

**Definition:**
Test-time adaptation for LLM agents inevitably borrows machinery from established research areas—activation steering/bias vectors, learned world models, and retrieval/workflow memory. Novelty in this subfield typically lies not in inventing wholly new mechanisms but in repurposing existing ones under the unique constraints of agentic deployment: no annotated trajectories, online streaming adaptation, episodic task boundaries, and permission for unsupervised pre-task interaction. Reviewers must therefore demand explicit, dimension-by-dimension contrast with the closest precedents, covering: adaptation target (local environment syntax vs. global behavioral traits), optimization paradigm (online self-supervised gradient descent vs. offline precomputed static vectors), lifecycle (episodic reset preventing cross-task interference vs. global application), knowledge source (active pre-task exploration vs. mining previously successful trajectories, enabling cold-start), and cost profile (in-context rules vs. training a separate dynamics model). The decisive question is whether the repurposing solves a problem the prior paradigm structurally could not—such as adapting to cold-start environments where no successful trajectories exist, or per-episode adaptation without interference. Superficial renaming of steering vectors or RAG should be penalized, while genuine constraint-driven redesign should be rewarded. In this review cycle, the lack of such contextualization was the central weakness raised by one reviewer, and the authors' explicit three-axis contrast with steering-vector literature (target, optimization, lifecycle) was instrumental in resolving it.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Dimension-by-dimension contrast**: Does the paper explicitly compare against nearest neighbors (steering vectors, trained world models like WMA, trajectory memory like AWM) along target, optimization, lifecycle, and data-requirement axes?
- **Constraint-motivated design**: Is each design choice traceable to a clearly formalized deployment constraint (annotation-free, online, episodic, interaction-permitted)?
- **Behavioral evidence of difference**: Is there evidence the repurposed mechanism behaves differently in practice (e.g., episodic reset prevents interference; a single gradient step suffices)?
- **Direct empirical comparison**: Where feasible, is the method compared against the closest prior approach under that approach's intended setting with controlled backbones?

**Principle 3: Cross-Family, Cross-Scale Generalization and Capability-Dependent Gain Characterization in Test-Time LLM Agent Adaptation**

**Definition:**
Scalability and generalizability were the central concerns flagged by the area chair, and a TTA method validated only within a single model family (e.g., Qwen2.5) risks being an artifact of that family's pretraining distribution rather than a general technique. High-quality work in this subfield must evaluate across multiple model families (open and closed) and scales (e.g., 7B–32B+), and—critically—must characterize *how* adaptation effectiveness depends on base-model capability. In-context dynamics grounding presupposes sufficient instruction-following ability to exploit extracted rules, and single-step parametric updates presuppose sufficient model capacity to be steerable; consequently, finding that gains scale with capability (e.g., +5% absolute on Gemma-3-27B but marginal or negative changes on 8B models) is a scientifically valuable boundary result rather than a weakness, provided it is honestly reported and used to scope the claims. This principle also extends to environment-side generalization: gains should be stratified by environment complexity, and the paper should explain where benefits concentrate (e.g., large gains where dynamics are unpredictable, negligible or negative effects where dynamics align with common-sense priors and longer context may even hurt). Reviewers should verify that claims are scoped to the regimes where the method demonstrably works, rather than overgeneralized from a favorable setting.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Model diversity**: Are at least three model families and multiple scales evaluated for the parametric method, and both open and closed models for prompting-based methods?
- **Capability-dependence analysis**: Does the paper analyze *why* smaller or weaker models fail to benefit, rather than silently omitting unfavorable results?
- **Environment-stratified results**: Are per-site/per-domain breakdowns provided, with explanation of where gains concentrate and why (e.g., complex multi-site vs. simple single-site environments)?
- **Hyperparameter robustness across scales**: Is the method shown to be robust across reasonable hyperparameter ranges, with principled analysis of scale-dependent settings (e.g., learning rate vs. hidden dimension)?

**Principle 4: Exploration Coverage, Amortized Cost Accounting, and Explorability Boundaries for Dynamics-Grounding World Models in LLM Agents**

**Definition:**
Deployment-time, exploration-based grounding—persona-driven probing, transition logging, and consolidation of filtered natural-language rules into an in-context world model—stands or falls on the quality and coverage of its exploration phase. Reviewers must require explicit coverage mechanisms, such as diversity drivers (personas or goals synthesized from environment descriptions) and novelty-seeking heuristics (avoiding already-observed state transitions), plus a consolidation stage that removes trivial or duplicate rules. Crucially, coverage must be empirically validated rather than assumed: for example, analyzing exploration logs to demonstrate that distinct personas discover disjoint environment functionality, ablating the number of exploration episodes, ablating the filtering step, and testing whether self-exploration matches stronger-model exploration (self-improvement robustness). Cost accounting must be transparent and correctly amortized: the upfront exploration cost (e.g., ~7M tokens per website) is incurred once and amortized across all subsequent tasks, while online parametric adaptation carries a per-step latency (~3% for a single gradient step, validated even at 20k+ token contexts)—and both should be benchmarked against baseline pipelines (e.g., WMA's 870 synthesized tasks, separate model training, and ~140s/step simulation overhead). Finally, applicability boundaries must be honestly stated: the method presumes explorable environments with explicit state transitions, so purely conversational or user-in-the-loop settings are legitimately out of scope and should be marked as such rather than silently excluded.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Coverage mechanisms and evidence**: Are diversity (personas/goals) and novelty heuristics specified, with log-level evidence that exploration covers disjoint environment features?
- **Exploration ablations**: Are the exploration budget, filtering stage, and choice of exploration/extraction backbone (self vs. stronger model) systematically ablated?
- **Transparent cost profile**: Are upfront amortized costs and per-step latency reported and compared against the full cost structure of competing pipelines?
- **Stated boundary conditions**: Does the paper explicitly acknowledge non-explorable or transition-free environments as limits, with appropriate N/A handling in evaluation tables?

**Principle 5: Applicability-Aware Baseline Fairness, Evaluation Stability, and Honest Interference Analysis for Combined Adaptation Strategies in LLM Agents**

**Definition:**
In this subfield, baselines and proposed methods often have heterogeneous applicability: world-model baselines may require web-specific training data and thus cannot run on conversational or function-calling benchmarks, while exploration-based methods cannot operate where no explicit state transitions exist. High-quality evaluation must explicitly mark and mechanistically justify N/A cells—rather than leaving blanks that invite suspicion of unfair comparison—and must isolate method contributions by controlling backbones (e.g., using the same GPT-4o-mini as the WMA baseline so gains are attributable to the method, not the model). Reviewers should also scrutinize benchmark selection and evaluation stability: deterministic, state-based evaluators (WebArena's string matching, BFCLv3's state checks) are preferable to high-variance LLM-judged settings, and where stochastic simulators are unavoidable (e.g., Tau-Bench's LLM-simulated user), variance-reduction protocols (temperature-0, Best-of-N sampling with self-critique, multi-seed averaging) are expected. Finally, when multiple complementary strategies are proposed, their combination must be evaluated; if naive combination underperforms due to interference between implicit gradient signals and explicit contextual rules (e.g., contextual overloading, signal conflict), this must be reported and mechanistically diagnosed rather than hidden, with principled integration (e.g., a meta-controller for gating) identified as future work. Treating non-synergy as a scientific finding strengthens credibility; cherry-picking only positive results is a red flag that should lower confidence in all reported gains.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Justified N/A handling**: Are inapplicable baseline/method cells explicitly marked with mechanistic reasons (no state transitions, non-transferable training pipeline), not left blank?
- **Backbone-controlled comparison**: Are comparisons against strong baselines conducted under identical backbone models to isolate methodological gains?
- **Evaluation stability**: Are deterministic metrics preferred, and are variance-reduction protocols plus multi-seed reporting used for stochastic benchmarks?
- **Mandatory combination analysis**: Is the hybrid of proposed strategies evaluated, and—if synergy fails—is the interference mechanistically analyzed (qualitative failure breakdown) instead of omitted?
