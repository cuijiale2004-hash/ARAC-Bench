## Principle 1: Theoretical Justification and Mechanistic Coherence of Embedding Generative Diffusion Processes into Discriminative Set-Prediction Detection Frameworks

**Definition:**
When a paper proposes to integrate denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)—originally designed for high-dimensional generative tasks such as image synthesis—into a fundamentally discriminative, set-prediction detection framework like DETR, the authors must provide rigorous theoretical and mechanistic justification for why this integration is principled rather than superficial. Specifically, the work must clearly articulate how the diffusion forward/reverse process maps onto the detection task's unique structure: unordered object sets, bipartite matching objectives, and low-dimensional reference point spaces. Reviewers should evaluate whether the paper distinguishes between using diffusion as a mere training auxiliary (analogous to DN-DETR's denoising queries) versus a genuine generative formulation that produces query anchors from noise at inference time. The mathematical formulation must be internally consistent: the noise distribution, the denoising parameterization, and the loss function must align coherently. A critical question is whether the model learns a meaningful conditional distribution over object locations or merely exploits the denoising objective as a regularizer. The paper should also clarify the relationship between its diffusion formulation and existing denoising-based training strategies (e.g., DN-DETR's contrastive denoising), demonstrating what is fundamentally new beyond incremental extension.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Formulation Coherence**: Is the mapping between the diffusion process (forward noising, reverse denoising) and the detection task (reference point generation, query initialization) mathematically precise and internally consistent? Are all symbols and operations unambiguously defined without conflicting notations?
- **Distinction from Existing Denoising Strategies**: Does the work clearly differentiate its diffusion-based query generation from prior denoising auxiliary tasks (e.g., DN-DETR, DINO's CDN)? Is the novelty at the level of mechanism (generative sampling at inference) rather than merely at the level of training signal augmentation?
- **Loss Function Alignment**: Is it clearly stated whether the method uses a diffusion-specific loss (e.g., simplified denoising objective) or retains the standard detection loss (classification + regression with Hungarian matching)? If the latter, is there a theoretical argument for why the standard loss suffices to supervise the denoising process?
- **Low-Dimensional Diffusion Justification**: Since reference points are 4-dimensional (vs. thousands of dimensions in image diffusion), does the paper provide theoretical or empirical justification for reducing the number of timesteps (e.g., T=100 instead of T=1000)? Is the connection between data dimensionality and required diffusion steps properly cited and reasoned?

## Principle 2: Inference Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off Analysis and Principled Selection of Iterative Denoising Steps in Lightweight Diffusion-Based Detection

**Definition:**
A defining characteristic of applying diffusion models to object detection—unlike image generation—is the severe latency constraint imposed by real-time or near-real-time deployment requirements. Therefore, any paper proposing diffusion-based detection must provide a thorough, quantitative analysis of the accuracy-efficiency trade-off as a function of the number of decoder evaluations (denoising steps) at inference time. Reviewers must assess whether the paper provides a principled rule or empirical protocol for selecting the optimal number of iterative steps, rather than simply reporting a single "best" configuration. The computational overhead analysis should be granular: reporting FLOPs, activation memory, and wall-clock time for each configuration, and comparing these against both the baseline and competing methods. A critical concern is whether performance degrades non-monotonically with additional steps (indicating over-denoising or false detection accumulation), and whether the paper acknowledges and explains this phenomenon. The paper should also demonstrate that the method provides meaningful gains even at minimal computational cost (e.g., single decoder evaluation), establishing a favorable lower bound for practical deployment.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Granular Compute-Accuracy Profiling**: Does the paper report FLOPs, activations, and ideally inference time for each decoder evaluation setting? Is the comparison fair and comprehensive, including both the proposed method at various steps and baseline methods?
- **Principled Step Selection**: Is there a clear, reproducible criterion for choosing the number of denoising steps (e.g., validation-based selection, diminishing returns analysis, or automated scheduling)? Or is the choice ad hoc?
- **Non-Monotonic Performance Explanation**: If performance peaks at an intermediate number of steps and degrades beyond that, does the paper provide a mechanistic explanation (e.g., duplicated detections, over-refinement, distribution mismatch)?
- **Zero-Overhead Baseline Comparison**: Does the method demonstrate improvement over the baseline even at the minimum computational cost (single decoder evaluation, identical FLOPs)? This establishes that the diffusion formulation itself—not merely additional compute—contributes to performance gains.
- **Comparison with Alternative Efficient Samplers**: Are different sampling strategies (DDIM, DDPM, progressive distillation) compared in terms of their AP-FLOPs trade-off? Is the choice of sampler justified?

## Principle 3: Robustness Verification of Stochastic Diffusion-Based Query Initialization Across Random Seeds, Scene Densities, and Dataset Distributions

**Definition:**
Because diffusion-based detection introduces stochasticity at inference time (sampling initial reference points from a prior distribution), reviewers must rigorously evaluate whether the method's performance is stable and reproducible across different random initializations. This is particularly critical because detection performance in crowded scenes—where multiple objects overlap and spatial ambiguity is high—may be disproportionately affected by initialization noise. The paper must provide multi-seed experiments reporting mean and standard deviation of AP and related metrics, ideally across multiple decoder evaluation settings. Furthermore, robustness should be evaluated not only on the full dataset but also on stratified subsets reflecting different scene complexities (sparse vs. dense, few vs. many objects). The analysis should extend beyond a single dataset to demonstrate that robustness holds across different data distributions (e.g., COCO vs. LVIS with long-tail categories vs. V3DET with extreme vocabulary). A failure to demonstrate initialization robustness would undermine the practical viability of the stochastic generation approach.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Multi-Seed Variance Reporting**: Does the paper report mean ± standard deviation across at least 3–5 random seeds? Is the variance acceptably small (e.g., < ±0.3 AP) relative to the claimed improvement margin?
- **Scene Complexity Stratification**: Are robustness results reported separately for sparse scenes (few objects) and dense/crowded scenes (many overlapping objects)? Does the method maintain stability in both regimes?
- **Cross-Dataset Robustness**: Is initialization robustness verified on datasets with fundamentally different characteristics (e.g., long-tail distributions in LVIS, extreme vocabulary in V3DET)? Or is robustness only demonstrated on COCO?
- **Interaction Between Seed Variance and Decoder Evaluations**: Does the paper analyze whether increasing decoder evaluations reduces or increases sensitivity to initialization noise? This reveals whether additional denoising steps serve as a stabilizing mechanism.
- **Per-Category or Per-Scale Analysis**: Are there any categories or object scales where initialization sensitivity is notably higher? This identifies potential failure modes of the stochastic approach.

## Principle 4: Comprehensiveness, Recency, and Fairness of Baseline Comparisons Across Multiple Detection Paradigms and Evolving State-of-the-Art

**Definition:**
In the rapidly evolving field of DETR-based object detection, where new variants are published at a high cadence (multiple per year at top venues), reviewers must assess whether the paper's experimental comparisons are sufficiently comprehensive, up-to-date, and fair. A paper published at a top venue should compare against not only the direct architectural ancestors (e.g., Deformable DETR, DINO) but also recent contemporaneous methods that represent the current state-of-the-art. The choice of baselines must be justified: if only a subset of methods is compared on certain datasets, the reason must be clearly stated (e.g., lack of standard benchmarks, computational resource constraints). Reviewers should also evaluate whether the comparison is fair in terms of training schedule, backbone, augmentation, and evaluation protocol. The inclusion of both discriminative DETR variants and other generative approaches (e.g., DiffusionDet, Pix2Seq) provides a more complete picture of where the proposed method sits in the landscape. Additionally, the paper should acknowledge the limitations of its comparison scope and not overclaim superiority based on incomplete evidence.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Recency of Compared Methods**: Does the paper include methods published within the last 1–2 years? Are the most recent DETR variants (e.g., those from the current or previous year's top conferences) included in the comparison?
- **Paradigm Diversity**: Are comparisons made against both discriminative DETR variants and generative detection approaches (autoregressive, diffusion-based)? This contextualizes the contribution within the broader detection landscape.
- **Training Protocol Fairness**: Are all compared methods trained under equivalent conditions (same backbone, same number of epochs, same augmentation)? If results are taken from original papers, are the training conditions comparable?
- **Dataset Coverage Justification**: If the paper evaluates on multiple datasets but limits baseline comparisons on some (e.g., only comparing against DINO on LVIS/V3DET), is this limitation clearly justified? Are there plans or acknowledgments for broader comparisons?
- **Adaptability Demonstration**: Does the paper demonstrate that its approach can be applied to multiple base architectures (e.g., Deformable DETR, DINO, Align-DETR)? This shows generality rather than overfitting to a single framework.

## Principle 5: Systematic Ablation of Diffusion Process Design Choices and Their Interaction with Detection-Specific Components

**Definition:**
The integration of diffusion models into detection introduces multiple new design dimensions that do not exist in standard DETR training: noise distribution type, noise scheduler, number of diffusion timesteps, number of inference sampling steps, interaction with existing components (e.g., contrastive denoising queries in DINO, two-stage proposals), and the choice of deterministic vs. stochastic sampling. Reviewers must evaluate whether the paper conducts a systematic and thorough ablation study that isolates the contribution of each design choice and reveals potential interactions between them. The ablation should go beyond simply reporting "which option works best" and provide interpretive insight into why certain choices are superior. For instance, why does Gaussian noise outperform Beta or Sigmoid Gaussian in this context? Why does the cosine scheduler work best? The paper should also ablate the interaction between the diffusion process and existing detection components (e.g., does diffusion-based initialization render DN-DETR-style denoising queries redundant? Does it complement or conflict with two-stage proposals?). Without such analysis, it is impossible to determine whether the gains come from the diffusion mechanism itself or from incidental hyperparameter advantages.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Completeness of Design Space Exploration**: Are all major diffusion-related hyperparameters ablated (noise distribution, scheduler, number of timesteps, number of queries, sampling strategy)? Are the results presented in a structured, comparable format?
- **Interpretive Depth**: Does the paper provide mechanistic explanations for why certain choices work better? For example, is the superiority of cosine scheduling linked to its signal retention properties at different timesteps? Is the Gaussian noise advantage explained in terms of the reference point space geometry?
- **Interaction Analysis**: Does the paper study how the diffusion process interacts with existing detection components (CDNs, two-stage initialization, EMA)? Are there synergies or redundancies identified?
- **Scalability of Ablation Findings**: Do the ablation conclusions hold across different backbones, datasets, and model scales? Or are they specific to a single configuration?
