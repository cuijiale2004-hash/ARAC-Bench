Given Topic: The field of neural network weight space generative modeling aims to learn the underlying distribution of trained parameters to enable rapid model sampling. Current approaches face critical scalability limitations due to the high dimensionality of modern architectures and inherent functional symmetries, such as permutation invariances, which fragment the loss landscape. Existing methods frequently compromise by generating only partial weight sets for large models, or they incur prohibitive sampling costs and require post-generation fine-tuning to recover functionality. Furthermore, reliance on auxiliary nonlinear dimensionality reduction models introduces substantial computational overhead and risks information loss. The core scientific challenge is to develop an efficient, scalable generative framework capable of producing complete, high-performance parameter configurations across diverse architectures and data modalities without task-specific conditioning or subsequent parameter updates, while robustly accommodating the symmetries and dimensionality of deep weight spaces.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical observation that neural networks trained on similar tasks exhibit statistical regularities in their parameter distributions, suggesting that weight space can be treated as a structured, high-dimensional data modality amenable to direct generative modeling.
2. The degeneracy of loss landscapes induced by architectural permutation symmetries, which motivates aligning parameter configurations into canonical equivalence classes prior to learning the generative distribution.
3. The computational bottleneck of iterative, diffusion-based sampling in high-dimensional spaces, which inspires the use of continuous flow-based frameworks that directly regress transport vector fields for efficient single-pass generation.
4. The prohibitive cost of training auxiliary nonlinear dimensionality reduction networks for large parameter spaces, motivating the use of simple linear subspace projections to compress weight vectors while preserving distributional structure.
5. The functional sensitivity of certain internal feature statistics to the training data distribution, which necessitates a post-generation recalibration mechanism to ensure immediate deployability without parameter fine-tuning.
6. The practical demand for rapid ensemble construction and cross-domain transfer learning, which requires a generative framework to produce complete, task-ready parameter sets rather than partial weight checkpoints.
