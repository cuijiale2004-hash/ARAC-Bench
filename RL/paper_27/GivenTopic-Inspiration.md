Given Topic:
Offline reinforcement learning via return-conditioned supervised learning has gained attention for its stability and generative capabilities. However, formulating reinforcement learning purely as conditional sequence modeling introduces a fundamental misalignment: the objective of reproducing training trajectories inherently conflicts with the goal of discovering novel policies that outperform any single trajectory in the offline dataset. Existing approaches tend to overfit to historical sub-trajectories, impairing the ability to stitch together optimal segments from multiple experiences and limiting performance beyond the behavioral policy. Moreover, conditioning on target returns alone provides an unreliable signal for optimality. The core challenge lies in developing a paradigm that balances the utilization of long-term temporal context with local, Markovian decision-making signals, thereby improving generalization and trajectory stitching without brittle dataset-specific tuning.
