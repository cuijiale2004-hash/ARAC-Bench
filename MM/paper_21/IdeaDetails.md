1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points
In recent years, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved significant progress by coupling pre-trained vision encoders and language models. A prevailing architectural paradigm involves augmenting a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) with visual capabilities using a pre-trained Vision Encoder (VE), typically a Vision Transformer (ViT). The VE partitions an image into patches and encodes them into visual tokens. A lightweight adapter module then projects these visual tokens into the LLM’s word embedding space to bridge the modality gap. Despite their powerful capabilities, emerging research has revealed inherent limitations in MLLMs, such as struggling with fine-grained visual details and unfocused cross-modal attention where visual tokens receive less focus than textual counterparts. 

A fundamental pain point is rooted in the ubiquitous Pre-Norm architectural design. In this paradigm, normalization is applied before the main computational block, leaving the residual path unaltered. While this ensures smooth gradient flow, the variance and L2 norm of the hidden states tend to accumulate and grow with network depth. This creates a severe norm disparity in MLLMs where high-norm visual tokens (generated by a deep, Pre-Norm ViT) and lower-norm text tokens are processed together within a shared Pre-Norm LLM backbone. This imbalance induces an "asymmetric update dynamic," where high-norm visual tokens exhibit "representational inertia," causing them to transform semantically much slower than their textual counterparts, fundamentally impairing effective cross-modal feature fusion and leading to visual information loss.

2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions
The core research motivation is to understand and resolve the fundamental architectural bottleneck in Pre-Norm MLLMs that causes visual information loss. The existing norm disparity between visual and text tokens is not merely a static issue but a catalyst for accelerated geometric divergence, suppressing cross-modal attention signals. 

The scientific questions guiding this research are:
RQ1: Do visual and text tokens exhibit a significant norm mismatch at the modality interface?
RQ2: Does the projection adapter harmonize the initial norm disparity before the tokens enter the LLM backbone?
RQ3: Do visual and textual hidden states exhibit different update rates, as predicted by the theory of asymmetric angular velocity?
RQ4: Does the significant norm disparity persist in architectures utilizing probabilistic visual tokenization?
RQ5: How can we enforce norm alignment without triggering vanishing gradients caused by minute gain initialization?

3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy
The overall core idea is that the Pre-Norm design inherently creates a destructive dynamic imbalance in MLLMs. By injecting features from a deep, pre-trained vision encoder (where norms have already accumulated) into the relatively low-norm embedding space of an LLM, a severe initial L2 norm disparity is established exactly at the cross-modal interface. This causes high "representational inertia" in visual tokens, leading to a slower semantic evolution compared to text tokens, which accelerates geometric divergence and collapses the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in attention mechanisms.

The design philosophy bridges theory and practice: first, formally proving the asymmetric update dynamic and SNR collapse through rigorous mathematical derivation; second, empirically validating that these norm disparities and asymmetric update rates are prevalent across mainstream open-source VL models; and finally, proposing a targeted architectural intervention—Gradient-Aware Norm Alignment. This intervention enforces strict norm alignment via a single LayerNorm layer with careful initialization, coupled with a Global Weight Compensation (GWC) mechanism to resolve the optimization dilemma caused by extreme norm compression, ensuring effective learning while decoupling forward norm compression from backward gradient magnitude.

4. Core Innovation Points
1. Theoretical Identification of Asymmetric Dynamics: The paper is the first to identify and theoretically formalize the issue of cross-modal norm disparity in Pre-Norm MLLMs. The analysis reveals an "asymmetric update dynamic" where high-norm visual tokens exhibit "representational inertia," leading to a slower semantic evolution compared to text tokens.
2. Formal Proof of SNR Collapse via Geometric Divergence: The paper provides a rigorous four-step theoretical framework (Kinematics, Dynamics, Optimization, and SNR Collapse) proving that the norm-induced geometric lag strictly reduces the maximum extractable attention score. It mathematically demonstrates that for any fixed capacity of the projection layers, the norm-induced geometric lag strictly reduces the maximum extractable attention score, collapsing the Signal-to-Noise Ratio.
3. Extensive Empirical Validation of Dynamic Imbalance: The paper provides extensive empirical validation across a suite of mainstream open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen-2.5-VL, KimiVL, GLM-4.1V, Ovis 2.5), demonstrating that the predicted norm disparities and asymmetric update rates exist. It proves that models with severe norm imbalance demonstrate a significantly larger gap in their update dynamics, and reveals that discrete tokenization paradigms inherently mitigate this representational inertia.
4. Gradient-Aware Norm Alignment with Global Weight Compensation: The paper proposes a remarkably simple yet effective solution—inserting a single, carefully initialized LayerNorm layer after the visual projector. To resolve the vanishing gradient problem triggered by minute initialization, a novel Global Weight Compensation (GWC) mechanism is introduced. This technique decouples the forward norm compression from the backward gradient magnitude, yielding significant performance gains on both multimodal and text-only benchmarks.

5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution
The overall technical solution enforces norm alignment between visual and text tokens by inserting an additional LayerNorm layer immediately after the visual projector. The learnable gain parameter $g$ of this layer is initialized to explicitly match the average L2 norm of the text tokens within the LLM’s embedding space ($g_{init} = T/\sqrt{D}$). Because pre-trained text embeddings exhibit an extremely small magnitude, aligning to this target requires initializing $g$ to a minute value, which naively triggers a vanishing gradient problem. To resolve this optimization dilemma, a Global Weight Compensation (GWC) mechanism is implemented via a backward hook. Instead of redefining the gradient simply as identity, the gradients are actively rescaled to counteract the dampening effect of the small initialization. In the backward pass, the gradient flowing back to the vision encoder is multiplied by the inverse of the global weight magnitude, effectively canceling out the scaling term and restoring the gradient flow to a unit scale while maintaining precise architectural norm alignment in the forward pass.

6. Detailed Module Design
1. Pre-Norm Residual Update Mechanism: The Transformer block refines representations via an additive update $h^{(l+1)} = h^{(l)} + \Delta h^{(l)}$. In the Pre-Norm design, normalization is applied within the residual branch, leaving the skip connection unaltered: $\Delta h^{(l)} = \text{Sublayer}(\text{Norm}(h^{(l)}))$, $h^{(l+1)} = h^{(l)} + \Delta h^{(l)}$. Because the residual output $h^{(l+1)}$ bypasses normalization, the variance and L2 norm monotonically accumulate with depth.
2. Asymmetric Angular Velocity and Geometric Divergence: The update vector $\Delta h$ is decomposed into a parallel and an orthogonal component. The effective angular velocity is driven solely by the orthogonal component. Because a uniform update magnitude $C^{(l)}$ is applied to hidden states of different norms, high-norm vision tokens exhibit a lower effective angular velocity than low-norm text tokens, imparting higher "representational inertia" to visual tokens. This asymmetry leads to an accelerated geometric divergence.
3. Signal Degradation and Attention Collapse: The geometric divergence prevents the semantic alignment of related tokens. The "representational inertia" creates a mechanical barrier where low-norm text tokens rapidly update their orientation, but high-norm visual tokens update too slowly, resulting in persistent angular misalignment for semantically related pairs. The dot product signal for a relevant pair is fundamentally capped, causing a collapse in the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) and unfocused attention maps.
4. Gradient-Aware Norm Alignment Layer (GlobalWeightCompensatedLayerNorm):
   - Forward Pass: Computes mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ across the feature dimension. The input is normalized and transformed by affine parameters (gain $g$ and bias $\beta$). $g$ is initialized to a small scalar value derived from the target text norm $T$, while $\beta$ is initialized to zero.
   - Backward Gradient Compensation: Registers a backward hook on the normalized tensor $\hat{x}$. Computes the mean absolute value of the gain parameter vector $g$ to obtain a global scaling scalar $\mu_g$. Clamps the scalar with a minimal threshold $\delta = 10^{-3}$ to ensure numerical stability. Scales the gradient $\nabla_{\hat{x}} \mathcal{L}$ flowing backwards through the normalization operation by the inverse of this scalar, decoupling the forward scale from the backward gradient scale.

7. All Mathematical Formulas and Symbol Definitions
- Pre-Norm residual update:
  $h^{(l+1)} = h^{(l)} + F(\text{Norm}(h^{(l)}))$
- Layer Normalization (where $g$ and $\beta$ are learnable gain and bias):
  $\text{LayerNorm}(x) = \frac{x - \mathbb{E}[x]}{\sqrt{\text{Var}[x] + \epsilon}} \odot g + \beta$
- RMS Normalization:
  $\text{RMSNorm}(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{D}\|x\|_2^2 + \epsilon}} \odot g$
- Tangent of effective angular velocity (where $C^{(l)}$ is uniform update magnitude, $\phi$ is consistent expected angle, $h$ is hidden state):
  $\tan(\theta_{eff}) = \frac{C^{(l)} \sin(\phi)}{\|h\|_2 + C^{(l)} \cos(\phi)}$
- Asymmetry condition (where $\|h_{vis}\|_2 > \|h_{txt}\|_2$):
  $\tan(\theta_{eff, vis}) < \tan(\theta_{eff, txt})$
- Attention score inequality for imbalanced ($imb$) vs balanced ($bal$) cases:
  $\mathbb{E}[S^{(imb)}_{rel}] < \mathbb{E}[S^{(bal)}_{rel}]$
- Target L2 norm $T$ calculation (where $W_e$ is text embedding matrix, $W^*$ are non-zero vectors):
  $T = \frac{1}{|W^*|} \sum_{w \in W^*} \|w\|_2$, where $W^* = \{w \in W_e \mid \|w\|_2 > \epsilon\}$
- Initialization of gain parameter (where $D$ is dimension):
  $g_{init} = T/\sqrt{D}$
- Global Weight Compensation backward pass (where $\bar{g}$ is mean magnitude of gain vector):
  $\text{Backward}(\nabla_{\hat{x}} \mathcal{L}) = \underbrace{(\nabla_y \mathcal{L} \odot g)}_{\text{Standard Gradient}} \times \underbrace{\frac{1}{\bar{g}}}_{\text{Compensation Factor}}$
- Recursive Decay Theorem (where $\Theta^{(l)}$ is angle between visual and text hidden states at layer $l$, $\gamma^{(l)}_{eff}$ is retention factor):
  $\mathbb{E}[\cos(\Theta^{(l+1)}) \mid \Theta^{(l)}] = \gamma^{(l)}_{eff} \cdot \cos(\Theta^{(l)})$
- Retention factor definition:
  $\gamma^{(l)}_{eff} = \cos(\theta^{(l)}_{vis}) \cos(\theta^{(l)}_{txt})$
- Lag Angle definition (accumulated geometric deficit):
  $\Delta \phi = \Theta_{imb} - \Theta_{bal} > 0$
- Orthogonal decomposition of imbalanced visual vector (where $v_{signal}$ is aligned with text vector, $v_\perp$ is orthogonal):
  $v_{imb} = \cos(\Delta \phi) v_{signal} + \sin(\Delta \phi) v_\perp$
- Spectral Degradation of covariance matrix:
  $C_{imb} \approx \cos(\Delta \phi) C_{bal}$
- Fundamental Spectral Upper Bound (where $R$ is magnitude bound of projection matrix, $\sigma_i$ are singular values):
  $\max_{\|W\|_F \leq R} \mathbb{E}[S^{imb}_{rel}] \leq R \sum \sigma_i(C_{imb}) \approx \cos(\Delta \phi) \left( R \sum \sigma_i(C_{bal}) \right)$
- Signal-to-Noise Ratio Gap definition:
  $Gap_{imb} = \mathbb{E}[S^{imb}_{rel}] - \mathbb{E}[S^{imb}_{irrel}]$
- Forward LayerNorm mean and variance:
  $\mu = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^D x_i, \quad \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^D (x_i - \mu)^2$
- Forward normalization and affine transformation:
  $\hat{x} = \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}}, \quad y = \hat{x} \odot g + \beta$
- Backward Global Gain Aggregation:
  $\mu_g = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^D |g_i|$
- Backward Safety Clamping (where $\delta = 10^{-3}$):
  $\mu_{safe} = \max(\mu_g, \delta)$
- Backward Compensation Factor Application:
  $\nabla_{\hat{x}} \mathcal{L}_{scaled} = \nabla_{\hat{x}} \mathcal{L} \times \frac{1}{\mu_{safe}}$
