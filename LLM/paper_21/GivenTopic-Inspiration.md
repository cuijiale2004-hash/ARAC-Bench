Given Topic: Machine unlearning for large language models aims to selectively remove undesirable knowledge while preserving general capabilities. Current mainstream paradigms predominantly rely on untargeted optimization strategies that suppress the likelihood of undesired training samples. This leads to three critical deficiencies: catastrophic degradation of unrelated capabilities, incomplete erasure of underlying knowledge that generalizes beyond literal training instances, and the generation of incoherent or degenerate outputs lacking meaningful semantic structure. The fundamental scientific issue is the absence of explicit guidance regarding what knowledge should be removed—specifically, distinguishing in-scope semantic equivalents from out-of-scope data—and how the model should behave after unlearning. Consequently, existing methods exhibit unpredictable loss-of-control, failing to achieve reliable scope-aware knowledge removal. The core challenge is to establish a principled mechanism that enables the model to recognize implicit membership within an unlearning scope and to produce coherent, explainable behavioral responses, thereby ensuring controllable and trustworthy knowledge elimination without compromising retained competencies.

Given Inspiration:
1. The realization that existing unlearning approaches suffer from a fundamental loss-of-control stemming from the simultaneous absence of explicit scope specification and post-unlearning behavioral guidance.
2. The observation that suppressing sample likelihood alone induces unpredictable side effects, including nonsensical outputs and collateral capability degradation, because the optimization lacks targeted behavioral objectives.
3. The insight that structured reasoning traces inherently expose the underlying logic and conceptual boundaries of knowledge domains, suggesting they can serve as semantic anchors to delineate what should be forgotten.
4. The understanding that undesirable knowledge manifests across linguistic variations and implicit reformulations, necessitating a scope-aware formulation that transcends literal sample matching.
5. The perspective that coherent refusal behavior must be explicitly prescribed rather than left to emerge chaotically, drawing on the capacity for structured, explainable justifications to guide post-unlearning responses.
6. The intuition that generalization across adversarial and cross-lingual reformulations can be achieved by internalizing conceptual rationales for refusal rather than memorizing superficial rejection templates.
7. The principle that effective unlearning requires harmonizing the thorough erasure of undesired knowledge with the acquisition of targeted behavioral norms to prevent collateral damage to unrelated competencies.
