Given Topic: The field of reinforcement learning for language model post-training currently faces significant challenges when applied to unstructured, real-world reasoning tasks. While existing approaches achieve strong performance in domains with automatically verifiable outcomes, they struggle in settings where correctness is nuanced, multi-dimensional, and lacks binary ground truth. Mainstream methods relying on preference-based reward models often suffer from overfitting to superficial response characteristics and impose substantial annotation burdens. Furthermore, structured evaluation instruments developed for expert domains have been confined to offline assessment rather than serving as training signals. The core scientific issue is how to construct reliable, interpretable, and scalable reward mechanisms for on-policy reinforcement learning in subjective, open-ended reasoning domains where multiple criteria of varying importance must be simultaneously satisfied.

Given Inspiration:
1. The limitations of existing reinforcement learning paradigms in handling tasks without verifiable correctness, where binary reward signals are insufficient for nuanced reasoning.
2. The prevalence of structured rubrics in expert-domain evaluation as a mechanism to articulate multifaceted, importance-weighted quality criteria.
3. The insight that evaluation instruments can be repurposed beyond static scoring to serve as dynamic, reusable reward functions during policy optimization.
4. The recognition that decomposing response quality into independent, self-contained criteria mitigates the opacity and instability of holistic preference models.
5. The observation that instance-specific contextual requirements and failure modes demand prompt-adaptive evaluation standards rather than one-size-fits-all reward formulations.
6. The principle that grounding reward signals in expert-aligned reference knowledge can reduce reliance on costly pairwise preference annotations while improving interpretability.
