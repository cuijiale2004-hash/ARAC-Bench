Given Topic: Graph representation learning fundamentally requires isomorphism invariance for generalization. Current mainstream approaches face a dichotomy: message-passing architectures enforce invariance architecturally but suffer from bounded expressivity, oversmoothing, and oversquashing; canonicalization-based methods circumvent these issues by mapping graphs to unique representatives, yet existing approaches rely on flattening graphs into single sequential representations. This sequence-based reduction inherently distorts the underlying metric structure—stretching and contracting shortest-path distances—and restricts the model's discriminative capacity to that of the initial node labeling function. The core scientific problem is to devise an invariant graph representation paradigm that transcends the expressivity and geometric fidelity limitations of sequence-based canonicalization, particularly for sparse graph domains where structural fidelity is paramount.

Given Inspiration:
1. The geometric observation that embedding graph metrics into linear sequential spaces inherently distorts shortest-path distances, whereas embedding into hierarchical acyclic metric spaces better preserves relational proximity.
2. The representational insight that a single canonical representative necessarily loses structural information, while an ensemble of complementary representatives can collectively recover the full graph topology.
3. The structural principle that sparse graphs admit efficient decomposition into a small number of overlapping spanning structures that jointly cover the original edge set.
4. The symmetry-theoretic perspective that probabilistic invariance can be achieved through canonicalization with randomized tie-breaking among structurally equivalent elements.
5. The algorithmic insight that iterative refinement of canonical representatives to prioritize previously uncovered elements yields progressively more complete structural coverage.
6. The inductive-bias consideration that bottom-up hierarchical aggregation aligns more naturally with acyclic graph propagation than with linear sequential processing.
7. The pragmatic motivation to balance the computational intractability of exact graph canonicalization against the limited power of inexpensive structural node descriptors.
