Given Topic: Reasoning segmentation aims to generate pixel-level masks by interpreting complex, context-dependent textual queries, representing a significant challenge in multi-modal perception and logical reasoning. Existing paradigms based on supervised fine-tuning of multi-modal large language models often encounter catastrophic forgetting of general reasoning abilities and exhibit poor robustness when transferred to out-of-domain data. Concurrently, alternative reinforcement learning strategies frequently decouple the language model from the segmentation foundation model during training, or fail to leverage the latter's native capacity for iterative interactive refinement, resulting in suboptimal prompts and constrained exploration spaces. Furthermore, prior attempts to mimic human annotation behavior rely on externally generated initial inputs and lack mechanisms for adaptive termination. The core scientific issue addressed is how to unify initial localization, iterative quality-aware refinement, and autonomous termination within a single reasoning-driven segmentation workflow, thereby improving generalization while preserving foundational multi-modal competencies.

Given Inspiration:
1. Human annotator interaction patterns: experienced users interact with interactive segmentation systems through iterative cycles of initial region indication, visual assessment of generated masks, targeted local corrections, and self-determined termination upon reaching satisfactory quality.
2. Limitations of existing training paradigms: supervised fine-tuning tends to erode the generalist reasoning capabilities of pre-trained multi-modal models and limits cross-domain transferability, whereas standard reinforcement learning approaches often fail to tightly integrate the segmentation foundation model's feedback loop.
3. Cognitive decomposition of the task: the overall objective naturally separates into two synergistic competencies—linguistic grounding for spatial localization and visual mask comprehension for quality assessment and error localization—which must be coherently developed within a unified agent.
4. Process-oriented optimization: treating segmentation as a sequential decision process in which intermediate masks serve as states and refinement signals as actions enables the derivation of dense, step-wise supervisory signals rather than relying solely on final outcome rewards.
5. Diversity-driven stabilization: ensuring diverse action coverage during policy gradient updates prevents degenerate gradient estimation and encourages robust exploration across spatial reasoning modalities, including both localization and refinement behaviors.
6. Controlled corruption for error perception: systematically introducing synthetic false positives and false negatives into perfect masks during training can explicitly cultivate the model's sensitivity to segmentation flaws and its ability to identify corresponding correction strategies.
7. Multi-faceted reward composition: effective learning signals should concurrently reflect spatial accuracy, incremental mask improvement, correct procedural decisions regarding continuation or cessation, and adherence to structured response formats.
