Given Topic: Automated evaluation of large language model outputs via learned judges has become indispensable for scalable model development. Dominant approaches rely predominantly on intrinsic text-based reasoning, which exhibits fundamental limitations in precise computation, symbolic verification, and complex constraint satisfaction. While external tools have been employed during inference, prevailing methods lack deep coupling between reasoning processes and execution capabilities during training, leading to constrained generalization across heterogeneous domains and evaluation protocols. The core scientific problem is to establish training paradigms that endow evaluative agents with autonomous tool-use competencies, enabling verifiable and accurate judgments across both objectively verifiable and qualitatively nuanced tasks without necessitating supervised distillation from superior teachers.

Given Inspiration:
1. The demonstrated efficacy of tool-integrated reasoning in mathematical and algorithmic domains indicates that grounding evaluative inference in executable verification can overcome the inherent unreliability of purely textual reasoning chains.
2. The persistent disconnect between inference-time tool augmentation and training-time policy optimization suggests that evaluative capabilities require end-to-end reinforcement of reasoning jointly with execution feedback.
3. Empirical observations that reinforcement learning can spontaneously incentivize extended cognitive processing imply that analogous mechanisms may elicit autonomous, strategic tool invocation behaviors.
4. The heterogeneity of evaluation scenarios, spanning deterministic constraints and open-ended qualitative criteria, motivates the necessity of adaptive judgment strategies rather than monolithic reasoning approaches.
5. Evidence that iterative policy refinement through self-generated trajectories enables capability bootstrapping indicates that sophisticated agentic behaviors can emerge independently of distilled supervision.
6. The susceptibility of models to overfit on literal execution outputs highlights the need to learn abstract tool-use competencies rather than memorize specific environmental feedback strings.
7. Principles from sandboxed computation demonstrate that controlled execution environments can safely provide ground-truth signals for complex evaluative decisions without exposing underlying system vulnerabilities.
