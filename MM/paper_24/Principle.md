**Principle 1: Denoising Mechanism and Pseudo-Label Reliability for Self-Distilled Attention in MLLMs**

**Definition:**  
In self-distillation frameworks that leverage internal attention maps as supervisory signals, the quality of pseudo-labels directly determines the efficacy of the learned module. MLLM attention maps are inherently noisy due to architectural artifacts such as sink tokens, semantic ambiguity between foreground and background, and task-dependent sparsity. A principled evaluation must assess whether the denoising pipeline is systematically designed, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated across diverse visual and textual inputs. This includes examining the rationale for threshold selection, explicit characterization of noise sources, and the robustness of label assignment strategies that ignore ambiguous regions. Without rigorous validation, methods risk building on unstable supervisory signals that collapse under distribution shift or complex queries.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the work identify and categorize specific noise sources (e.g., sink tokens, ambiguous foreground/background signals, incomplete activation) with empirical evidence?
- Are confidence thresholds and hyperparameters rigorously ablated across multiple datasets, or are they selected arbitrarily?
- Is there theoretical or statistical analysis linking raw attention scores to true localization accuracy?
- Does the pseudo-label quality remain robust across varying input complexities such as cluttered scenes, dense text, and abstract reasoning queries?

**Principle 2: Generalization Capability Across Diverse MLLM Architectures and Backbone Scales**

**Definition:**  
Methods proposing plug-in perception modules for MLLMs must demonstrate that their improvements are not artifacts of a specific base model or an outdated architecture. The field evolves rapidly, with significant capability jumps between model generations, so a rigorous evaluation requires validating the method across multiple architectural families and parameter scales. This ensures that observed gains persist or magnify with stronger backbones rather than merely repairing idiosyncratic weaknesses of legacy models. Such cross-architecture validation is essential for establishing the method as a general contribution rather than a narrow, model-specific hack.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- Are experiments conducted on multiple distinct MLLM families beyond a single baseline architecture?
- Does the method demonstrate consistent or improved relative gains when integrated with recent, state-of-the-art backbones?
- Is performance improvement maintained across different scales within the same model family?
- Are architectural adaptations and implementation details for each backbone clearly documented to ensure reproducibility and fair comparison?

**Principle 3: Wall-Clock Latency and Memory Efficiency Validation for ROI-Based Inference**

**Definition:**  
Claims of computational efficiency in high-resolution perception must be substantiated with concrete deployment metrics rather than asymptotic arguments or token-count reductions alone. For region-of-interest-based or two-stage methods, the overhead of region identification, feature extraction, and augmented generation must be dissected and benchmarked against both native high-resolution scaling and external specialist models. Reviewers must evaluate whether the method achieves a favorable accuracy-throughput trade-off under realistic hardware constraints and whether efficiency holds across varying input resolutions. Vague assertions such as "lightweight" are insufficient without measured breakdowns of each pipeline stage.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the paper report end-to-end wall-clock latency, GPU memory consumption, and throughput on standard hardware?
- Are efficiency comparisons performed against relevant baselines including external grounding models and native dynamic-resolution scaling?
- Is the performance-throughput Pareto frontier analyzed across varying input resolutions or visual token counts?
- Are claims such as "lightweight" precisely quantified with measured overheads for each pipeline stage?

**Principle 4: Comparative Justification Against External Supervision and Global Context Integrity**

**Definition:**  
When proposing annotation-free or self-distilled approaches, authors must rigorously justify why internal signals surpass external alternatives such as ground-truth bounding boxes, large-scale synthetic datasets generated by teacher models, or off-the-shelf grounding models. Furthermore, two-stage perception methods that crop regions of interest risk discarding global spatial relationships critical for multi-turn reasoning and holistic understanding. Evaluation must therefore assess whether the method preserves essential global context while introducing fine-grained detail, and explicitly characterize the conditions under which localized processing causes degradation relative to full-image baselines.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the work provide direct empirical comparisons showing self-distilled labels outperform ground-truth boxes or synthetic teacher-data on downstream tasks?
- Are failure modes systematically analyzed where context truncation or localization errors cause the method to underperform full-image baselines?
- Is there empirical evidence or ablation demonstrating that global visual context is retained and accessible alongside high-resolution region crops?
- Does the method compare favorably against external specialist grounding models in both accuracy and wall-clock efficiency?

**Principle 5: Fair Benchmarking and Methodological Rigor in High-Resolution Perception Evaluation**

**Definition:**  
High-resolution perception methods must be evaluated against appropriate state-of-the-art competitors under controlled conditions that account for confounding factors such as base model capability, training data scale, input resolution, and auxiliary tool usage. Discrepancies in underlying model capacity or pre-training data can create illusory performance gaps that misattribute base-model strengths to the proposed module. A rigorous evaluation contextualizes absolute scores against these variables and spans both text-rich OCR and vision-centric benchmarks to confirm broad applicability rather than narrow task specialization. Unseen benchmarks should be included to validate true generalization.

**Core Evaluation Criteria:**
- Are comparisons made with contemporary high-resolution methods using matched base LLMs and comparable training data regimes?
- Does the evaluation span both document/OCR and vision-centric high-resolution benchmarks to demonstrate general fine-grained perception?
- Are confounding factors such as native dynamic resolution support, supervised fine-tuning data overlap, or OCR token injection explicitly acknowledged and controlled?
- Is the method evaluated on benchmarks unseen during training to validate generalization beyond the distillation distribution?
