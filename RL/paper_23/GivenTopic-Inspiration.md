Given Topic: The research field concerns the development of autonomous AI agents for complex, long-horizon interactive environments such as web navigation, computer control, and mobile device operation. While contemporary systems demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities in structured domains, they exhibit substantial degradation when facing multi-step tasks that require faithful anticipation of environment dynamics. Existing paradigms either rely on reactive decision-making without prospective state evaluation, or depend on external search heuristics and synthetic data generated by powerful teacher models, which introduces prohibitive inference overhead and risks compounding prediction errors. The core scientific problem is how to ground an agent's reasoning process in accurate, experience-based world simulation, enabling it to internally forecast action consequences and evaluate alternative futures without requiring separate modular components or costly combinatorial search during deployment.

Given Inspiration:
1. Human cognitive neuroscience research on "vicarious trial and error," which posits that advanced decision-making emerges from the capacity to mentally simulate hypothetical future scenarios, evaluate their consequences internally, and select actions prior to physical execution.
2. Empirical evidence from interactive task domains indicating that an agent's ability to successfully complete long-horizon missions strongly correlates with the accuracy of its internal state predictions, suggesting that world modeling constitutes a fundamental performance bottleneck.
3. Reinforcement learning perspectives on the rich information contained in real environment trajectories, suggesting that interaction data can simultaneously inform both an agent's policy and its predictive understanding of state-transition dynamics.
4. Observed deficiencies in distillation-based training paradigms that rely on powerful but opaque teacher models to synthesize reasoning traces, where such synthetic simulations may diverge from actual environment physics and embed systematic biases.
5. Phenomena from sequential decision-making domains indicating that expert-level agents implicitly maintain compressed internal representations of world dynamics to support flexible look-ahead planning without exhaustive physical interaction.
