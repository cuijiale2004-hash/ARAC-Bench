Given Topic: The deployment of multimodal intelligent systems as general-purpose verifiers for open-ended agent trajectories represents a critical frontier in artificial intelligence, spanning interactive environments such as web navigation, desktop automation, and robotic manipulation. Despite their extensive world knowledge and reasoning capabilities, current multimodal evaluators exhibit a systematic tendency to over-validate agent behavior, particularly when assessing long-horizon, multimodal trajectories lacking clear-cut success criteria. This pervasive leniency bias persists across diverse architectural families and test-time scaling strategies, undermining the reliability of downstream applications including autonomous self-improvement, online policy supervision, and trajectory curation. Existing mitigation techniques—ranging from chain-of-thought prompting to ensemble aggregation and reasoning-oriented inference—fail to resolve the fundamental bottleneck in extracting and applying impartial judgment. Consequently, the field faces the core challenge of designing verification mechanisms that can faithfully distinguish flawed from correct behavior while leveraging the inherent knowledge and alignment properties of multimodal models.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that multimodal evaluators encode strong, human-aligned priors regarding desired task completion strategies, yet these priors remain inaccessible during standard trajectory-conditioned evaluation due to knowledge extraction bottlenecks.
2. The deficiency in prevailing paradigms where evaluation conditioned on the agent's execution trace induces confirmation dynamics, causing evaluators to rationalize observed behaviors rather than critically assess them against independent standards.
3. The principle of decoupling broad domain knowledge retrieval from specific trajectory critique, motivated by the hypothesis that model-generated task priors can serve as an impartial reference frame for verification.
4. The recognition that alignment and pretraining dynamics may conflate truthfulness with superficial satisfaction, suggesting that verification quality depends critically on how information is presented rather than merely on model capacity or reasoning budget.
5. The insight that leveraging a model's internal sampling distribution for independent knowledge elicitation prior to assessment can yield more balanced evaluative distributions without relying on external calibration or auxiliary tools.
