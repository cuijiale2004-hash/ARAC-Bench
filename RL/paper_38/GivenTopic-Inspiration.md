Given Topic: The integration of multi-agent collaboration mechanisms with reinforcement learning training represents a critical direction for advancing autonomous large language model agents. Current multi-agent systems primarily rely on static prompt engineering with frozen models, while existing reinforcement learning frameworks are predominantly designed for single-agent scenarios assuming homogeneous interaction patterns. This creates a fundamental mismatch: multi-agent environments introduce contextual heterogeneity across agent roles and sequential turns, rendering conventional training paradigms that depend on identical prompt groupings statistically invalid. Furthermore, prevailing training infrastructures lack support for concurrent multi-policy rollouts and workflow-based interactions, preventing collaborative agents from acquiring specialized, coordinated behaviors through online policy optimization. Addressing this gap requires reconciling multi-turn, multi-agent interaction dynamics with principled advantage estimation, while establishing whether collaborative policies should be shared or differentiated based on task characteristics.

1. The complementary nature of multi-agent role-based orchestration and environment-driven policy optimization suggests that integrating collaborative workflows with interactive training can transcend the limitations of static prompting and isolated agent learning.

Policy optimization methods that estimate advantages through grouped sample baselines assume contextual homogeneity within comparison sets, yet sequential multi-agent interactions inherently generate divergent contexts across roles and turns, necessitating stratified grouping principles based on agent identity and temporal position.

5. Multi-agent decision processes exhibit a branching structure where each interaction turn creates divergent historical trajectories, implying that valid policy comparisons must account for state-dependent tree-like expansion rather than parallel trajectory aggregation.
6. Monolithic single-policy training infrastructures are inadequate for heterogeneous collaborative ecosystems, motivating architecturally decoupled resource pools that preserve on-policy constraints for concurrent multi-policy updates.
7. Long-horizon collaborative tasks benefit from concentrating exploration on coordination-critical transitions by leveraging immediate verification signals to guide sequential agent interactions.
