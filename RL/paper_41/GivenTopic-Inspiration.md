Given Topic: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has emerged as a dominant paradigm for enhancing reasoning capabilities in language models. However, current training approaches predominantly rely on direct policy rollouts, resulting in sparse exploration patterns and premature convergence to high-probability yet low-diversity solution regions. Consequently, performance plateaus emerge after prolonged training, where additional computational investment yields diminishing returns. A fundamental limitation is the disconnect between systematic exploration mechanisms and the training loop: structured search is typically relegated to inference, while training lacks principled coverage of the reasoning space. This raises the critical scientific challenge of how to embed systematic, fine-grained exploration into the training process to achieve sustained performance gains without relying exclusively on scaling training duration or model parameters.

Given Inspiration:
1. The performance plateau observed under prolonged training suggests the bottleneck lies in exploration breadth rather than optimization depth, motivating a reorientation from scaling training duration to enriching training-time exploration.
2. Systematic search algorithms traditionally deployed at inference time indicate that structured, stepwise exploration of reasoning space can be transferred into the training loop to uncover critical paths missed by direct sampling.
3. Sequential decision-making theory suggests that maintaining a global perspective over the entire search space, rather than following greedy local trajectories, prevents premature collapse into narrow solution modes.
4. The observation that incorrect yet high-confidence predictions expose systematic reasoning flaws rather than stochastic errors suggests prioritizing such regions for targeted supervision.
5. Fine-grained credit assignment along multi-step reasoning chains, rather than sparse terminal feedback, is essential for discriminating useful intermediate patterns from detrimental ones.
6. Curriculum-style learning principles imply that dynamically concentrating computational resources on challenging unsolved problems, while preserving acquired knowledge, improves sample efficiency.
