Given Topic: Research in reinforcement learning for large language models with extended reasoning capabilities faces a critical efficiency bottleneck during the rollout phase, where autoregressive decoding and memory bandwidth constraints consume the majority of training time. Current approaches that accelerate generation through approximate or compressed inference introduce severe optimization instabilities, including progressive divergence between the data-generating policy and the target optimization policy, and suffer from a fundamental scale mismatch wherein minute parameter updates become invisible to the deployed low-precision actor. The core scientific challenge is to develop a training paradigm that enables efficient rollout acceleration while maintaining long-term optimization stability and ensuring that fine-grained policy gradients effectively influence the inference-time behavior distribution.

Given Inspiration:
1. The hybrid nature of reinforcement learning training, where the inference-time actor generating rollouts simultaneously serves as a target for ongoing full-precision policy gradients, implies that compression strategies must accommodate live optimization dynamics rather than treating quantization as a static post-training procedure.
2. The realization that off-policy correction factors introduce implicit multiplicative scaling into gradient estimates, which asymmetrically distorts trust-region bounds and progressively biases the optimization trajectory away from the target policy over long training horizons.
3. The scale invariance property of linear transformations, which allows reciprocal rescaling of weights and activations without altering the network's functional mapping, suggests that representational granularity and gradient sensitivity can be jointly manipulated through strategic scaling.
4. The observation that typical policy gradient updates in trust-region methods are orders of magnitude smaller than the discretization intervals of low-precision formats, creating a representational blind spot where parameter changes fail to propagate to the deployed actor.
5. The perspective that trust-region optimization constraints are fundamentally coupled to the divergence between the data-generating distribution and the target policy, motivating dynamic bound adaptation responsive to policy mismatch rather than static clipping thresholds.
