Given Topic: Graph representation learning via deep message-passing networks has achieved remarkable progress, yet prevailing pooling and readout paradigms almost exclusively utilize the final layer’s node embeddings to construct graph-level descriptors. This practice neglects the intermediate representations accumulated throughout propagation, which encode complementary local and global structural cues at varying receptive scales. In deep architectures, this deficiency is compounded by the progressive convergence of node representations, wherein discriminative early-stage signals are overwritten or diluted, leading to a loss of multi-scale information. The central scientific challenge is to devise a principled, efficient aggregation mechanism that dynamically leverages the complete trajectory of node representations across all propagation depths to preserve both fine-grained local structures and coarse global patterns without sacrificing permutation invariance or computational tractability.

Given Inspiration:
1. The observation that existing graph pooling discards the entire history of node representations generated during message passing, despite the fact that intermediate embeddings encode complementary local and global information at different depths.
2. The over-smoothing phenomenon in deep graph networks, where successive propagation layers progressively erode discriminative local signals, suggesting that early-layer activations must be revisited at the readout stage to recover lost distinguishability.
3. The analogy between layer-wise feature evolution in graph networks and multi-scale hierarchical representations in convolutional neural networks, wherein shallow layers capture fine-grained local motifs while deeper layers encode coarse global topology.
4. The perspective of node embedding trajectories across layers as dynamical system paths whose temporal evolution can be selectively filtered to emphasize or suppress contributions from specific propagation depths.
5. The insight that standard non-negative weighting schemes constrain the expressive power of cross-layer aggregation, motivating the need for normalization strategies that permit antagonistic (additive and subtractive) relationships among historical states.
6. The recognition that the sequence of internal representations produced during iterative message passing constitutes a rich meta-signal about information flow, which should be exploited rather than discarded at inference.
