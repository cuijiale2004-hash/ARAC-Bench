Given Topic: Multimodal large language models suffer from hallucinations where generated text diverges from visual input. Existing preference optimization approaches for hallucination mitigation uniformly process all response tokens during decoding, ignoring the heterogeneous degrees of visual association across tokens and their differing contributions to output quality. Furthermore, prevalent methods frequently depend on external detection modules, auxiliary noise injection, or proprietary interfaces, introducing extraneous overhead and failing to exploit the model's endogenous multimodal capabilities. The core challenge lies in developing a fine-grained alignment mechanism that leverages intrinsic cross-modal signals to distinguish token-level visual relevance, thereby enabling precise preference optimization without external dependencies.

Given Inspiration:
1. Empirical observation that generated tokens in multimodal systems manifest heterogeneous cross-modal attention intensities and visual-semantic affinities, indicating divergent degrees of visual grounding.
2. Recognition that uniform treatment of all tokens during preference optimization fails to account for the disparate contributions of visually grounded tokens versus visually irrelevant or hallucinated tokens.
3. Principle that a model's intrinsic cross-modal attention mechanisms and latent semantic embeddings encode self-sufficient supervisory signals for evaluating token-level visual relevance without external annotators.
4. Insight that global visual salience, local spatial concentration, and semantic coherence offer complementary and mutually compensatory criteria for assessing token-image alignment.
5. Motivation that targeting correction efforts toward tokens with stronger visual associations can yield finer alignment than applying flat, sequence-wide optimization.
6. Hypothesis that token-level visual relevance scores extracted from endogenous multimodal representations can serve as adaptive rewards to guide preference optimization.
7. Design intuition that distributional regularization should be weighted proportionally to token importance, allowing flexible optimization of visually critical tokens while preserving overall model stability.
