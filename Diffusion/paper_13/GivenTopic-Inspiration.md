Given Topic: In the field of diffusion-based visual generation, attention mechanisms constitute the primary computational bottleneck when processing long sequential data, particularly in high-resolution video synthesis where sequence lengths scale to tens of thousands. Existing efficient attention paradigms face fundamental limitations: sparse attention approaches struggle to achieve extreme sparsity without incurring significant quality degradation at moderate sequence lengths, while linear attention methods suffer from severe performance deterioration when applied to complex visual generation tasks beyond static images. The core scientific problem is how to exploit the intrinsic structural properties of attention distributions to substantially reduce computational complexity while preserving generation fidelity, particularly in scenarios requiring both high sparsity and faithful attention modeling.

Given Inspiration:
1. Attention weight distributions in deep generative models exhibit a natural structural dichotomy: a numerically small subset of dominant entries possesses high-rank characteristics, whereas the remaining dense majority resides in an extremely low-rank subspace.
2. Empirical analysis of softmax-normalized attention matrices reveals a heavy-tailed magnitude distribution, where a significant fraction of entries falls far below the average value, yet cannot be entirely discarded without incurring approximation error.
3. Pure sparsification strategies encounter a representational ceiling because the residual weights after aggressive thresholding, though individually small, collectively retain structural information that affects output fidelity.
4. Linear attention formulations are inherently rank-constrained, which fundamentally limits their capacity to approximate full-rank attention spectra in complex visual generation tasks.
5. The coexistence of high-rank sparse outliers and low-rank dense residuals within the same attention matrix suggests that homogeneous acceleration strategies are suboptimal compared to structurally-aware decomposition.
6. Statistical stratification of attention weights into distinct importance regimes—dominant, intermediate, and negligible—indicates that a monolithic computational treatment ignores the heterogeneous algorithmic requirements of different magnitude tiers.
