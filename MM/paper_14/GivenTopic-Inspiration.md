Given Topic: The rapid deployment of general-purpose multimodal large language models for cloud-edge applications has created an urgent demand for efficient visual data compression. Conventional coding paradigms are predominantly optimized for human visual perception or specialized machine vision tasks, rendering them fundamentally misaligned with the diverse, open-world reasoning requirements of multimodal foundation models. A critical bottleneck is that existing methods inadequately characterize how compression artifacts propagate through hierarchical visual features, resulting in erratic downstream task performance. Specifically, the interplay between low-level structural fidelity and high-level semantic coherence—which underpins cross-level feature synthesis—remains unprotected by current codecs. The central scientific challenge is to establish a compression framework that holistically preserves multi-granularity visual information in a manner consistent with the internal perceptual dynamics of multimodal models.

Given Inspiration:
1. The empirical observation that compression distortion induces heterogeneous performance degradation across multimodal tasks, with cross-level feature-dependent tasks exhibiting disproportionate vulnerability compared to pure low-level or coarse high-level tasks.
2. The hierarchical three-stage information flow in vision encoders, progressing from global preliminary screening to localized feature extraction and culminating in global semantic integration.
3. The identification of a critical fragility window during the early phase of global semantic integration, where the synthesis of cross-level features is highly sensitive to low-level structural corruption.
4. The insight that shallow-layer attention dynamics in vision encoders inherently highlight semantically informative spatial regions, providing a natural guidance mechanism for importance-aware bit allocation.
5. The realization that human-visual-system-centric distortion criteria discard information potentially vital for machine reasoning, necessitating a feature-level fidelity criterion aligned with multimodal model internals.
6. The principle that low-level structural preservation and high-level semantic fidelity are not mutually exclusive but jointly necessary, as abstract reasoning depends on coherent integration of fine-grained details.
7. The engineering imperative that cloud-based multimodal inference requires compression strategies explicitly designed for machine consumption rather than human viewing.
