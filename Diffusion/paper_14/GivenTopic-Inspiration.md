Given Topic: The research focuses on inference-time controllability in text-to-image diffusion generation. Current mainstream methods predominantly rely on static or heuristically predefined guidance scales to modulate the conditioning strength during the denoising process. Such fixed strategies impose a uniform trade-off across all prompts, failing to account for the varying demands of different semantic contents, generation stages, and target capabilities. Consequently, practitioners face an undesirable compromise between prompt alignment, visual fidelity, and output diversity. Existing corrective approaches, including gradient-based auxiliary guidance and fixed scheduling heuristics, introduce considerable complexity yet exhibit poor cross-model and cross-task generalization. The core scientific issue addressed is how to dynamically determine the optimal conditioning strength at each generation step in a prompt-specific and model-adaptive manner, without increasing inference cost or relying on brittle empirical rules.

Given Inspiration:
1. The prevailing paradigm of applying a uniform guidance strength throughout the iterative generation process imposes an inflexible alignment-fidelity-diversity trade-off that is insensitive to semantic variations in conditioning signals.
2. The synthesis trajectory is inherently multi-phasic, where coarse semantic structures are established early while fine-grained perceptual attributes and local details crystallize in later stages.
3. Intermediate noisy representations retain predictable signals regarding final output quality, indicating that online assessment can be conducted without awaiting fully decoded results.
4. Distinct conditioning contexts, such as those requiring precise compositional arrangement, stylization, or accurate rendering of discrete symbols, possess fundamentally divergent optimal guidance requirements.
5. Empirically derived scheduling heuristics are tightly coupled to specific model families and training regimes, motivating the need for online, self-adjusting strategies that discover guidance schedules on-the-fly.
6. Multiple orthogonal quality dimensions, including semantic alignment, perceptual fidelity, and capability-specific attributes, can be assessed concurrently to inform a composite guidance objective.
7. The predictive relevance of each quality dimension fluctuates across generation timesteps, necessitating a dynamic weighting strategy that modulates evaluator influence as signals become discriminative.
