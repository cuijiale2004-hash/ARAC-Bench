Given Topic: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) constitute a powerful paradigm for addressing complex problems through distributed reasoning and collective collaboration. However, the effectiveness of such systems is fundamentally constrained by the difficulty of optimizing inter-agent interactions. Existing methodologies are predominantly bifurcated into manual workflow orchestration, which is labor-intensive and induces cascading instability due to the acute sensitivity of language model agents to prompt perturbations, and automated topology search, which is plagued by combinatorial explosion and poor scalability. Although individual agent capabilities—such as reasoning, memory, and tool use—have progressed substantially, their integration into a cohesive and robust system remains a significant challenge. This work confronts the core scientific problem of enhancing cooperative efficacy within a fixed multi-agent topology, redirecting the research emphasis from architectural design to the dynamic optimization of collaborative interaction to maximize holistic system performance.

Given Inspiration:
1. Structural search deficiencies: The combinatorial intractability and inherent fragility of automated interaction topology search, coupled with the instability of manual orchestration, inspire a paradigm shift from discovering optimal architectures toward optimizing cooperative dynamics within predetermined structural constraints.
2. Functional decoupling: Grounded in the principle of separation of concerns, the perspective that guiding a reasoning process and executing the solution are distinct functions motivates the independent optimization of a dedicated guidance mechanism rather than the primary reasoning engine itself.
3. Human collaborative apprenticeship: Drawing from human cooperative intelligence, the underlying motivation reflects the dynamic between an expert performer and a learning assistant who adapts to provide strategic hints or feedback, thereby improving joint outcomes without modifying the expert's core competencies.
4. System-level policy optimization: Inspired by the observation that isolated improvements in individual agents do not automatically yield better collective performance, the approach treats cooperative interaction as a unified policy to be trained via end-to-end success signals, fostering emergent and robust coordination.
5. Asymmetric resource efficiency: Motivated by practical engineering constraints, the design insight recognizes that enhancing system-level reasoning through lightweight, task-specific auxiliary optimization is more scalable and cost-effective than exhaustive architectural enumeration or retraining large foundation models.
