Given Topic: In continuous-action reinforcement learning, particularly in offline and offline-to-online settings, optimizing highly expressive generative policies against a value function presents a fundamental dilemma. Existing expressive policy classes, which model complex multimodal action distributions through iterative sequential transformations, require backpropagating gradients through long trajectories to leverage critic information. This leads to severe numerical instability and compounding gradient ill-conditioning. Conversely, methods that avoid such backpropagation either forgo gradient-based critic feedback entirely, resulting in inefficient learning, or resort to simplified single-step approximations that constrain representational capacity and introduce bias. Furthermore, naively injecting value gradients into intermediate noisy stages of the generative process relies on unreliable out-of-distribution gradient estimates when critic networks are only accurate for clean actions. The core problem is how to directly exploit first-order critic information to optimize a fully expressive multi-step policy toward an optimal behavior-constrained solution without unstable backpropagation through the generative chain or biased intermediate approximations.

Given Inspiration:
1. The formulation of distribution tilting in generative modeling as a stochastic optimal control problem, where a base generative process is adjusted toward a target distribution defined by an external potential function.
2. The understanding that standard continuous adjoint methods for computing gradients through long trajectories are numerically unstable in this setting because the backward propagation depends recursively on the parameters of the policy being optimized.
3. The mathematical insight that if the backward gradient propagation is instead anchored to the fixed base generative dynamics and only retains terms that affect the optimum, the resulting gradient estimates remain unbiased while avoiding compounding instability.
4. The observation that direct application of external guidance gradients to intermediate noisy states implicitly assumes these gradients are valid throughout the noise spectrum, which is violated when the guiding function is trained exclusively on clean, denoised actions.
5. The principle from dynamic programming that terminal rewards or costs can be propagated backward through time to provide local supervision at each intermediate step, provided the backward dynamics are appropriately decoupled from the forward optimization variables.
