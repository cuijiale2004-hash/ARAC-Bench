**1. Research Background and Existing Pain Points**

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities on a wide range of vision-language tasks, with leading large-scale open-source and proprietary models achieving remarkable performance. However, this progress masks a critical and widening gap: while massive, expensive models show emerging competence, their smaller and more efficient counterparts struggle profoundly with complex reasoning, especially when they try to understand temporal and procedural image sequences. This capability gap is not merely an academic concern but a primary obstacle hindering the deployment of MLLMs in real-world and interactive applications. Downstream tasks like robotic manipulation, embodied navigation, and instruction following often operate on resource-constrained edge devices where deploying dozens or hundreds billion parameter models is infeasible. Consequently, the inability of small models to grasp state changes and procedural logic represents a fundamental bottleneck for the entire field of embodied AI.

The root of this deficiency lies not in model scale alone, but in a systemic failure of the prevailing training paradigm. Existing paradigms predominantly focus on aligning text with a single image or treating multiple images as an unordered collection. Although datasets like LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave incorporate multi-frame inputs derived from videos, they primarily emphasize general sequential content comprehension rather than fine-grained temporal and procedural understanding. This approach overlooks the critical distinction between understanding a set of images and comprehending a sequence of images. 

The community’s response has been to create evaluation-only benchmarks that repeatedly confirm this failure (such as MuirBench, STRIPCIPHER, TempVS, LEGO-Puzzles, MV-Math, BLINK, and MMRA), rather than addressing the root cause, which is the absence of large-scale, high-quality real-world sequential data for training. This oversight stems from the inherent difficulty of capturing the complex, continuous transformations of real-world actions. As a result, smaller models are evaluated on a sophisticated skill they were never systematically taught, leading to poor performance in detecting procedural errors and leaving genuine procedural understanding an unsolved problem for deployable AI systems. Furthermore, while general video pre-training provides fundamental temporal awareness, it is insufficient for precise procedural understanding, as general video understanding typically focuses on recognizing what events occur, whereas procedural multi-image understanding places a greater emphasis on inferring the precise sequence and consequential relationships between discrete actions.

**2. Core Research Motivation and Scientific Questions**

The core research motivation is to bridge the critical capability gap in temporal and procedural understanding for resource-constrained models, solving a fundamental bottleneck for the field of embodied AI. The scientific question addressed is: How can we systematically teach smaller MLLMs to grasp state changes and procedural logic, transitioning them from passive observation to active, cross-modal validation, given the current absence of large-scale, procedurally coherent training data? 

Specifically, the paper seeks to answer how to construct a dataset that not only provides large-scale sequential data but also structurally enforces the learning of temporal dynamics through complementary reasoning tasks. It also questions how to design a training methodology that enables small-to-medium-sized models to achieve and surpass the temporal understanding capabilities of vastly larger models, ensuring that targeted training on procedurally-grounded data can effectively close the reasoning gap without causing catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities.

**3. Overall Core Idea and Design Philosophy**

The overall core idea is to introduce TPRU (Temporal-Procedural Understanding dataset), a novel dataset designed to bridge the gap between evaluation and training for temporal and procedural understanding. The design philosophy centers on systematically cultivating deep procedural understanding through structured, complementary tasks and robust training mechanisms.

First, to address data scarcity and authenticity, TPRU provides a large-scale training set sourced from four diverse and authentic embodied scenarios: robotic manipulation, LEGO assembly, GUI operation, and embodied navigation. Second, TPRU is systematically structured to cultivate a deep procedural understanding through three complementary task formats: Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, and Previous-Frame Review. Third, to ensure models develop true comprehension beyond superficial heuristics, TPRU incorporates a significant number of challenging negative samples with deliberate inconsistencies, forcing models to transition from passive "seeing" to active validation. Fourth, the dataset is paired with a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methodology (GRPO) specifically targeting the enhancement of resource-efficient models. Finally, to benchmark this capability, TPRU includes a manually curated held-out test set (TPRU-Test) for rigorous evaluation, unifying the development and evaluation of procedural understanding.

**4. Core Innovation Points**

1.  **Construction of TPRU Dataset**: A new, large-scale (24,750 QA pairs, 126,000 images), high-quality multi-image dataset focused on fine-grained temporal and procedural understanding, designed to empower smaller models for embodied contexts.
2.  **TPRU-Test Benchmark**: A challenging held-out test set of 461 manually curated and verified instances across five application scenarios, rigorously evaluating temporal understanding in MLLMs.
3.  **Effective RL-based Training Methodology**: Proposal and validation of a reinforcement learning-based training methodology (GRPO) that enables small-to-medium-sized MLLMs (3B, 7B, 32B) to achieve and even surpass the temporal understanding capabilities of vastly larger models, including GPT-4o.
4.  **Systematic Data Generation Pipeline**: A pipeline that transforms raw sequential data from diverse sources into structured training instances through rigorous sequence filtering, text description generation with robustness enhancement, and complementary task formulation.
5.  **Negative Sampling Strategy for Robustness**: Introduction of a subset of instances where the textual description is deliberately mismatched with the visual content (negative samples), forcing the model to perform explicit cross-modal verification and reject invalid logic, advancing from pattern recognition to robust procedural understanding.
6.  **Three Complementary Task Formulations**: The design of Temporal Ordering, Next Frame Prediction, and Previous Frame Review tasks, engineered to jointly address temporal ordering, forward prediction, and backward review, advancing MLLMs beyond static image analysis to a profound and structured comprehension of procedural dynamics.

**5. Overview of the Overall Technical Solution**

The overall technical solution consists of dataset construction (TPRU-25k and TPRU-Test) and an RL-based fine-tuning methodology. 

TPRU-25k is a fine-tuning set with 24,750 samples sourced from: (1) Robotic Manipulation (ShareRobot dataset planning tasks), (2) LEGO Assembly (36 stop-motion videos from Arvin Bricks), (3) GUI Operation (four-step screenshot sequences from GUI Odyssey), and (4) Embodied Navigation (ordered visual observations from simulated environments like Habitat). The data generation pipeline involves: (a) Filtering and Quality Control using Qwen2.5-VL-72B to ensure high-fidelity, logically coherent 3-4 image sequences; (b) Description Generation using Qwen2.5-VL-72B for positive descriptions and introducing negative descriptions for robustness; (c) Task Formulation into Temporal Ordering (shuffling frames, predicting permutation), Next Frame Prediction (selecting the intervening third frame from candidates), and Previous Frame Review (identifying the initial frame from candidates).

TPRU-Test is a held-out evaluation set of 461 instances across 5 scenarios (the 4 core domains plus EPIC-KITCHENS), curated with multi-stage human review and cross-verification. 

The training methodology utilizes Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning with the Group-wise Preference Optimization (GRPO) algorithm on Qwen2.5-VL models (3B, 7B, 32B). Key settings include KL regularization with a coefficient of 0.01, 5 rollouts per sample, AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-6, and training for 2 epochs on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. The reward design includes task-accuracy rewards and format-specific rewards to ensure structured reasoning and output.

**6. Detailed Module Design**

**Module 1: Filtering and Quality Control Module**
This module employs Qwen2.5-VL-72B as an automated quality assessor to process heterogeneous inputs into a canonical format of 3-4 coherent image sequences.
*   **System Prompt**: "You are a professional visual data analyst responsible for filtering multi-image sequences for a machine learning dataset. Your task is to ensure that each sequence meets the following strict quality and coherence standards: 1. Continuity of Action: The images must depict a single, continuous, and uninterrupted action or process performed by the same subject or agent. 2. Temporal Coherence: The sequence must have a clear and logical chronological order. The state change between consecutive frames must be discernible and sensible. 3. Visual Quality: All images in the sequence must be clear and free of significant blur, corruption, or distracting artifacts. The main subject and object must be clearly visible. 4. Scene Consistency: The background and core environment must remain consistent throughout the sequence. Changes should be due to the action itself, not abrupt scene cuts or significant camera movement."
*   **User Prompt**: "Strictly analyze the provided image sequence based on the quality standards. Determine if it represents a high-quality, coherent, and temporally logical process. A sequence is considered unqualified if any of the following are true: • The images are from different, unrelated scenes or actions. • The chronological order is illogical or indiscernible. • The images are severely blurry, low-resolution, or contain duplicate frames. • The change in the scene is solely due to camera panning/zooming without a meaningful action occurring. • The main object of the action is swapped, disappears, or is completely occluded. Does this image sequence meet all the required standards? Please provide your answer as a single word: ’Yes’ or ’No’."

**Module 2: Description Generation and Robustness Enhancement Module**
This module generates textual descriptions to reinforce vision-language alignment and introduces a negative sampling strategy to mitigate hallucination.
*   **Positive Description Generation**: For each filtered sequence, a textual description is generated: "These photos show the process of + (The correct action in image sequences)".
*   **Negative Description Generation**: A subset of instances is created where the description is deliberately mismatched: "These photos show the process of + (The incorrect action about image sequences)". The target output for these challenging cases is formulated as "None of the choices provided".

**Module 3: Task Formulation Module**
This module converts curated sequences and descriptions into three structured tasks:
*   **Temporal Ordering**: The temporal order of frames for a given image group is shuffled. The question provides the textual description and asks: "What is the correct sequence of these images?". The model outputs the correct permutation (e.g., "4 2 3 1").
*   **Next Frame Prediction**: The model is presented with the initial, second, and terminal frames of a four-frame sequence. The question asks to select the correct intervening third frame from candidates (including distractors). Example: "The 1st step is <image1>, followed by step 2<image2>. Select the correct option of step 3, which can reach the final step 4<image3>".
*   **Previous Frame Review**: The model is given the final three frames. The question asks to identify the correct initial frame from candidates. Example: "The 2nd screen is step 2<image1>, followed by step 3<image2>, and the final screen is step 4<image3>. Please select the option of step 1."

**Module 4: Training Module (GRPO)**
This module implements the Group-wise Preference Optimization algorithm using the Easy-R1 framework. 
*   **Format Reward**: Enforces the model to enclose its reasoning process in  tags and the final answer in <answer> tags. Ablation studies show this reward provides training stability and minor gains, but the core reasoning stems from task-accuracy.
*   **Task Accuracy Reward**: Evaluates the correctness of the final answer for the three task types.

**Module 5: Prompting Module**
This module enforces strict output formatting through an instruction template:
"You will be presented with a task involving a set of images. The specific task is described in the content below. Carefully analyze the images and the specific task description provided above. Your response must strictly follow the format rules below. ANSWER FORMAT RULES Your response format depends on the specific task presented in the content above: 1. For an Ordering Task: If the question asks for the correct sequence of images (labeled A, B, C, D), your answer must be a single string representing the correct order of the labels. Do not include spaces or other characters. 2. For a Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ): If the question provides options (e.g., A, B, C, D, E), your answer must be the single letter corresponding to the correct option. If you believe none of the options are correct, choose the letter for the ”None of the choices provided” option if available."
