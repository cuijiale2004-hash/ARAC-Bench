Given Topic: The rapid advancement of multimodal understanding models has heightened the need for rigorous evaluation of human-centric visual comprehension, given that human behavior inherently reflects intelligence and environmental complexity. Existing assessment paradigms are predominantly constrained to isolated recognition tasks or overly broad scopes lacking diagnostic granularity, consequently overlooking nuanced dimensions such as fine-grained spatial relations, temporal dynamics, and social cognition. Moreover, current benchmarks inadequately probe complex reasoning capabilities, specifically the integration of multiple disparate visual evidence and the proactive extraction of implicit contextual information beyond explicit query cues. Therefore, a critical scientific issue lies in establishing a comprehensive, hierarchically structured evaluation framework capable of systematically diagnosing model limitations across perception, comprehension, and reasoning within diverse, realistic human-centric scenarios.

Given Inspiration:
1. The pursuit of artificial general intelligence necessitates that multimodal systems achieve profound human-centric understanding, as human activities represent a primary conduit through which intelligence and world knowledge are manifested.
2. Human cognitive processing inherently operates across hierarchical levels, progressing from elemental visual perception to conceptual comprehension and subsequently to complex reasoning, suggesting that evaluation frameworks should emulate this stratified architecture.
3. Human reasoning naturally synthesizes multiple disparate visual cues and proactively infers implicit contextual information, in contrast to existing paradigms that exhibit excessive reliance on explicit query-guided retrieval, revealing a fundamental behavioral gap.
4. The fragmentation and coarse-grained aggregation prevalent in current benchmarks indicate that fine-grained, task-specific diagnostic assessment is essential to expose precise capability deficiencies and guide targeted advancement.
5. Empirical observations suggest that merely expanding visual context volume yields diminishing returns for complex reasoning, whereas enhancing intrinsic reasoning mechanisms produces substantive gains, indicating that reasoning quality constitutes the primary bottleneck rather than perceptual scale.
6. The extensive diversity of human activities across quotidian and professional domains necessitates evaluation data sources that transcend single-domain bias to ensure comprehensive contextual coverage and ecological validity.
