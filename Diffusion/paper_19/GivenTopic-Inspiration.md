Given Topic: Diffusion-based language models parallelize generation by framing decoding as an iterative denoising process, yet they incur substantial computational overhead because each step must evaluate all future token positions despite ultimately retaining only a fraction of them. As generation length increases, this exhaustive evaluation of future contexts becomes a dominant bottleneck, compounded by the fact that distant future positions contribute diminishing contextual value and often converge to repetitive, low-entropy patterns. The fundamental challenge is to systematically reduce redundant computation over future token positions during inference while preserving the bidirectional contextual dependencies necessary for high-quality denoising, thereby achieving scalable and efficient long-sequence generation.

Given Inspiration:
1. The recognition that future token positions in iterative denoising generation serve as transient memory buffers aggregating contextual signals across depth, yet exhibit severe redundancy and low information entropy, implying that only a sparse local subset is functionally necessary for guiding current predictions.
2. The empirical observation that model attention weights assigned to future positions decay sharply with distance, revealing an inherent long-range sparsity that can be exploited to avoid exhaustive pre-computation of negligible contexts.
3. The analogy to human compositional planning, where creators allocate detailed cognitive resources to immediate upcoming segments while maintaining only coarse representations for remote future segments, suggesting that generation systems should similarly prioritize proximal future contexts over distal ones.
4. The hypothesis that dense evaluation of future contexts contains a sparse, sufficient subset capable of preserving semantic consistency, which can be dynamically discovered during the forward pass without additional training.
