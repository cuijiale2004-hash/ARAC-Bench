Given Topic: Discrete diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative models for discrete data domains. However, current approaches typically rely on factorized parameterizations of the generative process to ensure parallel sampling efficiency, which inherently limits model expressiveness and creates a substantial approximation error when the number of sampling steps is reduced. Consequently, discrete diffusion models generally require a large number of sequential generation steps—often comparable to data dimensionality—resulting in computationally intensive inference. Unlike continuous diffusion, discrete spaces lack continuous deterministic trajectories that facilitate few-step generation. Therefore, a critical scientific challenge lies in how to align the complex target distribution induced by the forward process with the constrained reverse parameterization, thereby enabling high-quality generation with significantly fewer steps while preserving inference efficiency.

Given Inspiration:
1. The structural asymmetry between continuous and discrete diffusion paradigms: continuous domains permit deterministic, parameterized trajectories that directly map noise to data, whereas discrete domains inherently lack such continuous paths, creating a fundamental barrier to few-step generation.
2. The probabilistic decomposability of complex joint distributions: certain distributions can be generated through a short sequence of factorized conditional operations if intermediate latent structures are appropriately chosen, suggesting that generation complexity depends crucially on noising trajectory design.
3. The representational bottleneck of fixed forward processes: pre-defined corruption schedules induce targets that may exceed the capacity of efficient factorized reverse parameterizations when steps are few, motivating data-adaptive alternatives.
4. The principle of mutual adaptation: rather than unilaterally fitting a reverse model to a fixed noising process, enabling forward dynamics to co-evolve with the reverse model's constraints can yield better alignment between target and approximating distributions.
5. The perspective that forward corruption need not treat each coordinate independently of the global data structure; allowing the degradation of individual elements to be informed by the entire data point provides additional flexibility in shaping the target distribution.
6. The mass transport view of sequential corruption: constructing intermediate distributions can be regarded as redistributing probability mass, where consistency between consecutive time steps is governed by how closely marginal distributions can be coupled.
